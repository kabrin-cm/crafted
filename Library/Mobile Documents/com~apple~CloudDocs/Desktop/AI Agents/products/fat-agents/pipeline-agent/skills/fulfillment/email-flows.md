# PART 1: THE EMAIL MATRIX — CORE PHILOSOPHY

## Context = More Money

This is the foundational principle of the entire Email Matrix methodology. Most organizations send robotic, generalized, sterile emails to their entire list and are shocked when they get low open rates and overall low email engagement.

**The fix:** Create context as much as you possibly can based on the data you have available in your CRM and from the segmentation you can create.

**The investment company example:** A client markets to investors who want the company to manage millions of dollars for them. Each investor that applies must answer with the amount they want to invest ($1M or less, $1M-$5M, $5M-$10M, $10M-$20M, $20M-$50M, $50M+). The difference between a lead that says they have $1M to invest and an investor with $50M+ is WIDE. They need to be treated differently — the strategies deployed to manage their money are different, so the communication must be crafted contextually for that person and their needs. If the person with $50M gets treated the same and you go over strategies of turning $1M into 12% a year more, it turns them off and you substantially lower your probability of converting.

**The solution:** Create a video series emailed out in a sequence to each type of lead for strategies that would be deployed to best manage their desired investable range. The concerns of these different ranges differ greatly too — address those in subsequent emails so each person knows their situation is known by the company, which makes people feel great.

**The Fear of God example:** Jeremy spent thousands on clothes from Fear of God — only ever bought black men's clothes in XXL, lives in Miami Florida. What he never gets (but would catch his attention 1000X more): an email that says "our latest black mens clothes in XXL" or "the perfect outfit for Miami summertime." The data exists in their CRM. The contextual communication would exponentially increase purchase frequency.

**The bank analogy:** Ever been on a call with a bank, had to get transferred, and had to repeat your entire story to the next rep? How frustrating is that? How good does it feel when the first rep explains everything to the new one before the handoff? Night and day difference. It's the same with your leads — when you tailor communication specific to their needs, wants, and desires using all the data you've collected, you increase spending probability by a lot.

**Core rule:** From here forward, ask at least one segmentation question in every lead form you've got, even if it's something simple like a newsletter opt-in. Get something — anything — that allows you to communicate in a more specific way rather than just throwing them into that generalized email list.

It's about slicing your list into smaller, more manageable groups based on certain traits they share. Think of it as the difference between a shotgun and a sniper rifle. The shotgun scatters, hoping to hit anything in its path, but the sniper rifle aims, targets, and hits with incredible accuracy. Your email marketing should be that sniper rifle.

## Complete 14-Way Email Segmentation System

The sharper your segments, the more your emails will feel like they're speaking directly to each recipient. Tags are unique identifiers you attach to customer data in your CRM system. They allow you to group people based on their needs and interests so that when it's time for marketing, they get the right message at the right time.

### Segment 1: Demographic Slice (Who They Are)
Think age, income, job. Why chat about retirement plans with a college kid? Tailor your message to fit their life stage.

### Segment 2: Geographic Segment (Where They're At)
Location, location, location. It's not just for real estate. From local events to weather shifts, make your emails resonate with local flavor.

### Segment 3: Behavioral Breakdown (What They Do)
Look at their shopping behavior, website clicks, and past interactions. Like a detective, use clues from their actions to personalize your approach.

### Segment 4: Engagement Filter (How They Interact)
Some folks devour your emails; others glance. Segment by engagement to keep the avid readers fueled and try to rekindle the spark with the skimmers.

### Segment 5: Buying Behavior (Past Purchases)
Like a chef suggesting wine based on your meal, recommend products related to their past purchases. It's about enhancing their experience.

### Segment 6: Sales Funnel Focus (Journey Stage)
Tailor your content like you're guiding them down a path. Newbies get the 101 content, while veterans might see the finish line offers.

### Segment 7: VIP Section (Loyalty Level)
Roll out the red carpet for your regulars with exclusive deals or sneak peeks. Show them love, and they'll keep coming back.

### Segment 8: Abandoned Cart Crew (Almost Buyers)
For those who left their cart lonely, a gentle nudge or a tempting offer might just get them across the finish line.

### Segment 9: Email Cadence Club (Their Pace)
Some like it hot, some like it not. Adjust your send rate to match their preference, keeping them cozy and connected without the overwhelm.

### Segment 10: Lead Magnet Group (Interest Indicator)
Segment by the freebies they've snagged. It's a window into their interests and how you can serve them next.

### Segment 11: Survey Squad (Opinion Owners)
Use survey insights to tailor your messages, addressing their specific feedback or interests with precision.

### Segment 12: Membership/Subscription Status
Different strokes for different folks. Customize your content based on the level of access or service they've signed up for.

### Segment 13: Seasonal Shoppers
Some customers come alive during certain seasons. Identify these patterns and you can be there with the right offer at the right time.

### Segment 14: Event Enthusiasts
For those who've shown up to your events, keep the conversation going. Offer resources, or tease the next big thing.

### Bonus: Tech Tendencies
Knowing if they're team mobile or desktop can help you design emails that hit the mark, visually and contextually.

## Email Send Time & Day Optimization Rules

Data compiled from millions of emails sent over a 365-day period across many different niches.

### Best Time of Day
- **Best:** 3 PM in the person's timezone (not your timezone, THEIR timezone)
- **Best range:** Noon to 6 PM
- **Worst times:** Anytime after 9 PM through 11 AM the following day
- **Exception:** Order notifications and support response emails perform best in the morning
- **Rule:** Content and value-driven emails that are lengthier or intended to get the person to browse for several minutes perform best in late afternoon

### Best Days of the Week
- **Best days:** Tuesday and Friday (beat the next highest days by a full 9% additional open rate on average)
- **Worst days:** Saturday and Sunday
- **Middle tier:** Monday and Wednesday (decent), Thursday (lags behind slightly)

### Day-of-Week Strategy
- **Tuesday and Friday:** Send sales-based emails that could lead to revenue — more trackable revenue compared to any other days
- **Weekends:** Send content emails instead — drive email traffic to social media channels or educational funnels/lead magnets that have a lot of consumption time since people generally have more time on weekends
- **Business-specific:** Most businesses have one part of the week that's great and one part that's slower. Be aware of that trend and play into it.

## Why People Open Emails — Complete Survey Data

Survey data from email lists of clients, compiled into ranked order:

### Rank 1: "Expecting It" (DOMINANT reason)
They opted into something expecting an access email. They contacted customer support and are expecting a response. They replied to an email and are expecting a response back. This was the most dominant reason by far.

**Tactical implication:** Think "how can I get all of my emails to be expected." That happens after someone knowingly opts in and is actively waiting for what you're going to send.

**"Email Options" technique:** Give leads the ability to select different options for what they want to see next (represented by buttons or links): content, top videos of the week, newsletter content, offers/sales/discounts, more of the same, and whatever else is applicable. ### Rank 2: Known Sender
Known senders have a higher likelihood of being opened. When you shop with companies you love, you open any email they send because you love that company. Same for brands you support or newsletters you're opted into and want to read. Unknown senders = brands they haven't heard from before.

### Rank 3: Subject Line
Third place behind expectations and sender recognition.

### Rank 4: Preview Text
Well behind in fourth place.

### Rank 5: Offer
Last place — the actual offer itself.

## Email Value Provision Types

In an era where inboxes are inundated with "buy now" and "limited time offer" emails, standing out requires delivering real, tangible value — not just relentless sales pitches.

### Type 1: Educational Content
Share insights, tutorials, and how-tos that align with your audience's interests and needs. If you're a financial services company and you know a segment is new homeowners, send a guide on managing home-related expenses.

### Type 2: Personalized Recommendations
Use data to offer personalized product suggestions, articles, or services. If someone's been browsing winter coats, send a curated selection of coats with care tips for winter apparel.

### Type 3: Exclusive Content
Content exclusive to email subscribers: early access to sales, sneak peeks at new products, or content created specifically for the list like an in-depth industry report.

### Type 4: Interactive Elements
Polls, surveys, or quizzes that engage and provide insights into subscriber preferences. This makes subscribers feel heard and offers more aligned content.

### Type 5: Behind-the-Scenes Looks
Stories about how products are made, team introductions, virtual tours of office or manufacturing. Humanizes your brand and builds deeper connection.

### The Golden Thread: Context
The more you know about subscribers (purchase history, engagement, willingly shared info) the more tailored and impactful your value-driven emails can be. Context transforms generic advice into "This was written for me" moments.

## ESP Requirements (What Email Service Providers Want)

Understanding ESPs is critical — they determine whether your emails reach the inbox or languish in spam.

### 1. Deliverability Is King
ESPs scrutinize sender reputation, engagement rates, and content quality. Your mission: prove your emails are not just wanted but welcomed.

### 2. Quality Over Quantity
ESPs prioritize senders who provide high-value content over those who bombard with high volume of low-quality. Mantra: send less, but send better.

### 3. Engagement Drives Favor
Open rates, click-through rates, and interaction frequency are all scrutinized. High engagement = boosted sender reputation and inbox placement.

### 4. Authenticity and Transparency
Clear "from" information, straightforward subject lines, easy-to-find unsubscribe options. Misleading practices are penalized.

### 5. Consistency Is Key
Consistent sending frequency and volume helps ESPs trust your patterns. Erratic sending triggers spam alarms.

### 6. List Hygiene Practices
Regularly remove unengaged subscribers, correct misspelled addresses, manage bounce rates. Signals you're a conscientious sender.

### 7. Adherence to Regulations
Compliance with CAN-SPAM, GDPR, etc. Direct reflection of commitment to ethical email practices.

### 8. Feedback Loop Utilization
Use ESP feedback loops (notification when recipients mark emails as spam) to adjust strategy and maintain positive relationships.

## Subject Line Optimization Rules

### Optimal Word Count
- **Best:** 6-10 words (12% higher open rate vs. 0-5 or 11-15 words)
- **Severe dropoff:** Above 15 words
- **Mobile reality:** Typical iPhone screen cuts off around 10 one-to-two syllable words
- **Desktop exception:** Older demographics viewing on desktop may benefit from longer subject lines

### Formatting Rules
- **Punctuation:** Limit to three punctuation marks max per subject line. Overdoing it sends emails to spam.
- **Preferred style:** No punctuation, lowercase sentencing = max open rates
- **Emojis:** Use sparingly — max one emoji to complement, not overshadow. They display differently across devices. Preview before sending.
- **Personalization:** Use "Personal Touch Tags" (recipient's name, location) to boost open rates. Especially effective with targeted outreach, customer milestones, or post-purchase.
- **Directness:** Be direct and concise. Avoid seasonal cliches ("Falling into discounts"). Highlight tangible benefits instead.
- **Character limit:** No more than 60 characters for maximum mobile digestibility.

### Testing Protocol
- A/B test subject lines by sending variants to different segments
- Keep a running doc of what's worked and what hasn't
- Review open rates of recent subject lines to illuminate what captures attention
- Most CRMs have built-in A/B testing functions

## "Keep It Simple and Remind Them" Email Template

When you run paid ads to a sales team used to organic leads, they'll say leads "didn't know who we are." This email fixes that immediately.

### What It Is
Take a screenshot of the ad(s) they responded to, the sales page or lead form they filled out, and send them their application data that they answered with, along with any links for wherever they originated from as a lead.

### When to Use
- Works best for leads that are then going to speak with a sales team
- NOT needed for ecommerce brands or lead magnets where there is no sales team verbal communication
- Send as soon as the lead comes in OR before the scheduled call

### Why It Works
It's amazing that leads forget after minutes or a few hours that they took the time to go through your lead funnel, but it happens. Reminding them of where they originated from increases show rates and establishes familiarity bias which leads to higher trust right away.

### Template Structure
1. Screenshot of the ad they responded to
2. Screenshot of the sales page/lead form
3. Their application data (answers they submitted)
4. Links to where they came from
5. Load it up with Credibility Assets (see below)

If you didn't need to send where the lead originated from, start off your sequence with credibility assets. These drastically increase the odds of a lead buying from your sales team in a shorter sales cycle.

### Asset Types

2. **Company website URLs**
3. **Company addresses for physical locations**
4. **Phone numbers**
5. **Team photos**
6. **Links to social channels**
7. **Anything that makes the lead conclude:** "this company is legit, I'm excited to buy from them"

### For E-Commerce Companies
Add:
- **Unboxing videos**
- **Instructional "how to use" videos**
- **"What to expect" videos**
- **Third-party YouTube reviews** (great for lowering buyer's remorse post-purchase)
- **Thank-you videos from staff** (leverages law of specificity — e.g., "4.7 stars out of 5" is more believable than "5/5")

### Contextual Cross-Sell Example (Fully/Herman Miller)
- Buyer of standing desk gets "how to put it together" video, then "monitor arm" upsell email
- Buyer of office chair (who hasn't bought desk) gets standing desk review video
- Desk buyer gets third-party review video to lower buyer's remorse
- Staff "thank you for reviews" video (4.7/5 stars — law of specificity) used for review requests or selling leads on customer happiness

**Key insight:** The video sold a $3,000 chair via email with subject line "zero gravity relaxation" — the video was the selling mechanism, delivered after trust was established through prior purchases.

# PART 2: EMAIL SEQUENCES

## Sequence 1: Pre-Call Nurture (The Money Maker)

**Purpose:** Maximize show rate and pre-frame leads so they arrive ready to buy
**Length:** 6-12 emails over 72 hours (2-4 per day)
**Goal:** Turn salespeople into "cashiers" — leads arrive pre-sold

### The 4 Content Pillars (From Hammer Them Strategy)

Every email maps to one of these pillars:

| Pillar | Purpose | Subject Line Style |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **Questions** | Answer what they fundamentally need to know | "Here's how [process] works..." |
| **2nd-Layer Questions** | Address follow-up questions | "But what if [concern]?" |
| **Objections** | Proactively handle resistance | "The #1 reason people hesitate..." |
| **Expectations** | Set realistic timelines and effort | "What your first 30 days look like" |

### Pre-Call Email Sequence Template

**Email 1: Booking Confirmation (Immediate)**
- Pillar: Expectations
- Subject: "You're confirmed — here's what happens next"
- Content: Confirm date/time, explain what the call covers, set expectations for call length, what to prepare, who they'll speak with
- CTA: "Add to your calendar" + link

**Email 2: The Story (Day 1, Morning)**
- Pillar: Questions
- Subject: "[Founder name]'s story — why we built [company]"
- Content: Founder/company origin story, the problem they noticed, why they're uniquely positioned to solve it, results achieved
- No hard CTA — pure trust building

**Email 3: Case Study (Day 1, Afternoon)**
- Pillar: Questions
- Subject: "How [client name] went from [before] to [after]"
- Content: Specific client case study matching the avatar, include numbers and timeline, show the transformation
- Soft CTA: "We'll discuss how we can do the same for you on our call"

**Email 4: Objection Handler #1 (Day 2, Morning)**
- Pillar: Objections
- Subject: "The #1 concern people have before our call"
- Content: Address the biggest objection head-on (usually price or "will this work for me"), reframe with evidence
- Soft CTA: "Bring any questions to our call — we'll cover everything"

**Email 5: The Process (Day 2, Afternoon)**
- Pillar: Expectations
- Subject: "Here's exactly what working with us looks like"
- Content: Step-by-step walkthrough of what happens after they sign up, timeline for results, what they'll need to do vs. what you handle
- CTA: "See you on [date] at [time]"

**Email 6: Social Proof Stack (Day 2, Evening)**
- Pillar: 2nd-Layer Questions
- Subject: "What [3 client names] have in common"
- Content: 3-5 brief testimonials with specific results, variety of industries/situations to show breadth
- Soft CTA: "Ready to be next? See you tomorrow."

**Email 7: Objection Handler #2 (Day 3, Morning)**
- Pillar: Objections
- Subject: "If you've tried [alternative] before and it didn't work..."
- Content: Address the "I've been burned before" objection, explain what makes this different (unique mechanism)
- CTA: "Our call is today at [time] — looking forward to it"

**Email 8: Day-Of Reminder (Day 3, 2 Hours Before)**
- Pillar: Expectations
- Subject: "Quick reminder — we're on in 2 hours"
- Content: Brief, friendly reminder. Reiterate value of the call. Link to join.
- CTA: "Click here to join at [time]"

**Email 9: 15-Minute Reminder (Day 3, 15 Min Before)**
- Pillar: Expectations
- Subject: "Starting in 15 minutes"
- Content: One line + join link
- CTA: "Join here"

### Optional Additional Emails (For 12-Email Version)

**Email 10:** Behind-the-scenes (team, office, process)
**Email 11:** FAQ format (5 most common questions)
**Email 12:** "What to expect on the call" (detailed agenda)

## Sequence 2: Post-Call Follow-Up (No Close)

**Purpose:** Re-engage leads who attended but didn't close
**Length:** 4-8 emails over 7-14 days
**Trigger:** Call completed, not closed

### Post-Call Email Sequence Template

**Email 1: Thank You + Recap (Same Day)**
- Subject: "Great speaking with you today, [name]"
- Content: Personalized recap of what was discussed, restate the specific plan/proposal, address any concerns raised on the call
- CTA: "Reply to this email if you have any questions"

**Email 2: The Urgency (Day 2)**
- Subject: "Quick question about [their goal]"
- Content: Reference something specific from the call, introduce soft urgency (limited spots, price increasing, timeline)
- CTA: "Ready to get started? [Link to payment/sign-up]"

**Email 3: Testimonial Match (Day 4)**
- Subject: "Someone in a similar situation as you..."
- Content: Case study that matches their specific situation/objection from the call
- CTA: "If [client name] can do it, so can you. [Link]"

**Email 4: The Cost of Waiting (Day 7)**
- Subject: "What another month of [pain point] is costing you"
- Content: Quantify the cost of inaction, contrast with the investment, show the ROI math
- CTA: "Let's not let another month go by. [Link]"

**Email 5: Objection Buster (Day 10)**
- Subject: "I wanted to address something"
- Content: Address their specific objection (ideally based on call notes), provide new evidence or angle
- CTA: "Want to hop on a quick follow-up? [Booking link]"

**Email 6: Final Offer (Day 14)**
- Subject: "Last chance — [specific incentive]"
- Content: Time-limited incentive (bonus, discount, payment plan), clear deadline
- CTA: "This expires [date]. [Link]"

# PART 3: OPT-IN EMAIL SEQUENCE (AI Copywriting SOP)

## 5-Email Opt-In Sequence — Complete AI Prompt

For VSL funnels that have an opt-in OR for lead magnet funnels that require inputting an email. These prospects demonstrated interest by opting in and potentially watching some or all of your VSL, yet something prevented them from taking immediate action.

### Verbatim Prompt

```
You have been provided with comprehensive documentation about an offer including Benefits, Value Proposition, Pain Points, Questions, Concerns, Sales Argument, and the core VSL script. Your task is to create a strategic five-email sequence for prospects who opted into the VSL funnel but did not immediately book a call. This sequence must work as an integrated system that progressively overcomes resistance while building desire and urgency to schedule a consultation.

Understanding the Psychological Context
These prospects have demonstrated interest by opting in and potentially watching some or all of your VSL, yet something prevented them from taking immediate action. They exist in a specific psychological state characterized by simultaneous interest and hesitation. Your email sequence must diagnose and address the specific barriers preventing action while maintaining the momentum created by the initial engagement. Each email serves as both a continuation of the VSL conversation and a standalone persuasion piece that moves them closer to booking a call.

Email Sequence Architecture and Timing
The sequence should deploy over five days, with strategic spacing that balances persistence with respect. The psychological journey moves from reengagement and curiosity renewal through education and trust building to urgency and final conversion. # PART 6: EMAIL COPY PRINCIPLES
```

## Structure Every Email

```
SUBJECT LINE (6-10 words, curiosity or benefit)
PREVIEW TEXT (expands on subject, never default)
---------------------
HOOK (first line — open loop or bold statement)
BODY (2-3 short paragraphs — one idea per paragraph)
PROOF (if applicable — stat, testimonial, case study)
CTA (one clear action — never multiple)
P.S. (most-read part of the email — put best hook here)
```

## Rules

- **One CTA per email** — Never ask them to do two things
- **Short paragraphs** — 1-3 sentences max
- **P.S. line is mandatory** — It's the second most-read part after the subject line
- **Open loops** — "Tomorrow I'll show you the exact framework..."
- **Personal tone** — Write like a person, not a company
- **Specific > generic** — "helped 47 advisors add $12M in AUM" beats "helped many clients grow"
- **Length:** 200-400 words for pre-call emails (1-2 minute read), 300-600 words for broadcast value emails, 800-1200 words for broadcast sales emails
- **Mobile-optimized** — short paragraphs, scannable, no dense text blocks
- **Reading level:** 4th-5th grade for pre-call, accessible level for all
- **Format:** Pure text with line breaks for readability. No bold, italics, bullets, or special formatting in broadcast emails.

## Voice Consistency Requirements Across Email Sequences

Every email must sound like it could only have come from this person:
- Use their specific phrases, typical examples, preferred analogies
- If they talk about business through sports metaphors, use sports metaphors
- If they never curse, don't curse
- If they frequently use rhetorical questions, include them
- Value emails should match their teaching/content voice
- Sales emails should match their enrollment conversation voice
- The shift between teaching and selling should feel natural — like the same person in different contexts

## Never Do This in AI-Generated Emails

- Make up fake stories — no fictional "clients," "buddies," or situations presented as real
- Create false case studies or testimonials — only use real examples from provided materials
- Present hypotheticals as facts — if using examples for illustration, clearly label them
- Lie or fabricate any information — everything must be truthful and authentic
- Plagiarize content — all content must be original or properly attributed

## SMO Pre-Call Email Frequency Strategy

> **Source:** Sell More Online (SMO) Pre-Call Email Outline by Evan Seech + 96-page Pre-Call Email Vault

The existing pre-call guidance in this skill (Part 5) recommends 1-2 marketing emails per day in the pre-call window. SMO operates at a significantly higher frequency and has documented results across multiple niches to support it.

### SMO Frequency Guidelines

- **Minimum:** 2 emails per day across a 3-4 day booking-to-call window
- **Maximum capacity:** 3-6 emails per day (SMO runs up to 21 emails across the full pre-call window at max volume)
- **Spacing:** Same-day emails should be spaced 3-5 hours apart
- **Ramp-up approach:** Start at 2-3 emails per day, then increase volume over time as long as you see:
  - Strong open rates consistently across all emails
  - Strong content consumption (video views, link clicks)
  - Good feedback from leads (replies, engagement)
  - Increased show rates

### Why This Frequency Works in Pre-Call Windows

These are NOT marketing emails or broadcast newsletters. They are trust-building assets sent to people who have already booked a call and are in a critical conversion window. The reasoning:

1. **The prospect already opted in.** They booked a call — they expect communication. Per the Email Matrix survey data (Part 1), "Expecting It" is the #1 reason people open emails.
2. **The booking-to-call window is a deterioration period.** Every hour between booking and the call, the prospect's excitement decays and competing priorities take over. High-frequency touchpoints counteract this decay.
3. **Each email serves a distinct trust-building function.** These aren't repetitive sales pitches — each email addresses a different objection, provides different social proof, or answers a different question. The goal is to make the prospect arrive pre-sold.
4. **Show rate directly impacts revenue.** A 20% improvement in show rate at scale can represent significant revenue. The cost of sending additional emails is near zero; the cost of no-shows is high.

### CRM Implementation Note

Running 3-6 emails per day in the pre-call window requires "Time Until" automations (countdown from the scheduled call date). Most CRMs support this through:
- **HubSpot:** Native "Time Until" workflows
- **GHL (GoHighLevel):** Appointment-triggered automations with delay steps
- **ActiveCampaign:** Date-based automations
- For CRMs without native support, use basic delay sequences: Email 1 immediately after booking, then space subsequent emails 3-5 hours apart using delay steps.

## Pre-Call Email Content Types (SMO Framework)

The SMO Pre-Call Email Outline defines 21 distinct email types, each serving a specific psychological function in the booking-to-call window. These map to and expand upon the existing 4 Content Pillars (Questions, 2nd-Layer Questions, Objections, Expectations) in Part 2.

### Type 1: Welcome/Confirmation Email
- Confirm the booking, set expectations for what happens next
- Re-send confirmation page or company research deck with breakout videos
- Add personal touches (selfies, personal note from the rep/founder)
- **Maps to Pillar:** Expectations

### Type 2: FAQ Attack Email
- Address the most common questions prospects have about the offer
- Hit the real questions in their mind that PREVENT them from showing up
- Be direct and impactful — don't hold back
- **Maps to Pillar:** Questions

### Type 3: Engagement Bait Email
- Short, personal-feeling question asking what their biggest concern is
- "What's the one question in your head that prevents you from taking action on [solving their problem]?"
- Goal: generate replies, create two-way dialogue, identify individual objections
- **Maps to Pillar:** 2nd-Layer Questions

### Type 4: Why People Don't Show Up Email
- Directly address every reason they have to not show up
- Combat each objection and reason head-on
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 5: Deeper Tactical Questions Email
- Follow-up to the FAQ email with more nuanced, tactical questions
- Provide real value in the answers
- **Maps to Pillar:** 2nd-Layer Questions

### Type 6: Real Urgency Email
- Give real reasons (backed by third-party data if possible) why they need to act NOW
- No manufactured scarcity — genuine market-driven urgency
- Tie urgency to macro trends, competitive dynamics, or time-sensitive factors
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 7: Tactics & Strategy Email
- Super valuable and value-dense — break down the system, how it gets results, what makes it different
- Visual diagrams of the system work well
- Can include engagement bait: "I have a podcast, an SOP, or a folder of quick videos breaking this down — which do you want?"
- **Maps to Pillar:** Questions

### Type 8: Objection Handling Email
- Hit the big objections head-on: cost, time, skepticism, "grand narrative" doubts
- Be direct — tell them explicitly you want to address their objections
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 9: Case Study Email
- Break down the actual TACTICS of a case study (don't just dump results)
- Use the case study to answer common questions, break limiting beliefs, and handle objections
- Articulate the transformation — before state, what was done, results, timeline
- **Maps to Pillar:** Questions

### Type 10: Expectations Email
- Paint a clear picture of what they can expect after moving forward
- Cover BOTH the downsides and upsides — builds trust through honesty
- **Maps to Pillar:** Expectations

### Type 11: Reminder/Why Email
- Reinforce the benefits and remind them why they booked in the first place
- Bring them back to their original "why"
- **Maps to Pillar:** Expectations

### Type 12: Third-Party Data Email
- Pull in external data that supports the effectiveness of the product/service
- Tie that data back to why the prospect should act now
- Powerful because it's not self-reported — it comes from a neutral source
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 13: Big Opportunity/Grand Narrative Email
- Frame the product/service within a larger market opportunity or macro trend
- Position the prospect at the forefront of a revolution, gold rush, or wave
- Create urgency through market timing, not artificial scarcity
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 14: Education/Value Email
- Pure value — share insights, tips, strategies, and tactics related to the offer
- Great place to hyperlink YouTube videos, podcasts, or educational content
- Build trust and authority
- **Maps to Pillar:** Questions

### Type 15: Comparison Email
- Show how the offer stacks up against alternatives, competitors, methods, programs, agencies
- Highlight why this is the obvious choice
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 16: Addressing Fears Email
- Address any fears or doubts about taking the next step
- Different from objections — fears are emotional (what if I fail, what if I waste money, what if it's a scam)
- **Maps to Pillar:** Objections

### Type 17: Future Pace Email
- Help them visualize the transformation they'll experience after saying yes
- Make it SUPER detailed and articulate — they should be able to see it
- **Maps to Pillar:** Expectations

### Type 18: Personal Story Email
- Share a relatable story that connects emotionally and reinforces the message
- Founder story, client story, or team story
- **Maps to Pillar:** Questions

### Type 19: Additional FAQs Email
- Second round of FAQ content, going deeper than Email 2
- Can address questions that tend to come up later in the consideration process
- **Maps to Pillar:** 2nd-Layer Questions

### Type 20: Sell The Call Email
- Explicitly sell what will happen on the call itself
- Frame the call as a valuable experience regardless of whether they buy
- **Maps to Pillar:** Expectations

### Type 21: Personal Contact Details & Socials Email
- Send contact details and social links so they can reach out before the call
- Re-link the confirmation page and any key trust/sales assets
- Reduce friction — make it easy to ask questions or reschedule
- **Maps to Pillar:** Expectations

### Implementation Notes

You do NOT need to use all 21 types. The outline is a menu to select from based on:
- **Length of the booking-to-call window** (1 day = 3-5 emails, 3-4 days = 10-20 emails)
- **Complexity of the offer** (higher ticket = more trust-building emails needed)
- **Avatar sophistication** (more sophisticated buyers may need more data/comparison emails, less sophisticated buyers need more FAQ/fear emails)
- **Available assets** (if the client has strong case studies, lean into Type 9; if they have great third-party data, lean into Type 12)

## Contradiction Note: Email Frequency in Pre-Call Windows

There is an apparent contradiction between the existing guidance in this skill and the SMO methodology:

| Source | Recommendation | Context |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **This skill (Part 5)** | 1-2 marketing emails per day | Conservative approach, paired with breakout videos on thank-you page |
| **SMO Pre-Call Email Outline** | 2-3 emails per day minimum, up to 6/day at max capacity | Aggressive approach across 3-4 day windows, documented across multiple niches |

### Resolution: Both Are Correct for Their Context

**Standard marketing emails** (broadcasts, opt-in sequences, campaign flows) should follow normal frequency guidelines. Over-mailing a general list degrades sender reputation, increases unsubscribes, and triggers spam filters. The ESP Requirements section (Part 1) applies here.

**Pre-call sequences are a different category entirely.** They are trust-building touchpoints during a critical conversion window, not broadcast marketing. Key differences:

1. **Audience state:** These people booked a call — they are high-intent and expecting communication
2. **Window duration:** The booking-to-call window is typically 1-4 days, not an ongoing relationship
3. **Email purpose:** Each email serves a distinct trust-building function (case study, objection handling, FAQ, etc.) — they are not repetitive pitches
4. **Success metric:** Show rate and close rate, not open rate or unsubscribe rate
5. **Deliverability impact:** Pre-call emails go to a small, high-intent segment — not your entire list — so volume has minimal ESP impact

### Practical Guidance

- **If the client has no pre-call sequence:** Start with 2-3 emails per day (the existing Part 5 template is a good starting point)
- **If the client already has a working pre-call sequence:** Incrementally add emails and monitor show rates, open rates, and lead feedback
- **If the client has a long booking-to-call window (3+ days):** The SMO approach of 3-6 emails per day becomes more appropriate to fill the gap and prevent excitement decay
- **If the client has a short booking-to-call window (same-day or next-day):** 2-3 total emails is sufficient — no need for high volume

# Email Sequences

> **Routing Note:** This skill handles **list-based funnel emails** — welcome, nurture, conversion, launch, re-engagement, post-purchase. Use when the funnel ends in a **checkout or opt-in** (no sales call involved). If the funnel ends in a **booked sales call** and you need pre-call, post-call, no-show, or broadcast emails, use **email-flows** instead.

Most lead magnets die in the inbox. Someone downloads your thing, gets one "here's your download" email, and never hears from you again. Or worse—they get blasted with "BUY NOW" emails before you've earned any trust.

The gap between "opted in" and "bought" is where money is made or lost. This skill builds sequences that bridge that gap.

---

## The core job

Transform a lead magnet subscriber into a customer through a **strategic email sequence** that:
- Delivers immediate value (the lead magnet)
- Builds trust and relationship
- Creates desire for the paid offer
- Converts without being sleazy

**Output format:** Complete email sequences with subject lines, preview text, full copy, send timing, and CTAs.

---

## Sequence Types

| Sequence | Purpose | Length | When to Use |
|----------|---------|--------|-------------|
| **Welcome** | Deliver value, build relationship | 5-7 emails | After opt-in |
| **Nurture** | Provide value, build trust | 4-6 emails | Between welcome and pitch |
| **Conversion** | Sell the product | 4-7 emails | When ready to pitch |
| **Launch** | Time-bound campaign | 6-10 emails | Product launch |
| **Re-engagement** | Win back cold subscribers | 3-4 emails | Inactive 30+ days |
| **Post-Purchase** | Onboard, reduce refunds, upsell | 4-6 emails | After purchase |

## Before Starting: Gather Context

Get these inputs before writing any sequence:

1. **What's the lead magnet?** (What did they opt in for?)

4. **What's the bridge?** (How does free → paid make logical sense?)
5. **What voice/brand?** (Run brand-voice skill first if not defined)
6. **What objections?** (Why might they NOT buy?)

## The Welcome Sequence (5-7 emails)

This is the most important sequence. First impressions compound.

### Purpose
- Deliver the lead magnet
- Set expectations
- Begin the relationship
- Identify engaged subscribers
- Plant seeds for the offer

### The Framework: DELIVER → CONNECT → VALUE → BRIDGE

```
Email 1: DELIVER — Give them what they came for
Email 2: CONNECT — Share your story, build rapport
Email 3: VALUE — Teach something useful (quick win)
Email 4: VALUE — Teach something else (builds authority)
Email 5: BRIDGE — Show what's possible with more help
Email 6: SOFT PITCH — Introduce the offer gently
Email 7: DIRECT PITCH — Make the ask
```

### Email 1: Delivery (Send immediately)

**Purpose:** Deliver the lead magnet, set expectations, get first micro-engagement.

**Subject line formulas:**
- "[Lead magnet name] is inside"
- "Your [lead magnet] + quick start guide"
- "Here's [what they asked for]"

**Structure:**
```
[Greeting — keep it simple]

[Deliver the goods — link to lead magnet]

[Quick start — one action they can take in next 5 minutes]

[Set expectations — what emails are coming]

[Micro-CTA — hit reply, answer a question, or take one action]

[Sign off]
```

## The Conversion Sequence (4-7 emails)

For when you're ready to pitch—either after welcome sequence or as a standalone campaign.

### The Framework: OPEN → DESIRE → PROOF → OBJECTION → URGENCY → CLOSE

```
Email 1: OPEN — Introduce the offer, core promise
Email 2: DESIRE — Paint the transformation, show the gap
Email 3: PROOF — Testimonials, case studies, results
Email 4: OBJECTION — Handle the biggest "but..."
Email 5: URGENCY — Why now matters (if authentic)
Email 6: CLOSE — Final push, clear CTA
Email 7: LAST CALL — Deadline reminder (if applicable)
```

### Timing
- Standard: Every 2 days
- Launch: Daily or every other day
- Deadline: Final 3 emails in 3 days

---

## The Launch Sequence (6-10 emails)

For time-bound campaigns: product launches, promotions, cohort opens.

### The Framework: SEED → OPEN → VALUE → PROOF → URGENCY → CLOSE

**Pre-Launch (Optional, 1-2 emails):**
- Seed interest, build anticipation
- "Something's coming" without revealing

**Cart Open (2-3 emails):**
- Announcement, full details
- Value deep-dive, transformation
- Social proof, testimonials

**Mid-Launch (2-3 emails):**
- Objection handling
- Case study or story
- FAQ or "is this for me?"

**Cart Close (2-3 emails):**
- Urgency (24-48 hours)
- Final testimonial
- Last call (deadline day)

### Launch Email Timing
```
Day -3: Seed (optional)
Day -1: Coming tomorrow
Day 0: Cart open (morning)
Day 0: Cart open (evening, different angle)
Day 2: Deep-dive on value
Day 4: Social proof
Day 5: Objection handling
Day 6: 48-hour warning
Day 7: 24-hour warning (morning)
Day 7: Final hours (evening)
Day 7: Last call (before midnight)
```

## The Re-engagement Sequence (3-4 emails)

For subscribers who haven't opened in 30+ days.

### The Framework: PATTERN INTERRUPT → VALUE → DECISION

```
Email 1: Pattern interrupt — different subject line style, acknowledge absence
Email 2: Pure value — best content, no ask
Email 3: Direct question — do you want to stay?
Email 4: Final — removing from list (creates urgency)
```

### Subject Line Examples
- "Did I do something wrong?"
- "Should I stop emailing you?"
- "Breaking up is hard to do"
- "You're about to miss [thing]"
- "[First name], still there?"

---

## Subject Line Formulas

### What Gets Opens

**1. Curiosity Gap**
- "The [X] mistake that cost me [Y]"
- "Why [surprising thing] actually works"
- "I was wrong about [topic]"

**2. Direct Benefit**
- "How to [outcome] in [timeframe]"
- "[Number] ways to [benefit]"
- "The fastest way to [result]"

**3. Personal/Story**
- "Quick story about [topic]"
- "What happened when I [action]"
- "The email I almost didn't send"

**4. Question**
- "Can I ask you something?"
- "What would you do with [outcome]?"
- "Are you making this mistake?"

**5. Urgency (when real)**
- "[X] hours left"
- "Closing tonight"
- "Last chance: [offer]"

**6. Pattern Interrupt**
- "." (just a period)
- "So..."
- "Bad news"
- "[First name]"

### What Kills Opens

- ALL CAPS
- Excessive punctuation!!!
- "Newsletter #47"
- "[COMPANY NAME] Weekly Update"
- Clickbait that doesn't deliver
- Same format every time

---

## Email Copy Principles

### The P.S. Is Prime Real Estate
40% of people read the P.S. first. Use it for:
- The core CTA
- A second hook
- Personal note
- Deadline reminder

### One CTA Per Email
Multiple CTAs = no CTAs. Every email should have ONE clear action.

Exception: Delivery email can have "download" + "reply with question"

### Short Paragraphs
1-3 sentences max. Email is scanned, not read.

### Preview Text Matters
First 40-90 characters appear in inbox preview. Make them count.

**Bad:** "Having trouble viewing this email?"
**Good:** "[Continuation of subject line curiosity]"

### Open Loops
Create curiosity within emails:
- "I'll explain why tomorrow."
- "But that's not even the interesting part."
- "The third one surprised me."

### Specificity Creates Credibility
- Not "made money" → "$47,329 in one day"
- Not "many customers" → "2,847 customers"
- Not "recently" → "Last Tuesday"

---

## Sequence Architecture Patterns

### The Straight Line
```
Email 1 → Email 2 → Email 3 → Email 4 → Pitch
```
Simple. Works for short sequences. No branches.

### The Branch
```
Email 1 → Email 2 → [Clicked?] → YES: Pitch sequence
                              → NO: More value sequence
```
Behavior-based. More sophisticated. Requires automation.

### The Hybrid
```
Welcome (5 emails) → [Wait 7 days] → Conversion (5 emails) → [No purchase] → Nurture (ongoing)
```
Full lifecycle. Most complete.

---

## Timing Guidelines

### Send Frequency by Sequence

| Sequence | Frequency | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Welcome | Days 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 | Front-load value |
| Nurture | Weekly or 2x/week | Consistent rhythm |
| Conversion | Every 2 days | Enough touch without annoying |
| Launch | Daily or every other day | Intensity justified by deadline |
| Re-engagement | Days 0, 3, 7, 10 | Give time to respond |

### Best Send Times
- B2B: Tuesday-Thursday, 9-11am recipient time
- B2C: Tuesday-Thursday, 7-9am or 7-9pm
- Avoid: Monday morning (inbox overload), Friday afternoon (checked out)

### When to Start Selling
- Low price (<$100): After 3-5 value emails
- Medium price ($100-500): After 5-7 value emails
- High price (>$500): After 7-10 value emails or sales call

Trust required scales with price.

## The Test

A good email sequence:

1. **Delivers value before asking** — At least 3-5 value emails before pitch
2. **Has clear purpose per email** — Each email does ONE job
3. **Sounds human** — Not corporate, not guru, not AI
4. **Creates momentum** — Each email makes them want the next
5. **Handles objections** — Addresses the "but..." before they think it
6. **Has one CTA** — Every email drives one action
7. **Respects the reader** — Can unsubscribe easily, not manipulative

If the sequence feels like "content, content, content, BUY NOW BUY NOW" — it failed.
