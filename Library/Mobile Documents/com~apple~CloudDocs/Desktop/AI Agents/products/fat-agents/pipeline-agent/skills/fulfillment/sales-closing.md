# PART 1: THE INTEREST SPECTRUM FRAMEWORK

## The Core Framework

Every lead that comes through your funnel exists somewhere on the Interest Spectrum. This is not theory -- it is how human psychology works with buying decisions. Interest levels are NOT static. They move up and down the spectrum constantly. If you do not actively work to move someone up, they naturally drift down.

```
No Interest --> Curiosity --> General Interest --> High Interest --> Conviction
```

### Level 1: No Interest
These people have completely checked out. They are not thinking about your solution at all. They were once curious or interested but drifted back down because nobody nurtured them.

### Level 2: Curiosity
Something caught their attention. Maybe they saw an ad, maybe a friend mentioned something. They are curious but not committed to anything. Most inbound leads enter here.

### Level 3: General Interest
They have moved beyond curiosity. They are actually considering solving the problem you address, but they are not in a rush. They are open to learning more but will not prioritize it.

### Level 4: High Interest
These are in-market buyers. They have a specific problem they need solved, and they are actively looking for solutions. They respond quickly and ask good questions.

### Level 5: Conviction
They are ready to buy. They just need a few final questions answered before they pull the trigger. Deals close in this zone.

### Critical Insight: Interest Decay

A lead that booked with high interest on Monday can be at curiosity by Thursday if you do not actively nurture them. Every single interaction you have with a lead should be designed to move them UP this spectrum. Every email, every text, every piece of content you send should increase their interest level, not maintain it.

**The setter/closer's job is to move leads from wherever they enter (usually Curiosity or General Interest) to High Interest or Conviction before the call happens. Deals get done in the zone between High Interest and Conviction.**

---

## The Comparison Bias Problem

SDRs develop what is called "comparison bias." They start comparing leads to each other instead of evaluating each lead on their own merits.

**How it manifests:** When an SDR talks to someone at High Interest or Conviction level, that person sounds excited, gives detailed answers, asks great questions. Then when they talk to someone at Curiosity or General Interest level, that person gives shorter answers, seems less enthusiastic, maybe asks basic questions.

**The mistake:** The SDR thinks the second person is "unqualified" when in reality they are just at a different interest level. They might be completely financially qualified and capable of buying -- they just need more information to move up the spectrum.

**Why it is dangerous:** When SDRs have high lead volume and feel abundance, they start treating leads like they are not scarce. They will cancel perfectly good leads because they seem "less qualified" compared to higher-interest leads they just talked to.

**The rule:** Interest level and qualification are two completely different things. A curious lead with a million dollars in the bank is still a qualified lead. They just need more nurturing than someone already at high interest.

# PART 2: THE REAL JOB OF AN SDR

## Perceived Job vs. Actual Job

**Perceived job of an SDR:** Qualify and schedule.

**Actual job of an SDR:**
1. Qualify leads appropriately (not over-qualify based on interest level)
2. Increase the probability of scheduled calls showing up
3. Gather data for tailored communication
4. Set a positive tone for the business relationship

When an SDR reaches out, they should be focused on gathering information that can be used to customize the sales process for that specific lead: What are their specific challenges? What solutions have they tried before? What is their timeline? What is driving their interest right now?

This information should go into your CRM so that both the SDR and the closer can send tailored content that actually resonates with that specific person's situation.

## The Restaurant Analogy

Think about booking a reservation at a fancy Michelin star restaurant. Most people book through OpenTable or Resy. Then someone from the restaurant calls to confirm the reservation.

**Bad experience:** The person sounded rude, pretentious, or like they did not care about you coming in. That makes you question the restaurant before you even arrive.

**Good experience:** The person was friendly, professional, made you feel valued and excited about your upcoming dinner. You show up primed for a great experience.

**The parallel:** Your SDRs are the hostess confirming the reservation. They are setting the tone for the entire experience your lead is going to have with your business.

**A great restaurant does not just call to confirm.** They ask about dietary restrictions, special occasions, seating preferences. They gather information to make your experience better. That is what SDRs should do -- gather information that allows your business to customize the experience.

**When done right:** The lead hangs up thinking "Wow, these people really care about helping me. I'm excited for our conversation."

**When done wrong:** The lead thinks "These people just want to qualify me out or sell me something. This feels pushy."

**The difference between those two outcomes is often the difference between a 35% show rate and a 65% show rate.**

## Avoiding the Selfish Qualification Trap

Most SDRs are trained to ask questions that benefit the business:
- "What's your budget?"
- "When are you looking to start?"
- "Have you worked with someone like us before?"

These questions come across as selfish and one-sided. The lead immediately feels like they are being interrogated rather than helped.

**Instead, train SDRs to ask consultative questions:**
- "What's driving your interest in this right now?"
- "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with [problem]?"
- "What would an ideal solution look like for you?"
- "What have you tried before? What worked and what didn't?"
- "If we could solve [their specific problem], what would that mean for your business?"
- "What's your timeline for getting this resolved?"
- "Who else is involved in making this decision?"

These questions gather the same qualification information, but they do it in a way that feels consultative rather than interrogative.

## SDR Timing Sweet Spot

When an SDR reaches out matters. If they reach out too quickly after someone books, they interrupt the natural nurturing process from the confirmation page, email sequences, and content remarketing. If they wait too long, the lead's interest level starts dropping.

**The sweet spot:** Somewhere between your confirmation page experience and your value-dense email sequences. The SDR outreach should feel like a natural part of the process, not an interruption or afterthought.

# PART 3: SHOW RATE OPTIMIZATION

## The 30% Multiplier

When a salesperson successfully establishes a communication line with a lead prior to their scheduled call, show rates improve by up to 30%. Just from getting communication going before the call.

| Approach | Typical Show Rate |
|----------|------------------|
| No pre-call nurture | 35-40% |
| Automated emails only | 45-55% |
| Manual outreach + emails | **65-75%** |
| Manual + emails + SMS + retargeting | **75-85%** |

## Why Manual Beats Automation Every Time

Think about your own behavior. How easy is it to ignore an automated email? Pretty easy. How easy is it to ignore an automated text from a business number? Also pretty easy.

But how easy is it to ignore a text that comes from an iPhone number from a real person who clearly took the time to write something specific to you? Much harder.

That is the edge that manual outreach gives you. You are a human being reaching out to another human being. Automation cannot replicate that. The marketing team will send automated emails and texts, but that does not permit laziness from the salesperson. It is a team effort -- the salesperson's personal outreach makes the difference.

## The Engagement Bait Technique

The key to successful manual outreach. Instead of trying to qualify someone or pitch them right away, you get them to engage with you in conversation.

**How it works:** Reach out with a text (ideally from an iPhone) that offers two specific, valuable pieces of content related to their situation. Let them choose which one they want, and let them choose how they want to receive it.

### Engagement Bait Script Template

```
"Hey [Name]! [Your name] here with [Company]. I just had some time to get
back to you about our [product/service] you filled out the form for. I have
two things I can send you that I think will help you a ton. I've got [specific
asset #1] and I've got [specific asset #2]. Let me know which one you'd like
and whether you'd want it sent here via text or email. By the way, I'm the
guy/gal you're talking to at [call time]. Excited to connect!"
```

### Why Engagement Bait Works

1. **It is personal and human** -- obviously not automated
2. **It offers immediate value** -- not a pitch
3. **It gives them choices** -- which creates engagement (they have to respond to pick one)
4. **It establishes you as their point of contact** -- they know who they are talking to
5. **It builds anticipation for the call** -- references the upcoming conversation
6. **It opens a conversation channel** -- once they reply, you have a live text thread

### Matching Assets to Interest Levels

The key to successful engagement bait is matching your assets to what the person actually needs based on their interest level.

**For someone at Curiosity level, offer:**
- Educational content about the problem
- Case studies showing the solution works
- FAQ videos addressing common concerns

**For someone at General Interest level, offer:**
- Specific implementation strategies
- ROI calculators or assessments
- Comparison guides between different approaches

**For someone at High Interest level, offer:**
- Detailed process breakdowns
- Timeline and next steps information
- Pricing and package comparisons

The goal is always to move them up the interest spectrum by giving them exactly the information they need at their current level.

## Selfie Video Texts: The Secret Weapon

Quick personable video texts are one of the most effective forms of manual outreach for getting response rates higher and increasing show rates.

**Why selfie videos work:**
- Obviously not automated
- Personal and human
- Stand out in text messages
- Show you took extra effort
- Build rapport before the call

**Scaling with AI:** Use tools like Descript to make this scalable. Record one video, then use AI to replace the name for each new lead. The AI matches your voice and makes it sound like you recorded a custom video for each person. One personalized selfie video from you or the business owner -- just edit the name to the new lead's name, AI replaces what you said with the new name, export and send.

## PDF of Proof Technique

Compile all proof assets into one massive PDF that sales teams can send to leads to increase trust and lower skepticism through extreme transparency.

**Case study:** A client with 500+ Amazon stores had P&L (profit and loss) statements for every client every month. They compressed all P&Ls into one giant PDF each month so sales and marketing could send it to potential clients.

**Results after implementing PDF of Proof:**
- Response rate on initial new inbound leads: 37% --> 53%
- Show rate on scheduled calls: 40% --> 67%

**The principle:** Most salespeople do not have enough resources at their disposal to leverage to get leads to respond and show up at higher rates. Press the business owner or sales manager for more value-driven follow-up assets. Get creative and deal with what the lead is really thinking about.

## Pre-Call Asset Stack

Create these assets to send between booking and call:

| "What to expect on the call" | Text/email | Sets expectations, reduces anxiety |
| ROI calculator or assessment | PDF or link | Shows potential return specific to their situation |
| FAQ document | PDF or video | Pre-answers the top questions that eat up call time |

## Reminder Cadence

# PART 4: CONNECTION RATE OPTIMIZATION

## The Contact Everyone Rule

**The principle:** Contact EVERY lead and treat them like they are qualified until they show clear signs they are not. Do not pre-qualify based on interest level. Financial qualification and interest level are two completely different things.

**Why this matters:** A client instructed her sales team to contact every lead no matter what, citing that she herself would lie on qualification forms even though she was financially qualified. Her sales team crushed the numbers of other teams in the same niche because they contacted more people. The other teams only contacted "qualified leads" which limited their numbers.

**Supporting data:** When polled, a majority of high-level agency owners and marketers said even if they were financially qualified, they would lie on lead forms.

**The mindset shift:** You as a salesperson only want to talk to the most qualified people to make the most money. That is fair. But there is a very good chance you are not close to being that busy. Most salespeople have a majority of their day open for texting and calling leads. Only when you reach superstar levels and have a setter laying up confirmed calls all day do you get that privilege. Until then, work the trenches.

## Rich, Busy People Prefer Text

High ticket products can be sold entirely over text without a phone call. Busy, wealthy buyers prefer text because it is efficient and respects their time.

**Key insight:** Most salespeople are only trained to sell over the phone. Therefore all their text communication is just pushing leads to a call, which leads view as selfish. Leads want to communicate via text by default and only get on the phone if necessary or once they have vetted you out.

**Demographic consideration:** The less qualified or older the buyer, the more they bias toward phone calls. The busier and wealthier the buyer, the more they bias toward text and Instagram DM.

## Social Media Lead Lookup Process

Get the lead's first and last name and look them up on Instagram, LinkedIn, and Facebook. This is huge for establishing communication outside of just text and emails.

**Impact:** This one piece of advice alone makes a 30% positive difference in getting in contact with unresponsive leads.

**Best practice:** Optimize your own social media profile for the business you represent so leads know you are a real person and work for the company you are reaching out on behalf of.

**Process:**
1. Get lead's full name from CRM
2. Search Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook
3. Review their content to find personalization hooks
4. Send a DM that references something specific from their profile
5. Use this as an additional communication channel alongside text and email

## Multiple Channel Strategy

Do not rely on one channel. Use all of them:
- iMessage / SMS text
- Email
- Instagram DM
- LinkedIn message
- Facebook message
- Phone call
- Selfie video text

Maximum reach = maximum connection rate.

# PART 5: INITIAL COMMUNICATION SCRIPTS

## Initial Outreach Script

```
"Hey [Name]! [Your name] here with [Company]. I just had some time to get
back to you about our [product/service] you filled out the form for. Would
be great to get on a call to discuss. Would you be open to that?"
```

If no response, pivot to a different style of communication. Get creative.

**Critical prerequisite:** None of these scripts work if you do not have conviction about what you are selling. If you do not genuinely believe that what you are trying to talk to them about will actually help them, and you do not deep down actually want to do a deal with that lead, there will be no deal. You have to actually want their business and mean it. It is transparent to a lead when you do not care about them versus when you do.

## The "We're All Busy" Hook

Use when a lead has shown interest and started ghosting you. Acknowledges that everyone is busy and positions your outreach as understanding and respectful of their time.

### Script Template

```
"Hey [Name], I know we're all busy, but I wanted to reach out because I
think I can help you with [specific problem]. I've got [specific solution/
case study/resource] that I think would be really valuable for your
situation. Would you like me to send that over?"
```

### Why It Works
- Shows empathy for their situation
- Positions you as helpful, not pushy
- Offers specific value
- Asks for permission (which people appreciate)

**Best used as:** A selfie video text follow-up. Record yourself saying this naturally and send as a video message. The personal touch of video combined with the empathetic hook produces very high response rates.

## The "Red Flags" Follow-Up

Use when a lead has shown interest and started ghosting. This is a pattern interrupt that flips the dynamic -- instead of chasing, you challenge.

### Script Template

```
"Lmk - scares me when potential clients aren't responsive to simple
questions and comes off as red flag [red flag emoji]

Our best deals respond, are transparent with us, and get us what we need
when we ask

If we want to do a few million a month together I need you onboard fully"
```

### Why It Works
- Pattern interrupt: leads expect more "checking in" messages, not this
- Flips the frame: positions YOU as the one evaluating THEM
- Creates urgency through potential loss
- Usually results in the real objections or stall coming out

**Case study:** A potential client went silent for about 2 weeks. The Red Flags follow-up was sent. He replied with a lengthy text about needing 2 extra weeks to launch. Two weeks later, the deal closed successfully. The Red Flags text surfaces the real reason behind the ghosting.

## Value-First Outreach Method

**The problem with 99% of businesses:** They use one of two approaches:

**Approach 1 -- Random conversational opener (trying to build rapport):**
- "yo, hey how's it going"
- "hey saw you were an [insert prospect type], would love to connect"
- "hey how long have you been running your xyz business?"

**Approach 2 -- Straight pitch:**
- "hey x, saw you were an [insert prospect] and wanted to reach out. We will get you [insert outcome] in [timeframe] with [risk reversal], are you interested?"

**What to do instead -- Lead with value:**

Prospects do not want to be reached out to, qualified, and booked. They want to be educated on an inefficiency in their life and shown a more efficient way of achieving their desired outcome.

**The method:**
1. Lead with value first
2. Then ask permission to explain how your customers are achieving X outcome
3. Notice: you are NOT asking if they want X outcome outright. You are asking if you can educate them on the specific process your customers use to achieve X outcome. Subtle, but powerful difference.
4. Once they agree to learn more, explain your offer in greater detail
5. Then invite them to a free value session

# PART 6: THE MICRO COMMITMENTS FRAMEWORK

## The Problem

A lead agrees to a value session. You send your booking link or times to talk. Crickets. They never book. What happened?

Even though they showed interest, there is still that last moment of inertia, fear, and hesitancy before committing to the action.

## The Solution: Stacking Micro Commitments

The more times someone says yes to something, the more likely they will keep saying yes in the future. Each small "yes" builds momentum toward the big "yes."

### The Micro-Commitment Ladder

1. "Would you want me to send that case study?" --> Yes
2. "Which format -- text or email?" --> Text (a choice, which is also a yes)
3. "Was that helpful?" --> Yes
4. "Want me to show you how [client name] implemented it?" --> Yes
5. "Would it make sense to hop on a quick call to discuss your specific situation?" --> Yes (the booking)
6. **Final micro commitment before sending the link:** "Awesome. I'll send the link to my calendar then, ok?" --> Ok (one last yes)
7. Then send the booking link

**Why this works:** By the time you send the booking link, they have said "yes" 5-6 times. The momentum of those yeses carries them through the final action of actually clicking the link and booking. Without this ladder, you are asking for a big commitment (booking a call) after only one or two interactions, which feels like too big a leap.

# PART 7: FOLLOW-UP STRATEGY

## Selfish Follow-Up (What NOT to Do)

These messages are one-sided and only geared toward your benefit:

- "Following up on my last message"
- "Following up"
- "Want to jump on a quick call together?"
- "I have 15 min free if you're available?"
- "Haven't heard from you, are you still interested?"
- "Just following up..."
- "Free 15 minutes?"
- "What's your budget?"

**The rule:** Selfish follow-up that is perceived as annoying is usually one-sided and only geared toward your benefit. Always go by the golden rule of earning the call with value first.

## Value-Driven Follow-Up Sequence

Instead of selfish follow-up, every touchpoint should offer something valuable:

**Follow-up 1 (Day 1 after no response):** Engagement Bait -- offer two specific assets
**Follow-up 2 (Day 2-3):** Selfie video text with the "We're All Busy" hook
**Follow-up 3 (Day 4-5):** PDF of Proof or relevant case study
**Follow-up 4 (Day 7):** Social media DM with personalized message referencing their content
**Follow-up 5 (Day 10-14):** The "Red Flags" follow-up (pattern interrupt)
**Follow-up 6 (Day 21+):** Re-engage with new asset or updated case study

## Be Persistent But Not Pushy

Find the right balance by following up at reasonable intervals (every few days) and using a mix of different value-driven styles. This is why most salespeople give up -- they simply run out of creative valuable follow-up and revert to selfish one-sided follow-up or just give up on the lead completely.

## Low Morale = No Deals

Your morale is EVERYTHING to making sales. Like sports, it is a game of morale. As soon as one team thinks they are losing, they start losing worse. The team that believes they have momentum starts crushing even more.

You have to stay sold -- as convicted as it gets -- that you are going to pull deals from the leads you are working. The moment you are not convinced of that, you are wasting the business's money by working leads you do not believe in closing. Either recommit or get off that strategy and onto something else.

# PART 9: CONFIRMATION PAGE OPTIMIZATION (SHOW RATE SYSTEM)

## Why Confirmation Pages Matter

When you push people through any funnel (call funnel, webinar registration, challenge funnel), the confirmation page is the first place they land after taking action. This page can dramatically influence your show rate, frame prospects in the right mindset, and shorten your sales cycle.

### Key Functions of the Confirmation Page
1. **Acknowledge the Action:** Confirm they successfully booked or opted in
2. **Address Immediate Objections:** Eliminate real reasons for them to bail
3. **Accelerate Due Diligence:** People are going to research you anyway -- guide them
4. **Modern Best Practice:** Skip the useless "Thanks, see you soon" approach. Equip them with what they need to feel confident in showing up

## Modern Due Diligence: Controlling the Narrative

In the minutes, hours, or days between booking and the actual conversation, prospects are going to do their homework on you. If you do not guide it, they will find (or invent) answers on their own.

### What People Specifically Look For

**Legitimacy and Credibility:**
- "Are these people even real?"
- "Have they scammed anyone?"
- "Is their address, contact info, or company presence legit?"
- "Who else has trusted them?"

**Price and Cost Objections:**
- "Why is this so expensive?"
- "Can I get it cheaper elsewhere?"
- "Is it worth the money?"
- "Will I go into debt or is there a financing option?"

**Risk and Worst-Case Scenarios:**
- "What if this fails?"
- "What if they disappear or file bankruptcy?"
- "Do they offer a refund or guarantee -- and is it real or just marketing fluff?"
- "Am I stuck paying if I don't see results?"

**Timeline and Logistics:**
- "How long does it take to see results?"
- "What's required of me?"
- "Will this conflict with my schedule?"
- "Is this a one-time session or an ongoing program?"

**Success and Expectations:**
- "Are these testimonials cherry-picked?"
- "What's the average or realistic outcome?"
- "Can I see someone who tried and didn't do well -- what happened then?"
- "How many people achieve big wins?"

**Social Proof and Outside Perspectives:**
- "What do Reddit users say about them?"
- "Are their Trustpilot reviews genuine or fake?"
- "Has anyone posted a rant about them on social media?"
- "Do people keep talking about the same issues?"

### Where Prospects Research You
1. **Reddit** -- The #1 place for unfiltered opinions. People believe Reddit more than many "official" review sites. Address it head-on in your confirmation content.
2. **Google** -- Searching "[Your Name] + Scam," "[Your Company] + Complaints," or "[Offer Name] + Reviews" is standard. If a high-ranking negative page exists, assume your prospect will see it. Proactively mention and reframe it.
3. **YouTube and Social Media** -- They hunt for in-depth video reviews, rants, or exposures. Show them you are aware of any chatter and are not avoiding it.

### Best Practices for Controlling the Narrative

**Acknowledge the Downside:** If you have a 10% fail rate, be transparent. "We see about 9 out of 10 people thrive, while 1 out of 10 doesn't due to [reasons]. Here's what we do in those cases..." This honesty disarms skepticism faster than anything else.

**Show Them Where to Look:** Encourage them to check your Reddit threads or real reviews. Tag on a mini-video: "Here's how to interpret what you'll see if you search us on Google or Reddit." Position yourself as a partner in their research, not a salesperson who wants to keep them in the dark.

**Highlight Third-Party Verification:** Strong Trustpilot or BBB reviews, known brand or influencer endorsements, low dispute rate in Stripe or PayPal (show the screenshot).

**Contextualize Guarantees:** Do not just say "we have a money-back guarantee." Explain how and why it works, and what you do if someone is unhappy. Address the real fear: "What if they offer a guarantee but go bankrupt?"

### The Outcome of Guiding Their Research
- **Higher Show Rates:** When all the "Is this legit?" jitters are calmed, they keep that appointment
- **Shorter Sales Cycles:** Less time spent on the phone telling them how real you are -- they already know
- **Better Deal Flow:** By the time they talk to you or your closer, they are 80% convinced. You just finish the final 20%

## The Main Confirmation Video

At the very top of your confirmation page, include a short primary video that does four things:

1. **Welcomes and Thanks them** (briefly)
2. **Explains you will help them do due diligence** and guide their research
3. **Addresses major risks or objections**, not shying away from worst-case scenarios
4. **Redirects them to further info on the page:** breakout videos, testimonials, etc.

**Pro Tip:** State plainly, "I'm here to help you do your research so that by the time we speak, you know exactly whether this is for you."

**Tone and Style:**
- Be transparent: People can smell hype and fluff from a mile away
- Own any negative or doubtful points: If something spooks prospects, say so and frame it properly

## Breakout Videos: The Digestible Q&A Modules

Immediately below your main confirmation video, embed short breakout videos (1-5 minutes each) that tackle the key questions your future buyers have. Label each video by its specific topic so they can jump to the sections that matter most.

### Why Breakout Videos Work

1. **Digestible and On-Demand:** Instead of one long video, create smaller clips addressing each concern ("How do I verify your reviews?" "Why does this cost $X?" etc.)
2. **Stronger Pre-Call Framing:** Eliminate repetitive Q&A that normally chews up half the sales call, so leads show up warmer and better prepared
3. **Boost Trust and Decrease Skepticism:** Prospects assume companies hide the worst parts. When you proactively talk about them, they trust you more

### How to Select Breakout Topics

1. **Sales Calls and Applications:** Talk to your closers. Ask what objections eat up time or kill deals.
2. **Address Downside and Skepticism:** "What if it's not right for me? What if the results aren't as promised?" Do not avoid these -- embrace them.
3. **Leverage Public Reviews and Scam Threads:** If you find comments criticizing your price or approach, record a mini-video clarifying that exact point.

### Crafting Each Mini-Video

1. **Title Clearly:** "What If It Doesn't Work?" "Why It Costs $XXXX," "Our Real Success Rate," etc.
2. **Open with the Question:** "A lot of people ask how safe this is..." Be direct.
3. **Answer with Evidence:** Show your dispute rate, a screenshot of your refund policy, or a quick story about how you handled a worst-case scenario.
4. **Invite Further Review:** Encourage them to watch more breakouts or bring it up on the call if they need deeper clarity.
5. **Keep It Short (Under 5 Minutes):** Micro-attention spans are real. Do not ramble.

### Placement on the Page

- Cluster them right under the main video in a grid or bullet list: "Common Questions" or "Important Topics Before Our Call"
- Order by Priority: If cost is the #1 query, make that breakout the first or most prominent
- Mention in Main Video: Remind them below your intro video that these Q&As exist

## Testimonials and Trust Assets

Modern prospects do not just want "highlight-reel" testimonials. They want the full picture:

1. **Home-Run Success Stories:** Show the big wins -- everyone loves a wow factor
2. **Average or Mid-Level Wins:** Far more realistic for many buyers, which increases perceived honesty
3. **Worst-Case or "Less Than Ideal":** If you have an example of someone who barely broke even or did not see insane gains but still found value, sharing it builds immense trust

### Third-Party Platforms
- **Reddit:** Encourage real customers to post about their experience. Reddit is where many prospects go for an unfiltered view.
- **Video Interviews:** Post quick, genuine conversations with customers who walk through their journey.

### Quoting Real Data
- **Dispute/Refund Rates:** If yours is low, call it out. "0.7% dispute rate after 5,000 transactions." That is tangible social proof.

Using a transparent confirmation page + short breakout videos + retargeting and email sequences = show rates climbing from 50-60% to 80%+.

When people know your price, your average/worst-case scenarios, and your guarantees, they arrive with fewer unanswered questions. Expect more "paid in full" commitments.

# PART 12: CONVICTION AND MORALE

## The Conviction Requirement

None of the scripts, techniques, or frameworks in this document will work if you do not have conviction about what you are selling. If you do not genuinely believe that what you are trying to talk to leads about will actually help them, and you do not deep down actually want to do a deal with that lead, there will be no deal.

You have to actually want their business and mean it. It is transparent to a lead when you do not give a fuck about them versus when somebody does care about getting their business.

## Sales is a Game of Interest

You as the salesperson have to keep YOUR interest at the highest level in getting a deal done in order to pull your lead's interest up to your level. Poor salespeople give up early, dropping down to their lead's level of low interest, and therefore no deal gets done.

When salespeople give up on a deal, it comes in the form of these excuses:
- "Was a bad lead" (lead simply ghosted them)
- "Couldn't get a hold of them" (lead simply ghosted them)
- "Person scheduled with me and never showed up" (lead simply ghosted them)
- "I got them to text back but they've been ghosting me" (lead simply ghosted them)

The pattern: the lead ghosts the rep, and the rep gives up rather than pivoting how they are communicating.

# PART 13: PRE-CALL SALES MASTERY (SMO)

> Source: Evan Seech, Sell More Online (SMO). Complete pre-call framework for increasing show rates, close rates, and accelerating cash collection cycles through proper sales process optimization before the call ever happens.
> File: `directives/incoming/copy-of-pre-call-sales-mastery.md`

## Core Philosophy

The majority of the heavy lifting of a sale comes on the back end of the ad system -- what happens AFTER somebody books in. Salespeople are one of the biggest assets for warming prospects up properly before a call, educating them, pre-framing them, and getting them to show up ready to buy.

Even with a great VSL, backend email flows, content remarketing, and confirmation page -- some prospects still will not consume any of that. They will ONLY interact with the salesperson. That is why the actions a salesperson takes BEFORE a sales call are so crucial.

**The objective:** We are trying to AVOID prospects showing up to sales calls looking for education and information. That is what kills a sale. That is what causes long sales cycles. That is what causes aggravation with salespeople.

## Interest Curve Management (SMO Framework)

When somebody books a call, fills out an application, or buys a low-ticket product -- that is their PEAK interest point. That is when they are the MOST interested. That interest fades rapidly over time and will go to zero if you do not upkeep it during the sales process.

**Without active nurture (interest decay):**
```
Interest ^
         |  * (peak at booking)
         |   \
         |    \
         |     \___________  (flatline / zero interest)
         |
         +-------------------------> Time
```
Very little re-spikes in interest because there is very little consumption of pre-call material, education, and information.

**With active manual outreach + asset deployment (interest maintained/increased):**
```
Interest ^
         |  *     *   *  *  * (re-spikes from manual outreach)
         |   \   / \ / \/ \/
         |    \ /   *   *  *  (sustained high interest into call)
         |     *
         +-------------------------> Time
```
By establishing a connection with a human rep and disbursing assets at a steady rate, you can actually INCREASE the interest and intent as time goes on.

**The entire job of a salesperson before the call:** Increase interest and intent by sending custom tailored content that educates, informs, pre-frames, serves the prospect, and gives value. You keep yourself, your products, your services, your solutions, and the sales call top of mind.

## Speed to Lead Requirements

Speed to lead is insanely crucial. Ideally you have a **5-minute or less speed to lead** (the faster the better -- get hungry). You want to hit the prospect as close to their peak interest point as possible -- the moment they opted in, purchased a product, or booked a call.

**The highest probability of response is text.** Everyone sees text. Keep that in mind.

## Blue Text Messaging (iMessage via Sendblue/Linq)

Blue texts (iMessage) instead of green texts (SMS) typically get a MUCH higher response rate.

**Why blue texts outperform green texts:**
- Blue iMessage bubbles signal "real person texting from an iPhone" -- not a business bot
- Green SMS bubbles trigger the same mental filter as automated marketing messages
- iMessage enables read receipts, typing indicators, and richer media -- all of which increase perceived human connection
- Prospects subconsciously trust blue texts more because they associate them with personal communication, not marketing

**Tools:** Sendblue, Linq App (SMO client -- reach out for discount), or just use your iPhone directly.

**Key principle:** The entire point is that manual outreach from a real person on a real phone number produces dramatically higher response rates than automated messages from business numbers. Blue texts are the tactical implementation of this principle.

## The "5-Minute Trick" (Pre-Text Double Dial Method)

Text a new prospect once they opt in or book a call with something like:

```
"Hey NAME, Evan here from COMPANY NAME. I saw you wanted some more
information about OFFER. I'm just wrapping up a call right now and will
shoot you a call in 5 minutes or so. Chat soon."
```

**Why this works:**
1. If you send that text BEFORE you dial, sometimes the recipient will see "MAYBE: [NAME]" on their iPhone caller ID, which lowers the likelihood of the caller ID saying "SPAM LIKELY" when you dial them
2. It comes off as very human-like -- a real person wrapping up another call, not a bot auto-dialing
3. It primes them to expect and answer your call within minutes
4. It creates a micro-commitment: they read your text, they know your name, they are mentally prepared for the ring

**Execution:** Send the text, wait 3-5 minutes, then double-dial. The combination of the text priming + double-dial to bypass Do Not Disturb = maximum connection rate.

## Manual vs. Automated Outreach Philosophy

Content remarketing, breakout videos on the confirmation page, and automated emails are spray-and-pray tactics. They do have a good lift in show rates and pre-framing, but the **show rate drivers really come from salespeople consistently doing these things, giving a shit about every lead, and making very custom tailored follow-up per prospect.**

**Spray-and-pray (marketing side):**
- 6-20 breakout videos on the confirmation page
- 3-6 emails per day that are value-dense
- 40-60 content remarketing videos in-feed
- Effective, but you have no idea what that specific lead is consuming or needs to consume

**Sniper approach (manual outreach from salespeople):**
- Collect info based on the conversation with the prospect
- Judge where they are on the interest spectrum
- Send information organized categorically based on what THEY need to raise their interest levels
- This is hyper-personalized and dramatically more effective

**The rule:** You cannot lean only on automations. Manual outreach has the highest visibility, highest trust building, and is consumed the most.

## Hyper-Personalization Requirements (No Cookie-Cutter Follow-Ups)

Every follow-up must look like a human wrote it, not a bot. Most follow-up is boring, templated, and gets ignored.

**Requirements for personalized outreach:**
1. Reference something specific from their application questions
2. Reference something you noticed about them online (website, social profile)
3. Address them by name naturally (not in a mail-merge-obvious way)
4. Tailor asset recommendations to their specific situation, goals, and desires
5. Vary the format: text, selfie video, voice note, Loom, social DM
6. Vary the timing: do not send at the same time every day

**What NOT to do (cookie-cutter examples):**
```
BAD: "Hey NAME! Saw you booked in a call. What are some things you are
struggling with now in your business and preventing you from scaling?"
```
- Does not sound human
- Not custom
- Not specific
- Easily ignored

**What TO do (personalized examples):**
```
GOOD: "Hey NAME, just saw you booked in a call with us. I have 2 things
I can send to you based upon your application questions that I think would
help. Here they are: I have X and Y. Let me know which one you'd like and
if you'd want it sent here or via email."
```

```
GOOD: "Hey NAME, just confirming your reservation -- how many people are
going to be in your party tonight?"
```
(The second version elicits a response. The first can be easily ignored.)

**Engagement bait framing:** Which question is a better conversation starter via text? The one that requires a response to answer, not the one that can be passively acknowledged and ignored.

## Using Application Questions As Ammunition

Application questions exist for 2 reasons:
1. **To increase lead quality** by adding friction to filter out bad leads
2. **To give the salesperson ammo** for follow-up and to know what direction to steer the conversation initially

Use them in every communication with prospects. Reference specific answers. Build your outreach around what they told you. This is the most underutilized source of personalization data.

## Selfie Video Best Practices for Follow-Up (SMO)

Film a 30-60 second selfie video or Loom addressing the lead by name and referencing something specific they wrote in their application or something you noticed about them online.

### Selfie Video Script Example

```
"Hey John -- saw you booked in with us for tomorrow at 3PM. I actually
read your app and noticed you mentioned your team's burned out from
outbound. I have a cool resource I'll show you on the call that fixes
that. Anyway, just wanted to say I'm pumped to meet and I have a few
resources I can send your way that I think will be super useful to your
specific situation... I have X, Y, and Z. Which one do you want and where
should I send them? Here or email?"
```

**Why this works:**
- You have humanized the process
- They know your face, your voice, and your intention
- You are no longer another stranger
- Show rates skyrocket with this one addition

**Pro tip:** If you have a founder or public figure leading the brand, have THEM send these. Response rates jump even more.

**Group chat handoff variation:** If you have a setter + closer, create a group chat with the prospect, closer, and setter. Do the handoff with a voice note or selfie video. Can also add the CEO/owner (especially if they are a well-known influencer) and send a selfie video or voice note.

## The Loom Video Tactic (Owner-to-Prospect)

If you have a closer who is different from the founder or public face (especially if the founder is well-known), use this nightly process:

**Step 1:** Populate tomorrow's calls into a spreadsheet -- Name, phone, website (if applicable), company name (if applicable), time of call, and application questions.

**Step 2:** Send the spreadsheet to the owner.

**Step 3:** The owner records a Loom video personalized to each lead (60 seconds to 2 minutes):

```
"Hey NAME! Saw you're booked in with CLOSER NAME tomorrow at TIME.
CLOSER NAME is awesome -- she's been with me for X years (or months) and
is incredible at [something]. Anyway, I see [something you noticed that
is personal to them based on their application or what you see on their
website/social profile]. Talk about how you can help them briefly. Anyway,
I'm excited for you to chat with CLOSER NAME and if you need anything
please reach out to them or me! Hope to see you on the other side."
```

**Step 4:** The closer sends this to the prospect in the morning via text:
```
"Hey, OWNER NAME has a message for you before the call today. Let me know
if there's anything me or them can answer or get you before the call later."
```

**Results:** Good pre-call response rates on the day of the call and a measurable lift in show rates.

## Creating Real Urgency (Not Fluff)

Establish REAL urgency in the prospect's mind -- not manufactured scarcity or fake deadlines. Real, tangible, logical reasons why somebody should move forward NOW.

**The method:**
1. Sit down and think about your product or service
2. Identify what things can inspire real urgency and action
3. If you can use third-party data to strengthen your case, do so
4. Use logic, not fluff, to paint the picture of what happens if they do not act now

## Setter vs. Closer Role Delineation (SMO)

### The Setter's Actual Job

The perceived job of a setter is to qualify and schedule. But it is also to nurture and get them to show up to the call pre-framed.

**What setters should actually do:**
1. Create prioritization of the call in the lead's mind
2. Send tailored and custom communication (sales assets, trust assets)
3. Collect information from the lead that can be used in the sales process to aid in closing
4. Build trust and relay information
5. Input lead information into the CRM for the closer to leverage

**What setters should NOT do:**
- Aggressively pre-qualify leads just to get "bad ones" off the closer's calendar
- Treat their role as just getting rid of trash leads
- Apply comparison bias (comparing leads' interest levels and shortchanging lower-interest leads)
- Be short with or not invest effort into financially qualified leads who are simply at a lower interest level

**The frame problem:** Most people in the internet marketing community think setters are just there to aggressively pre-qualify and remove bad leads. When you have that frame, it shines through to the prospect and starts the experience off improperly.

**The correct frame:** If it is a totally dud lead, of course remove them. But if not, the goal is to collect information, build trust, relay information, and input data that aids in closing.

### Setter Conversation Analysis at Scale

As a business owner, marketer, or sales manager, run setter calls through an LLM to assess:
- What questions are being asked
- How closely setters are following the script
- On a scale of 0 to 100, are conversations wildly selfish to the organization or are they less direct, selfless, curious, and friendly

### Aligning Setter Payments and Incentives

Setter incentive ideas to align with nurturing lower-interest but qualified leads:
- **Speed bonus:** Pay a bonus based on when a lead closes within a tight time frame (ideal sales cycle). Pay less if the sales cycle is longer. This pushes setters to get lower-interest leads the information they need FAST.
- **Clear DQ criteria:** Definitively define -- black and white -- what a "bad lead" is that should be hard disqualified. Use as many quantitative metrics as possible.
- **Mission reinforcement:** Remind the team of the substantial impact they make on prospects' lives. Every time a setter cuts corners, it is another person left behind because someone did not take the time to determine if they were truly disqualified or just at a lower interest level.

### Making It Easy for Setters to Nurture

Humans are inherently energy conservers. Setters and closers will naturally bias toward spending more time on warmer leads because they are incentivized on closed deals and warmer leads are easier.

**Solutions:**
1. Make it EASY for them to send material by having organized asset libraries at their disposal
2. Set up a dropdown in the CRM showing reasons the prospect has a lower interest level
3. Those reasons get added to the contact record and fire off contextual marketing automations
4. Combine CRM automations with manual outreach for maximum interest-raising

### CRM-Triggered Contextual Automations

Set up a dropdown in the CRM that captures why a prospect has lower interest:
- These reasons get added to the contact record
- Contextual marketing automations fire off tailored communication to the individual
- Automations are based on what the prospect needs to raise their interest level
- Do this IN CONJUNCTION with setters/closers manually sending material
- This dramatically increases the probability of raising interest and intent before the call, lowering the probability the prospect shows up for an education-based call

### The Setter Asset Deploy Script

When a prospect has questions about the business model, offer, or process:

```
"Hey NAME, yeah I really appreciate you asking that question about the
business model. I can do two things. First, I can explain it to you right
now. But second of all, generally what people like you need in order to
make this a priority and actually take action is just information. I have
a handful of things I can send you: I have written SOPs with a bunch of
XYZ, I have a bunch of videos (30 sec to 3 min), I even have a longer
form podcast if you prefer that format, and I also have some case studies
and testimonials that articulate how our business model works and what
you'll learn. Do any of those sound like a good preference to you?"
```

**The goal from here:** Send the prospect custom tailored information based on what format they prefer and what questions they have.

## Pre-Call Asset Deployment Strategy (SMO)

Consumption of information is far greater and more effective when a salesperson establishes a connection with a lead and builds a relationship pre-call. This is because you can now send pre-call assets that MATCH the prospect's real-time interests, situation, goals, and desires.

**Asset categories to organize and have ready:**
- Written SOPs and breakdowns
- Short videos (30 seconds to 3 minutes)
- Long-form podcasts or deep-dive content
- Case studies and testimonials
- Trust assets (proof compilations, P&Ls, third-party verification)
- FAQ documents
- System diagrams and visual breakdowns
- Spreadsheet comparisons (with vs. without your solution)

**Example trust asset benchmark:** A real estate syndication client who walks around the subject property at all times of day, acts like a tenant, takes tours in disguise, goes inside surrounding buildings, walks to popular destinations, checks for homeless people, records at 5AM and 9PM -- all documented in an insanely detailed resource. That is the level of commitment that builds massive trust.

**Organize assets by interest level and objection type** so setters can quickly deploy the right content for each prospect's specific situation.

## The "Don't Just Sell the Call" Principle

Too many reps treat the call like it is the prize. It is not. The outcome is the prize. If someone would rather close the deal over text -- let them.

Text-based sales are exploding. Busy buyers especially hate hopping on calls unless they feel it is already worth their time.

**Script:**
```
"Hey NAME, if you prefer to chat here and work through things first,
totally fine. I can send you a full breakdown on how this works, and if
it feels aligned, we can hop on a call after that."
```

**Why this works:** They feel respected and in control. Ironically, once they feel that, they are way more likely to take the call.

## What Is "Too Much" Follow-Up?

Look at it relative to how many people are getting "annoyed" versus actually consuming and buying. If you get overwhelming feedback that people are not going to buy because of the amount of outreach (not 1 or 2 people a week -- a high volume relative to new leads), then dial back.

**The rule:** Until you see a dip in show rate as a result of volume AND a dip in close rate and cash collected, continue to increase follow-up volume.

## Struggling to Get a Hold of Someone? (SMO Checklist)

Before giving up on a lead, verify:

- [ ] Are your outreach times intentionally varied? Try different times of day.
- [ ] Does your outreach look human and come from the frame of eliciting a response?
- [ ] Have you tried selfie videos?
- [ ] Have you tried calls (double dial)?
- [ ] Have you tried texts?
- [ ] Have you sent trust assets?
- [ ] Have you tried voice notes?
- [ ] Have you emailed manually (not just automated)?
- [ ] Have you tried social media outreach (Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook)?
- [ ] Are you varying the method, timing, AND tone of outreach?
- [ ] Are you being selfless in your messaging?

## Best Practices When Calling Leads (SMO)

Most people do not answer unknown numbers anymore. Very low trust.

**Tactical calling protocol:**
1. **Double dial** to get past Do Not Disturb mode
2. Call at different times of day -- 2x per day double dial, spread out
3. Consider the prospect's demographics and schedule (selling a biz-op to 9-to-5ers? Do not call between 9 and 5)
4. Two completely different times of day for each attempt
5. Wide availability for reps to be dialing AND for prospects to book in
6. Be varied when trying to communicate within probable windows each day before the call

## The 72-Hour Pre-Call Checklist (SMO)

### Day 1 (Booked Call Day):

**Immediately:**
- [ ] Selfie video ASAP (personalized, referencing application)
- [ ] Call (Double dial)
- [ ] Text (engagement bait or personalized message)

**6-8 hours later (if no response):**
- [ ] Text follow-up with trust asset based on application questions
- [ ] Call (Double dial)

### Day 2:

**Varied time (e.g., morning):**
- [ ] Selfie video follow-up about the asset you sent
- [ ] Call (Double dial)

**6-8 hours later (if no response):**
- [ ] Voice note
- [ ] Call (Double dial)

**1-2 hours later (if still no response):**
- [ ] Text

### Day 3 (Day of the Call):

**Varied time (e.g., morning):**
- [ ] Selfie video delivering a trust asset, pre-framing the call, sending owner Loom (if applicable)
- [ ] Call (Double dial)

**1 hour before call:**
- [ ] Text
- [ ] Text the call link
- [ ] Call (Double dial)

**15 minutes before call:**
- [ ] Closer selfie video at the desk looking "ready"
- [ ] Text the call link again

## SMO Engagement Bait Examples

### Example 1: Application-Based Asset Offer
```
"Hey NAME, just saw you booked in a call with us. I have 2 things I can
send to you based upon your application questions that I think would help.
Here they are: I have X and Y. Let me know which one you'd like and if
you'd want it sent here or via email."
```

### Example 2: CRM Compatibility Check (for SaaS/tech offers)
```
"Hey NAME, just saw you booked in. Just so I can see if we're compatible
with your workflows, what CRM are you using?"
```

### Example 3: Pre-Call Objection Surface
```
"Hey NAME, just wanted to reach out before the call tomorrow. Most people
have a very specific question that if left unanswered would hold them
back from showing up. And I just want to know, what's that question for
you? Because I have a TON of information that I can send you that's going
to be really helpful to you and help you determine if this is right for
you. Is there anything specific that I can answer before the call
tomorrow so I don't waste your time? I want to make sure we spend it well
tomorrow. What's that thing that's going to ensure you show up tomorrow?"
```

### Example 4: Anticipation + Specific Question
```
"Hey NAME, looking forward to our call coming up tomorrow at 3PM about
SOLVING PROBLEM. Is there any specific questions that need answered that
would prevent you from showing up?"
```

# PART 14: PRE-CALL EMAIL SEQUENCE STRATEGY

> Source: Evan Seech, Sell More Online (SMO). Pre-call email flow framework for maximum show rate lift within the booking-to-call window.
> Outline file: `directives/incoming/copy-of-pre-call-email-outline.md`

## Core Principle

Deploy a minimum of 2 emails per day within the booking-to-call window. These are not "reminder" emails -- they are value-dense, objection-handling, trust-building assets that move the prospect up the interest spectrum before the call happens.

**Start at:** 2-3 emails per day across a 3-4 day period.
**Scale up to:** Higher volume over time as long as you see strong open rates consistently, strong consumption, good feedback from leads, and increased show rates.

**Spacing:** Use basic marketing automation within your CRM. Space same-day emails 3-5 hours apart depending on volume.

## The 21-Email Pre-Call Sequence (SMO Framework)

| # | Email Type | Purpose | Key Content |
|---|-----------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Welcome | Confirm their action, set expectations for what is next | Re-send confirmation page or company research deck with breakout videos. Add personal touches (selfies). |
| 2 | FAQs | ATTACK the most common questions that PREVENT prospects from showing up | Be impactful, do not hold back. Answer the real questions in their mind. |
| 3 | Engagement Bait | Short question designed to elicit a reply | "What's the one question preventing you from showing up?" + offer to send specific materials. Must feel personal. |
| 4 | Why People Don't Show Up | Address every reason they have to not show up and combat each one | Proactively name and destroy every excuse. |
| 5 | Deeper Questions (Tactical) | Dive into more nuanced, tactical questions as follow-ups to Email 2 | Give real value here. Go deeper than surface-level FAQs. |
| 6 | Real Urgency | Give real, data-backed reasons why they NEED to act NOW | Use third-party data if available. No fluff. See "Creating Real Urgency" section. |
| 7 | Tactics and Strategy | Super value-dense breakdown of your system and differentiation | Visual diagrams of your system. Hyperlink to podcasts/videos. Include engagement bait offering format choices. |
| 8 | Handle Objections | Hit the big objections head-on: cost, time, skepticism | Be direct -- tell them you literally want to combat their objections. |
| 9 | Case Study(s) | Break down actual TACTICS of the case study, not just results | Use case study to answer questions, break limiting beliefs, break objections. Articulate, do not just dump. |
| 10 | Expectations | Paint a clear picture of what they can expect moving forward | Talk about both the downsides AND the upsides. |
| 11 | Reminder | Reinforce benefits and remind them why they booked | Bring them back to their "why." Why is this the solution they have been looking for? |
| 12 | Third Party Data | Pull in external data supporting your product/service effectiveness | Tie third-party data back to why you do what you do and why they should act now. |
| 13 | The Big Opportunity | Tie your offer to a macro trend or grand narrative | Position urgency around market timing, competitive advantage, or industry shifts. |
| 14 | Education | Share insights, tips, strategies, tactics related to your offer | Hyperlink YouTube videos or podcasts. Build trust and authority. |
| 15 | Comparison Email | Show how your offer stacks up against alternatives and competitors | Highlight why you are the obvious choice for the result they want. |
| 16 | Addressing Fears | Address fears or doubts about taking the next step | Head-on, direct, empathetic. |
| 17 | Future Pace | Help them visualize the transformation after saying yes | Make this SUPER detailed and articulate. They should be able to visualize it. |
| 18 | Personal Story | Share a relatable story that connects emotionally | Reinforce your message through narrative. |
| 19 | More FAQs | Break down additional FAQs | Catch the questions that Email 2 did not cover. |
| 20 | Sell The Call | Sell what will happen on the call itself | Make the call sound valuable, not just a pitch. |
| 21 | Personal Contact Details and Socials | Send contact details and socials for pre-call questions | Re-link confirmation page, key trust/sales assets. |

## Email Content Types and Their Jobs

| Content Type | Job | Example |
|-------------|-----|---------|
| Case studies | Build belief through proof | Tactical breakdown of how a specific client achieved results |
| Trust assets | Lower skepticism through transparency | P&L compilations, dispute rates, third-party verification |
| Objection handling | Remove barriers to showing up | Direct head-on combat of cost, time, skepticism objections |
| Urgency creation | Create real reasons to act NOW | Third-party data, market timing, competitive pressure |
| Education and value | Position as expert, build trust | System breakdowns, tactical tips, strategy overviews |
| Engagement bait | Elicit replies and open conversation | "What's your one question?" with offer to send specific materials |
| Future pacing | Make the outcome feel real and achievable | Detailed visualization of life after transformation |
| Social proof | Validate through others' experiences | Testimonials, community size, third-party reviews |

## Key Email Principles

1. **Volume management:** Start at 2-3/day, scale up as long as open rates, consumption, and show rates remain strong
2. **No fluff:** Every email must deliver real value or address a real concern
3. **Engagement bait emails must feel personal:** Not mass-marketing. Reference their situation.
4. **Urgency must be real:** Backed by logic, data, and market conditions -- not fake countdown timers
5. **Case studies must be articulated:** Break down the tactics, do not just dump results
6. **Expectations email must include downsides:** Honesty builds trust faster than hype
