## PART 1: FOUNDATIONS OF DIRECT RESPONSE VIDEO ADS

### 1.1 What Is Direct Response Advertising?

Direct response advertising compels a specific, immediate, measurable action: "Click here now," "Schedule a call," "Download this guide," or "Buy now." Everything in the ad -- script, visuals, structure, hook -- supports that singular next step. Unlike branding ads, every element is optimized for conversion.

**Why video over static images:**
- Moving visuals and human faces are harder to scroll past
- In 30-90 seconds you can convey urgency, credibility, and address objections far better than a single image
- Dynamic ad platforms (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube) give more cost-effective reach to video ads, especially short-form vertical

### 1.2 Trust the Algorithm -- Dynamic Creative Methodology

**Old Way:** Create a single ad combination (one video, one headline, one body copy) and let it run.

**Modern Way:** Supply multiple variations and turn on Dynamic Creative. The platform automatically combines elements and serves best-performing variants to each user.

**The 250-Combo Formula:**
- Up to 10 videos
- Up to 5 headlines
- Up to 5 body copy texts
- = 250 possible combinations the platform tests automatically

**Asset split:**
- Half your assets SHORT (30-second ad, minimal copy)
- Half your assets LONG (60-90 second ad, deeper reasons, short testimonials)
- Let the platform decide which length/format each user sees

### 1.3 The 3-Tier Audience Model

This is the most critical strategic concept. Based on Chet Holmes' "Stadium Selling":

| Tier | % of Market | Description | Messaging Approach | Cost to Convert |
|------|-------------|-------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **In-Market** | 3-4% | Already believe in the category/vehicle. Looking for the best provider. Already want what you sell. | Sell YOUR unique angle. Assume they're sold on the category. "Since you already [do X], here's [Y]." | Lowest. Shortest sales cycles. |
| **Needs Convinced** | ~30% | Aware they have the problem, know solutions exist, but not actively seeking. Need persuasion to act now. | Sell the opportunity first. Create urgency. "Why [category] is the right move right now." | Higher CPL. Higher no-show risk. More "why" content needed. |
| **Mass Market** | ~33% | Don't even know they have the problem. Need problem-awareness created first. | Create problem-awareness before selling the solution. Ultra-expensive to convert initially. | Highest. Longest sales cycles. |
| **Non-Buyers** | ~30% | Will never buy. Don't waste time or money. | Exclude. | N/A |

**Core Lesson:** Begin campaigns with in-market messaging. Master that 3-4% first. Only expand to Needs Convinced once in-market is saturated. Mass market requires strategies like The Tornado or The Harvester.

### 1.4 Circle Talk Framework

From the "circles, triangles, and squares" communication model:

- **Circle People** (visionaries, decision-makers): Want the big picture -- fast. Don't drown them in details.
- **Triangle People** (analytical): Want data and proof points.
- **Square People** (process-oriented): Want step-by-step methodology.

**For ads targeting in-market audiences, default to Circle Talk:**
> "You've tested the waters. You believe real estate builds millionaires. This new short-term rental method can add an extra 5-figure monthly cash flow without endless phone calls and property visits."

Broad enough to get them nodding immediately. Not drowning in tiny data points or disclaimers.

### 1.5 Qualifying and Disqualifying Language Patterns

Because in-market prospects are valuable, ads should do light gatekeeping:

**Qualifying examples:**
- "If you already own at least one property and want more monthly income -- this 2-minute video is for you."
- "This is for experienced [niche] professionals who want to scale past [milestone]."

**Disqualifying examples:**
- "This isn't for people who think [category] is too risky or unproven."
- "If you're not ready to invest at least [amount], this isn't for you."

**Result:** Attract the segment who truly resonates. Deter time-wasters and "needs convinced" skeptics. Trains the pixel to find better prospects.

### 1.6 Visual Environment Alignment

Your ad's visual environment should resonate with your audience's feed:
- **Luxury real estate:** Film in front of premium properties
- **Trading/investing:** Clean professional office or screens with charts
- **Fitness:** Gym or active setting
- **Professional services:** Well-lit office, business casual

Don't overdo it or fake it. Authentic beats forced "Lambo & Mansion" vibes.

## PART 2: THE UNIVERSAL AD STRUCTURE

### 2.1 The 3-Part Formula: Hook > Body > CTA

Every direct response video ad follows this structure:

1. **Hook** (first 3-5 seconds) -- Pattern interrupt, creates curiosity, decides if they watch or scroll
2. **Body / Reasons** (15-45 seconds) -- Emotional + logical justification for why your approach is better
3. **CTA** (5-10 seconds) -- Clear instruction with benefit statement and friction reducer

### 2.2 Hook Formulas (15+ Complete Formulas)

| # | Formula | Template | Example |
|---|---------|----------|---------|
| 1 | **Bold Claim** | "We helped [client] go from $X to $Y in Z days" | "We helped Sarah go from $8K to $47K/month in 90 days" |
| 2 | **Question** | "Are you still [painful activity]?" | "Are you still cold-calling 50 leads a day with nothing to show for it?" |
| 3 | **Pattern Interrupt** | "Stop scrolling if you [qualifier]" | "Stop scrolling if you run an agency doing under $30K/month" |
| 4 | **Statistic** | "[X]% of [audience] make this mistake" | "87% of agency owners make this one scaling mistake" |
| 5 | **Story** | "[Time] ago, [subject] was about to [disaster]..." | "6 months ago, our client was about to shut down his agency..." |
| 6 | **Contrarian** | "Everything your [authority] told you about [topic] is wrong" | "Everything your marketing guru told you about Facebook ads is wrong" |
| 7 | **Real-time Context** | "It's [current month/season], and if you haven't [action]..." | "It's Q4, and if you haven't locked in your ad strategy..." |
| 8 | **Circumstance** | "If you're a [specific person] who [specific situation]..." | "If you're a coach doing $10K months who wants to hit $50K..." |
| 9 | **"You Already Know" Opener** | "You already know [belief they hold], but have you considered [your angle]?" | "You already know real estate builds wealth, but have you considered short-term rentals?" |
| 10 | **"You're Doing This, But..." Opener** | "You've already tried [mainstream method], but [problem]. There's a simpler approach..." | "You've already tried day trading, but it's draining you. There's a simpler approach..." |
| 11 | **Objection-First** | "You think [biggest objection]? Let me prove you wrong." | "You think you need massive capital for real estate? Let me prove you wrong." |
| 12 | **Case Study** | "How did [name] go from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]?" | "How did Tom go from broke to $15K/month in just 3 months?" |
| 13 | **Q&A Style** | "A lot of people ask me, '[common question].' Let's answer that." | "A lot of people ask me, 'Aren't fix-and-flips riskier now?' Let's answer that." |
| 14 | **Pain-Point Direct** | "Still [frustrating activity]? Let me show you why that might be a disaster." | "Still riding meme coins? Let me show you why that might be a disaster." |
| 15 | **Aspirational** | "Ready to [desired outcome] [with qualifier]?" | "Ready to lock in 20 qualified appointments next week, guaranteed?" |

### 2.3 Body Copy Structures

**Structure 1: Problem > Agitation > Solution (PAS)**
- State the problem they have
- Agitate it (make it feel urgent, painful, costly)
- Present your solution as the relief

**Structure 2: Story > Lesson > Offer**
- Tell a client/personal story (15-20 seconds)
- Extract the key insight or lesson
- Bridge naturally to the offer

**Structure 3: Myth > Truth > Proof**
- State the common belief or industry myth
- Reveal the actual truth (contrarian angle)
- Prove it with evidence, data, or case study

**Structure 4: Before > After > Bridge**
- Paint the "before" picture (where they are now)
- Paint the "after" picture (where they want to be)
- Bridge with your offer as the path between them

### 2.4 Objection Handling by Ad Length

| Ad Length | Objections to Address | How |
|-----------|----------------------|-----|
| **Short (15-30 sec)** | 1 big objection only | Weave into body. Focus on biggest friction (time, money, or trust). |
| **Medium (30-60 sec)** | 1-2 objections | Handle primary objection by second 15-20, secondary by second 30. |
| **Long (60-90 sec)** | 2-3 objections | Handle time + capital + trust. Keep each segment to ~10 seconds. |

**Always finish by pivoting back to CTA. Never end on an objection answer.**

### 2.5 CTA Formulas by Framework

**Universal CTA Formula:**
> "[Action verb] the [button/link] below to [what happens next] so you can [desired outcome]. [Friction reducer]."

**Examples by format:**

| Framework | CTA Example |
|-----------|------------|
| **Talking Head** | "Click the link below to book your free strategy call so you can finally scale past $50K/month. Takes 30 seconds. No obligation." |
| **Case Study** | "Watch the same method Tom used. Click below to see the full breakdown." |
| **Demo/Screencast** | "Ready to replicate this? Click to see the detailed version." |
| **Objection-First** | "Interested? Click now to see the details or schedule a call." |
| **Q&A Style** | "Got more questions? I answer them all in my free mini-training -- click below." |
| **Interview** | Interviewer: "So where can folks learn more?" You: "Just click the link below." |
| **Skit** | "If you feel like [character], watch how we solved it in minutes. Link below." |

### 2.6 The 8 Creative Frameworks

Rotate these frameworks to prevent fatigue and reach different audience segments:

#### Framework 1: Talking Head + B-Roll
- Speak directly to camera in single shot
- Overlay relevant B-roll midway (results, sites, charts)
- Return to talking head for CTA
- **Why it works:** Personal connection + visual variety

#### Framework 2: Case Study Snapshot
- **Hook:** "How did [name] go from X to Y in Z months?"
- **Body:** Quick bullet or short footage of client praising method, before vs. after
- **CTA:** "Watch the same method [name] used. Click below."
- **Keep to 15-30 seconds.** Don't let it become a full testimonial.

#### Framework 3: Demo / Screencast
- **Hook:** "3 steps I took on my laptop to earn an extra $3K a month"
- **Body:** Show screen doing the key steps
- **CTA:** "Ready to replicate this? Click to see the detailed version."
- **Perfect for tech, step-by-step, or SaaS offers.**

#### Framework 4: Objection-First
- Flip the script: Start with the biggest objection as the hook
- **Hook:** "You think short-term rentals require massive time or capital? Let me prove you wrong."
- **Body:** Address that single objection in 15-20 seconds with counter-evidence
- **CTA:** Standard

#### Framework 5: Q&A Style
- **Hook:** "A lot of people ask me, '[common question].' Let's answer that."
- **Body:** Short, direct reply with anecdote or success snippet
- **CTA:** "Got more questions? I answer them in my free mini-training."
- Feels organic. Great for warm audiences.

#### Framework 6: 2-Person Interview / Podium
- Interviewer lobs questions, you answer with DR points
- Breaks the "one-person monologue" pattern
- Keep to 30-60 seconds total. Micro-bites.

#### Framework 7: Skit / Scenario
- Quick role-play or comedic scenario underscoring your pitch
- **Hook:** "Meet Sarah. She's done with chaotic day-trading."
- Show Sarah's frustration, then you appear with solution
- **Warning:** Can flop if too cheesy or long-winded. Keep it tight.

#### Framework 8: Blend (Dynamic Creative Multi-Framework)
- Upload a talking-head angle, a Q&A snippet, AND a case-study snippet
- Let the platform test which resonates best with each viewer
- Effectively uses multiple frameworks simultaneously

### Strategy 1: Hammer Them

**Best for:** Booked-call funnels, high-ticket offers, pre-call nurture
**Budget:** $100-$200/day CBO minimum; scale based on frequency targets
**Content needed:** 30-50 short-form videos + 15-30 long-form videos
**Core idea:** Hit each lead with 15-20+ content exposures within 72 hours of booking a call. Turn salespeople into "cashiers."

#### The 4 Content Pillars

| Pillar | Purpose | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Questions** | Answer foundational questions buyers need answered | "How does [service] work?" "What makes this unique vs alternatives?" "Who is this best for?" |
| **2nd-Layer Questions** | Address follow-up questions that arise from initial answers | "You said the water is better -- what do you mean?" "How long until results?" "What level of support?" |
| **Objections** | Proactively handle resistance before the sales call | "Isn't this expensive?" "I've tried this before." "I don't have time." "How do I know this works?" |
| **Expectations** | Set realistic timelines and requirements | "Here's what month 1 looks like..." "The commitment required is..." "Here's the full arc of the journey." |

#### Psychology Behind It
1. **Familiarity Bias:** Repeated exposure builds preference and trust
2. **Mere-Exposure Effect:** People develop preference for things simply because they're familiar
3. **Selling the Next Step:** Don't sell the end result -- sell showing up to the call
4. **Proactive Objection Handling:** Preempt roadblocks so salespeople close, not educate

#### Complete Campaign Setup (6 Steps)

**Step 1: Build Campaign Foundation**
- Campaign objective: Engagement
- Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO): ON
- Daily budget: $100-$200/day minimum (adjust until frequency of 15-20+ in 72 hours is achieved)
- Name: "Hammer Them - [Offer/Audience]"

**Step 2: Pinpoint Target Audience**
- Ad set level: Optimize for Video Views > ThruPlay
- Target: People who booked a call in last 72 hours, OR DMs talking to setters, OR showed up but didn't buy
- Minimum audience: 100 people
- DISABLE "Expand Audience" -- laser-focused targeting only
- Exclude: Audience Network placement (it's trash)

**Step 3: Choose Content**
- 1 piece of content per ad set
- Post content organically to IG page first, cross-post to FB
- Select "Use Existing Post" at ad level
- **Do NOT use copyrighted music** -- won't be usable for paid ads

**Step 4: Set Up Exclusion Audiences**
- Create 3-second video view Custom Audience for EACH video
- Name: "VID 1 [Name] | 3 Sec View" (365-day window)
- Each ad set excludes its own 3-second view audience
- This ensures content rotation -- once someone watches 3 sec, they move to the next piece

**Step 5: Duplicate and Repeat**
- Duplicate ad set for each content piece
- Rename and swap video at ad level
- Add exclusion audience for new video in new ad set
- Target: 30-50 unique ad sets within the campaign

**Step 6: Assess and Adapt**
- Monitor Frequency and Reach columns
- Target: 15-20+ frequency within 72 hours
- If frequency too low: increase budget or tighten audience
- If frequency too high on single piece: hook sucks, people aren't watching past 3 seconds -- change the hook

**Final campaign structure:** CBO campaign, $100-200/day, 25-50 individual ad sets, each with 1 unique content piece and 1 matching 3-second exclusion audience.

#### Long-Form Addition to Hammer Them
Run a SEPARATE campaign from short-form, with same structure:
- Campaign objective: Engagement
- ABO recommended (ensures each video gets budget; CBO might over-optimize to 1-2 favorites)
- One long-form video per ad set (10 min to 2 hours)
- Same audience targeting (booked call, etc.)
- Same 3-second exclusion audiences
- Start $5-10/day per ad set
- Target library: 15-30 long-form pieces

**Short Form = Awareness** (rapid familiarity, 15-20 exposures in 72 hrs)
**Long Form = Safety** (deep expertise demonstration, kills buyer skepticism)
**Sales Team = Personalized Touch** (reference content: "Check out that deeper training on [topic]")

#### 6 Emails Per Day Protocol
Send 6 targeted emails/day in the window between call booking and the call:

**Content categories:** Same 4 pillars (Questions, 2nd-Layer Questions, Objections, Expectations)

**Crafting principles:**
- Be educational and informative, no fluff
- Engaging subject lines that pique curiosity
- Include social proof where appropriate
- Gentle CTAs reminding about upcoming call
- 3-4 emails can be reminder/logistical; rest should be substantive framing content

**Alignment with ads:** Consistent messaging between emails and retargeting ads. Repurpose content across channels.

**Target metrics:**
- Open rate: 40-50% minimum
- Track: click-through rates, reply rates, show rates, conversion rates
- Sales team feedback is the LEADING KPI -- ask if leads are warmer

### Strategy 2: Venus Fly Trap 2.0

**Best for:** Brand building + direct response hybrid, bigger budgets, long-term audience building, high-ticket offers ($2,000+)
**Budget:** $15K-$60K+/month
**Content needed:** 15 long-form videos (5 per step, each 10-120 minutes)
**Core idea:** 3-step content funnel with progressive 25% watch thresholds, followed by direct response to the most indoctrinated audience

#### How the 2.0 Works

The VFT 2.0 is a three-step content funnel. Each step contains 5 long-form videos. Watch 25% of any one video in a step to advance. After passing through all 3 steps, leads see direct response ads.

```
Step 1 (Cold) --> [25% watch] --> Step 2 --> [25% watch] --> Step 3 --> [25% watch] --> Step 4 (Direct Response)
```

#### Step-by-Step Setup

**Step 1: Campaign #1 -- Cold Traffic Entry**
- Campaign name: "Campaign Step 1 -- Venus Fly Trap 2.0"
- Objective: Engagement > Video Views (maximize through plays)
- 5 ad sets, each with 1 unique long-form video
- Same cold/interest-based/lookalike targeting across all ad sets
- Exclusion: 10-second viewers of that same video (prevents re-showing to non-engagers)
- Create custom audiences: 25% View (365 days) + 10-second View (365 days) per video
- Budget: Largest chunk (~50% of total). Example: $30,000 of $60,000 total.

**Step 2: Campaign #2 -- Retarget Step 1 Completers**
- Campaign name: "Campaign Step 2 -- Venus Fly Trap 2.0"
- Audience: Include all five 25% view audiences from Step 1
- Exclude: 10-second viewers of each Step 2 video
- 5 new long-form videos (#6-#10), one per ad set
- Same optimization: Engagement > Video Views
- Budget: ~25% of total. Example: $15,000.

**Step 3: Campaign #3 -- Final Indoctrination**
- Campaign name: "Campaign Step 3 -- Venus Fly Trap 2.0"
- Audience: Include all five 25% view audiences from Step 2
- 5 new long-form videos (#11-#15)
- Budget: ~17% of total. Example: $10,000.

**Step 4: Direct Response Campaign**
- Audience: 25% viewers from any Step 3 video
- Optimization: Conversions (leads, calls, purchases, registrations)
- Use dynamic creative: Short punchy 15-30 sec videos + static images + multiple headlines + multiple body copy
- Budget: ~8% of total. Example: $5,000.
- These leads are HOT -- three separate long-form content pieces consumed

#### Budget Split Example ($60K/month)

| Step | Budget | Audience | Purpose |
|------|--------|----------|---------|
| Step 1 | $30,000 | Cold (biggest pool) | Fill the funnel |
| Step 2 | $15,000 | Step 1 completers | Deepen buy-in |
| Step 3 | $10,000 | Step 2 completers | Final indoctrination |
| Step 4 | $5,000 | Step 3 completers (red hot) | Convert |

#### Key Rules and Metrics
- **25% watch threshold** = minimum engagement to advance between steps
- **10-second exclusion** for non-engagers within each ad set
- **Every 45 seconds** in long-form content, deliver a "banger" insight -- "aha!" moment
- **KPI: Cost per 25% view should be $0.25-$0.75**
- Cost per fully indoctrinated lead (3 videos at 25%): **$2.50-$10.00**
- Reference: $1.07 for someone to watch 100% of a 1hr 55min video
- **Burn window:** Expect a front-loaded spend period (days to weeks) before conversions appear. This is normal. Don't panic on Day 3.
- Monitor: Cost per 25% view, drop-off rates per step, time-to-migrate, direct response conversions, lead quality

#### Content Quality Requirements
- 15 total long-form videos (5 per step)
- Every 45 seconds: deliver a genuine "aha!" moment
- Cut all filler: "Hey guys," "Please like & subscribe," casual side-chatter
- Open strong: First 5-10 seconds call out the big problem or outcome
- No CTAs in Steps 1-3 content -- pure value/education

#### Testing and Iteration
- Swap underperforming videos (2-3x higher cost per 25% view than others): yank and replace
- Tweak one variable at a time
- Test varied lengths within each step
- Rotate creatives when fatigue sets in
- Scale by 20-30% increments every few days
- If Step 2 is stalling, it might be a Step 1 problem

#### Post-Conversion: Deploy Hammer Them
Once someone converts at Step 4, deploy the Hammer Them strategy to keep them warm and drive show rates.

### Strategy 3: The Tornado

**Best for:** Leveraging existing organic content, warming audiences cheaply, supplementing other strategies
**Budget:** Test budgets: $10K-$30K over 30 days. Scaled: $300K+/month for large brands.
**Content needed:** 50-100+ organic reels/TikToks (minimum 50; up to 383+ for large brands)
**Core idea:** Blast organic content as paid ads to both cold and warm audiences simultaneously, then run DR to the warm audience built by the Tornado

#### The Process

**Step 1: Content Selection**
- Pull from Instagram Reels (cross-posted to FB preferred for dual placements)
- Select EVERY non-holiday reel going as far back as content is quality
- Separate short-form (60-90 sec) and long-form into different ad sets
- Minimum: 50-100 unique pieces of content

**Step 2: Build Warm Audiences**
Create these custom audiences (max date ranges):
- Instagram interactions (365 days, all options)
- Facebook page interactions (365 days, all options)
- Website pixel activity (180 days)
- Email list uploads (lifetime)
- Lead form interactions (90 days)

**Step 3: Cold Content Campaign**
- Campaign objective: Engagement > Post Engagement
- Ad Set Budget Optimization (ABO)
- Target: Same cold audience stack as DR campaigns
- EXCLUDE warm audiences from cold ad sets
- Placements: IG Reels, Feed, Stories, Explore (if cross-posted: add FB placements; always exclude Audience Network)
- 50 ads max per ad set. If 383 pieces of content: 8 ad sets (7 x 50 + 1 x 33)
- Duplicate campaign, change objective to ThruPlay to widen reach

**Step 4: Warm Content Campaign (Two Paths)**

**Path 1 (Fast -- 5 minutes):**
- Duplicate cold campaign ad sets
- Remove cold targeting, add warm audience targeting
- Uncheck "Expand beyond custom audiences"
- Pro: Fast setup. Con: Same content may repeat to same person.

**Path 2 (Precise -- 1-4 hours):**
- Create 3-second video view audience for EACH video
- One video per ad set
- Each ad set excludes its own 3-second view audience
- Guarantees content rotation -- once viewed, person sees next piece
- Pro: No repeat content. Con: Tedious for large libraries.

**Step 5: Direct Response Campaigns**
- Campaign objective: Leads or Sales (ABO)
- Ad Set 1: Cold audience (same targeting as cold content campaign)
- Ad Set 2: Warm audience (same targeting as warm content campaign)
- Split test cold vs warm to measure the lift
- Exclude warm from cold ad set

#### Ideal Frequency
- Week over week frequency target: **3-12 pieces per person per week**
- Ad account limits: 250 active ads max if never spent $100K+/month historically

#### Expected Results (Documented Outcomes)
- **50% reduction in CPL** for warm audience vs. cold
- **17% lift in email open rates**
- **4.5% lift in email click-through rates**
- **~28% increase in webinar show rates**
- **~2.8% increase in webinar conversion rates**
- Warmer leads, shorter sales cycles, elevated follower growth and DMs
- Results are indirect -- no direct analytic attribution to content campaign, but the lift is unmistakable

#### Cost Benchmarks
- Short-form (60-90 sec): ~$0.01-$0.10 per 100% playthrough
- Long-form (1-2 hours): ~$1.05 per 95-100% view
- Cost per engagement: ~$0.01

### Strategy 4: The Forester

**Best for:** Personal brands, content creators, product funnels at lower budgets
**Budget:** $5-$30/day per ad set (scales well to thousands/day)
**Content needed:** 5-12 attention-grabbing videos per cycle bin (no pitch, no CTA in Bin 1)
**Core idea:** 5-step spider web process to build warm audiences at $0.01/engagement, then hit them with DR

#### The 5-Step Spider Web Process

**Step 1: Content Cycle Bin 1 (Cold > Warm)**
- Campaign: Engagement objective, ABO
- 5-12 ad sets, each with 1 video
- Target: Cold audience (lookalike stack of past customers, past qualified leads, email list; OR interest-based cold targeting)
- Budget: $5-$30/day per ad set
- Content: Attention-grabbing, no pitches, no CTAs. Pure value/entertainment.
- Goal: Get cold audience to watch 10+ seconds, capturing them into warm audience
- **Cost per engagement: $0.01 (sometimes as low as $0.0001)**
- Duplicate campaign and change to ThruPlay optimization for wider reach

**This is NOT a sequence -- it's a spider web.** Multiple options for people to get caught. Facebook decides which video each person sees based on their 52,000 tracked data points.

**Step 2: Direct Response to Warm Audience**
- Campaign: Conversion objective (purchase, lead, scheduled call, etc.)
- Target: Warm audience (10-second video viewers from Bin 1 + IG/FB social engagers + website visitors + email list). 365-day audience buckets.
- Select ALL interaction options: visited profile, engaged with post/ad, pressed button, messaged page, saved post
- Budget: Same per-ad-set as Bin 1 for equal reach ($5-$30/day)

**Step 3: Content Cycle Bin 2 (Warm Audience Framing)**
- Campaign: Engagement objective, ABO
- Same warm audience targeting as DR
- 5-12 DIFFERENT videos from Bin 1
- Content focus: Authority, social proof, credibility, belief management, objection handling
- Runs SIMULTANEOUSLY with DR ads
- Frequency target: 3-5 pieces of content per month

**Step 4: Cold DR Split Test**
- Run standard cold DR ads to same cold audience as Bin 1
- Compare costs: warm DR vs. cold DR
- Warm audience typically: **50% lower cost, 2x+ volume, higher close rate**

**Step 5: Scale**
- Scale Content Cycle Bin 1 budgets FIRST (feeds more people into warm audience downstream)
- Only scale Bin 2 and DR when warm audience volume increases from scaling Bin 1

#### Content Cycle Bin Content Guidelines

| Bin | Target | Content Type | Purpose |
|-----|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Bin 1** | Cold audience | Pure attention/value, no pitch, no CTA | Get into warm audience cheaply |
| **Bin 2** | Warm audience | Authority, social proof, objection handling, belief management | Support the sales cycle, frame for conversion |

### Strategy 5: The Harvester

**Best for:** Scale-ready companies, high-ticket, evergreen funnels, long sales cycles
**Budget:** $100-$500+/day minimum, built for millions/month at scale
**Content needed:** 15-25 videos per cycle bin, multiple cycle bins, 20+ retargeting angles
**Core idea:** Evergreen multi-stage ad system that respects the full sales cycle. Build warm audiences at 100-1000x cheaper than direct response.

#### Cost Advantage
- **Cost per video view / thruplay / engagement: $0.001-$0.02**
- **Competition's cost driving to website: $0.50-$2.00 average**
- **Your dollar goes 100x-1000x further**

#### The 11-Step Harvester Process

**Step 1: Video 1 -- Cold Audience Introduction**
- Launch intro video to cold audience via engagement + thruplay campaigns
- Content: Establish curiosity, authority, social proof in under 60 seconds
- No CTA. Pure content.
- Test 2-3 different Video 1 options
- Target: Ideal future customers (same as DR targeting)

**Step 2: Build Warm Audiences with Multiple Date Windows**
- Create audiences at 5-day, 10-day, 15-day increments up to 365 days
- Sources: Website traffic, FB interactions, IG interactions, video viewers, email lists
- Use bulk audience creation tools for efficiency

**Step 3: Content Cycle Bin 1 (Days 1-5)**
- Target: Newly warm audience (3-5 days old)
- 15-25 videos establishing authority, social proof, belief shaping
- Engagement + thruplay campaigns
- Goal KPI: Frequency of 3-5 in these 3-5 days
- AI decides which 3-5 videos each person sees based on engagement probability

**Step 4: Direct Response Ads (Days 5-30)**
- Target: Warm audience days 5-30
- 5-10 creative variations per placement
- Build out creative for: right column, FB newsfeed, IG newsfeed, Stories, Messenger, in-stream, marketplace
- Conversion objective for leads/calls/purchases

**Step 5: Content Cycle Bin 2 (Alongside DR)**
- Target: Same warm audience as DR (days 5-30)
- 20-25 additional videos
- Focus: Shape beliefs, manage perceptions, handle objections, establish biases
- Runs alongside DR to support conversion

**Step 6: Opt-In/Webinar Page Retargeting**
- 2-day trust funnel post opt-in
- Send 10-20 minute value videos via email and messenger
- Videos can contain light CTA at end
- Use on-demand/just-in-time webinar features for higher attendance

**Step 7: On-Page Time-Based Retargeting**
Set up audiences based on time spent on page:
- **< 30 seconds:** "You didn't stay to watch -- here's what you missed" + different angles to drive back
- **< 2 min, < 5 min, < 10 min:** Contextual follow-up based on where they stopped
- **> 60 min, > 90 min:** Drive to sales page directly

**Step 8: 180-Day Retargeting (Non-Buyers)**
- Target: Sales page visitors who didn't buy
- 20+ different creatives with 20+ different hooks/angles
- Ad sets spaced 5-7 days apart
- Map 20+ different buying motives (the "Lamborghini" principle -- people buy for 25 different reasons)
- Retargeting goes up to 180 days (FB's targeting limit)
- Mirror in email and messenger sequences

**Step 9: Post-Purchase Customer Treatment (Days 1-5)**
- Target: Thank you page visitors, 3-5 day window
- Content: Product demos, customer successes, best practices, getting started guides
- **DO NOT upsell yet** -- build relationship and product usage first
- Reduces attrition on recurring products

**Step 10: Content Cycle Bin 3 (Post-Purchase, Days 5-180)**
- Target: Thank you page visitors days 5-180 (exclude days 1-5)
- Content: Brand values, strong messaging, core knowledge
- Frame them for the next product/upsell
- Example: Show mastermind-setting lessons without pitching the mastermind

**Step 11: Upsell Sequence**
- Timing: 15-45 days after initial purchase (varies by product type)
- Leverage the framing built in Cycle Bin 3

#### Campaign Flow Summary
```
Cold (Video 1) --> Cycle Bin 1 (Days 1-5) --> DR + Cycle Bin 2 (Days 5-30) --> Opt-in Retargeting --> Time-Based Retargeting --> 180-Day Non-Buyer Retargeting --> Post-Purchase (Days 1-5) --> Cycle Bin 3 (Days 5-180) --> Upsell (Days 15-45)
```

## PART 4: AD SCALING FRAMEWORK

### 4.1 The Chadvertising Mindset

**ROAS trumps every other metric.** There is no static answer for what constitutes a "good" CPM, CTR, or any other metric. The only question: "What's the ROAS?"

Example: $230 CPM, 0.48% CTR -- looks terrible on paper. But ROAS is 6.7x. Those metrics are perfect.

**Key principles:**
1. **Strike while the iron is hot.** When campaigns are profitable, scale FIRST, optimize while scaling.
2. **Don't split test into poverty.** Scale winners instead of endlessly testing variations.
3. **Front-end + back-end ROAS.** A campaign at 1.9x front-end can be 12x+ when you factor in back-end upsells.
4. **Two modes -- pick one:** Optimizing for Profit (maximize margins) vs. Optimizing for Growth (accept lower margins for higher volume). Can't do both simultaneously.

### 4.2 Minimum ROAS Calculation

```
Step 1: Total Business Expenses (everything except ad spend) = X
Step 2: Target Profit % = Y
Step 3: Revenue Requirement = X / (1 - Y)
Step 4: Minimum ROAS = Revenue Requirement / Planned Ad Spend
```

**Example:**
- Expenses: $100,000/month
- Target: 25% profit
- Revenue needed: $133,333
- Ad spend: $50,000
- Minimum ROAS: $133,333 / $50,000 = **2.67x**

**Physical products:** Factor in COGS. A 2x ROAS with 40% COGS = barely breaking even. Recommend minimum **3x ROAS** for physical products.

### 4.3 Collected Dollars Per Booked Call

A powerful scaling trigger for call funnels:
```
Total Collected Revenue / Total Calls Booked = Collected $ Per Call
```

**Scaling triggers based on ratio (Collected $ / Cost per Call):**
- **> 10x:** Scale aggressively
- **5-10x:** Scale aggressively
- **3-5x:** Scale aggressively
- **Under 3x but profitable:** Optimize while scaling

### 4.4 ABO vs CBO Decision Rules

| Factor | ABO (Ad Set Budget) | CBO (Campaign Budget) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Best for** | Precise control, testing, lower budgets | Higher spend, letting algorithm optimize |
| **Budget threshold** | Under ~$300/day total | Over $300-500/day |
| **Advantage** | Control per ad set, force distribution | Algorithm pushes money to top performers |
| **Disadvantage** | More micromanagement | May neglect some ad sets |
| **Hammer Them** | Use ABO for long-form (ensures each video gets budget) | Use CBO for short-form campaign |
| **Forester** | ABO recommended | CBO at scale |

### 4.5 Scaling Methodology

#### Gradual Scaling (10-20% Every 2-3 Days)
- The "safe" approach
- Doesn't jolt Facebook's learning phase
- Example: $100/day > $120/day (2 days) > $150/day (2 days) > ...
- Use when: Starting to scale, risk-averse, want stable costs

#### Aggressive Scaling (Doubling or More)
- For super-stable funnels that can handle double volume
- Risk: Platform re-enters learning phase, costs may spike for a few days
- Only use after 5-7 days of consistently low cost per lead/call

#### Scale by Duplication
- Duplicate winning ad set/campaign instead of raising budget
- Fresh learning phase captures different audience pockets
- Keep original "winner" running at same budget
- New duplicate gets larger budget
- Potential overlap if audience is small; fine if targeting broad (millions+)

#### Scale in Waves
- $100/day > $300/day (hold 2 days, evaluate) > $500/day (hold 2 days) > ...
- Each wave: gather leads, handle calls, check show-up/close rates
- Then expand to next wave

### 4.6 Scaling Tiers with Frequency Thresholds

#### Early Stage ($1K-$10K/day)

| Component | Allocation |
|-----------|-----------|
| Foundation campaigns | 80-90% of budget |
| Testing campaigns | 10% of budget |
| Optimization campaigns | 10-20% of budget |

**Creative rotation:** 5-Video Rule per audience segment
**Creative refresh:** Every 2-4 weeks
**Frequency watch:** Weekly monitoring
**Scale trigger:** ROAS consistently above minimum for 5-7 days

**Audience Progression:**
- Phase 1 ($1K-$5K/day): Dominate In-Market audiences
- Phase 2 ($5K-$15K/day): Expand to Needs Convinced
- Phase 3 ($15K+/day): Test Mass Market (use Tornado or Harvester)

#### Mid-Level ($10K-$50K/day)

| Component | Allocation |
|-----------|-----------|
| Foundation campaigns | 80-90% |
| Scaling campaigns | 10-20% |
| Testing campaigns | 10% |
| Optimization campaigns | 5-10% |

**Frequency monitoring:**
- Frequency < 2: Probably fine
- Frequency 2-3: Prepare fresh creative, performance may decline soon
- Frequency > 3: Refresh creative urgently

**Creative refresh:** Every 1-2 weeks
**Multi-cam production:** Primary talking head + secondary angle = dozens of variations from single shoot. 15 hooks > 100+ unique creatives.

#### High-Level ($50K+/day)

| Component | Allocation |
|-----------|-----------|
| Foundation campaigns | 80-90% |
| "Doing okay" campaigns | 10-20% |
| Testing budget | 5-10% |

**Creative production:** Monthly shoots, 10-20 different hooks every other week
**Creative refresh:** Every 3-5 days (monitor frequency; refresh when frequency hits 5 AND ROAS diminishes)
**Always have backup creative ready.** Cannot wait until fatigue to start producing.

### 4.7 Infrastructure for Scaling

**Required before serious scaling ($10K+/day):**
1. Multiple Business Managers (minimum 2, preferably 3) -- truly independent (different entities, payment methods, admin access)
2. Payment method diversification -- different cards from different banks
3. Facebook Invoicing -- net 30 terms at $10K+/month spend
4. FOX Tag implementation -- prevents AI disapprovals
5. Credit lines formula: Target Daily Spend x Sales Cycle Days x 1.5 buffer = Minimum Credit Lines
   - Example: $20K/day x 14 days x 1.5 = $420K minimum
6. Operational capacity: Sales team, fulfillment, customer service all ready for increased volume
7. 90-120 days operating expenses in cash

### 4.8 When NOT to Scale

| Signal | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Declining ROAS with increased spend | Audience saturation or ad fatigue (possibly Andromeda-related) | Scale back to stable spend level, expand audience or improve creative |
| High frequency (>3-4) with low reach | Minority hook problem -- creative only appeals to small segment | Create majority hooks with broader appeal |
| Operational strain | Team can't handle current volume | Fix operations first |
| Cash flow issues | Stretching credit lines | Build cash reserves first |
| Inconsistent performance | Profitable some days, unprofitable others | Find and fix the cause before scaling |

## PART 5: AD DIVERSIFICATION MASTERY & THE ANDROMEDA ALGORITHM

### 5.1 The Andromeda Revolution

Meta's Andromeda algorithm (fully rolled out ~July 2025) fundamentally changed ad serving:
- System processes **10,000x more ad candidates** for enhanced personalization
- Uses complex latent relationships between interests, products, and services
- **Rewards creative volume and diversification** with better performance, lower costs, higher reach
- The old approach (3-4 creatives/week, subtle variations of same hook) is now punished with extreme costs

**Key insight:** Creative controls targeting now. Your messaging diversity IS your targeting strategy.

### 5.2 The Dog Food Exercise

To find diverse messaging angles, do this exercise for your offer:

1. Write down what you're selling
2. Write down EVERY reason someone would buy it
3. Each reason becomes a hook AND the center of an entire ad

**Dog food example (illustrative):**
- Healthier ingredients
- Joint health for older dogs
- Live longer/healthier life
- Gut balance and digestion
- Tastier (doesn't smell bad)
- No dog farts
- Less hair shedding
- More energy
- Couch doesn't get messy
- Not highly processed
- Fewer vet visits
- Better coat and skin

Each is a completely different ad with hook, body, and CTA all aligned around THAT specific reason.

### 5.3 Hook-Body-CTA as One Cohesive Unit

**Old approach (now less effective):** Film body + CTA once, swap 10-15 different hooks. Interchangeable components.

**New approach (Andromeda-optimized):** Film the ENTIRE ad from start to finish as one cohesive unit. Hook, body, and CTA all aligned around one specific reason someone would buy.

**Why:** Hook-swapping lasts shorter duration and causes significant volatility. Cohesive unique ads have much longer lifespan and better performance.

### 5.4 The Two Paths Forward

**Path 1: High Volume (50+ creatives per week)**
- Sheer volume can overcome the algorithm
- Don't need wide range of diverse messaging if you can produce 50+/week
- Requires relaunching campaigns and following ad fatigue protocol weekly
- Abnormal for most businesses (~200 creatives per month)

**Path 2: Diverse Messaging (15-20 unique ads per month)**
- Each ad built around a completely different REASON to buy
- Accounts implementing this saw zero cost inflation during Andromeda rollout
- More manageable for most businesses
- Better results with less resource investment

## PART 6: IF-THIS-THEN-THAT CAMPAIGN MANAGEMENT RULES

### 6.1 Problem: Low Conversions

**Step 1:** Find the bottleneck in the funnel by examining data at each step:
```
1,000 hit opt-in page > 100 opted in (10% CVR) > 98 hit webinar > 12 hit order page (12.2% CVR) > 2 purchased (16.7% CVR)
```
**Action:** Focus on whichever step has the lowest conversion rate. Fix that bottleneck first, then repeat.

### 6.2 Problem: CTR Below 2%

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| CTR < 2% and NOT profitable | Rotate new creatives every 72 hours. Test: new headlines, new body copy, new images/videos, dynamic creative vs static. |
| CTR < 2% but still PROFITABLE | Do NOT change the ads. Duplicate campaign, test new audiences with same creative. **Never turn off something profitable.** |

### 6.3 Problem: Learning Limited

| Approach | When to Use | How |
|----------|-------------|-----|
| Relaunch ad set | Close to conversion threshold on first run | Duplicate ad set (ABO), relaunch as new test |
| Fix funnel bottleneck | Way short of needed conversions | Find and fix choke point, then relaunch |
| Test new audiences | Multiple funnel pivots haven't worked | Use best creatives on new audience segments |
| Cyclic campaigns | Need more data volume | Duplicate campaign optimized for each funnel step (purchase + add to cart + view content). More data points help exit learning phase faster. |

### 6.4 Problem: CPA Too High

| Cause | Solution |
|-------|----------|
| Audience too small (<1M) | Widen targeting OR drop daily budget 20% until CPA stabilizes, then scale back 10-20% daily |
| Hyper-competitive demographic | Switch to manual bid campaigns. Start $200/day with $50 bid cap. Monitor reach every 2 hours. Bump bid cap $10 if reach throttled. Increase daily budget by cost-per-conversion for each new conversion. |
| Scaled too fast | Budget increases >10-30% on automated bidding cause CPA spikes. Solution: duplicate campaign at desired budget and shut off original (avoid overlap). |
| Wrong audience | Test new audiences with same creative to find lower-CPA segments |

### 6.5 Problem: High No-Show Rate on Calls

**Benchmark:** 20% or less no-show = good. 30% = OK. Above 35% = unacceptable.

| Action | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Audit marketing automation | Replace generic reminders with substantive resell content + testimonials |
| Add text reminders | Collect phone number in scheduler. Send SMS reminders. |
| Video text from closer | Send IMMEDIATELY after booking. Real, custom video from iPhone. "Excited to chat, here's my face, send questions." |
| Retargeting campaign | $5-10/day budget targeting booked-call audience (min 100 people). Content: "We're excited, here's what to expect." |
| Confirmation page VSL | Explain what to expect on call. Add testimonials. Congratulate them. |
| Deploy Hammer Them | Full 30-50 piece content retargeting within 72-hour window |

### 6.6 Problem: Lots of Add-to-Carts, Few Sales

**Optimization checklist for order/checkout pages:**
- Dynamic product retargeting ads (catalog feed via Shopify/WooCommerce)
- Green CTA buttons
- Support options (email, phone) on page
- Physical address visible
- Short testimonials formatted like tweets
- FAQ section (digital objection handling)
- Product visualization / unboxing videos
- Live chat widget (bottom right)
- Clear shipping times / free shipping
- Social proof popups (purchase notifications)
- Trust icons (security badges, guarantees)

### 6.7 Problem: Lots of Calls Scheduled, Low Close Rate

| Root Cause | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| **Framing issue** | Deploy content retargeting (VFT, Harvester, Hammer Them). Audit ad-to-funnel messaging consistency. |
| **Wrong hook / right audience** | Test new hooks with same audience |
| **Right hook / wrong audience** | Test same hooks with new audiences |
| **Lead quality** | Increase spend on successful campaigns for larger data sample. Add qualifying questions to funnel. |
| **Closer quality** | Sales training, role-playing, game footage sessions, secret shopping |

### 6.8 Problem: Leads Not Framed Properly

**Audit process:**
1. Go through the funnel as a customer (secret shop yourself)
2. Start with ads, work through funnel, watch VSLs, schedule/call
3. Note everything that's off in: ads, funnel, marketing automation, sales conversation
4. Check for: Missing VSLs on pages, generic automation copy, no content framing campaigns
5. **Solution:** Incorporate content framing at ALL stages using VFT, Harvester, or Hammer Them strategies

### 6.9 Complete Decision Rules Quick Reference

| Situation | Threshold | Action |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| ROAS > 3x for 3+ days | Profitable and stable | Scale budget 20% |
| ROAS > minimum for 5-7 days | Proven winner | Scale aggressively (20-50% or duplication) |
| ROAS 1-3x for 3+ days | Marginal | Optimize (new creative, audience tweaks) |
| ROAS < 1x for 3+ days | Losing money | Pause and diagnose (creative fatigue? wrong audience?) |
| Frequency > 3 (with declining performance) | Ad fatigue | Refresh creative immediately |
| Frequency < 2 | Healthy | Continue, monitor |
| Frequency 2-3 | Warning | Prepare fresh creative |
| CPL increasing week over week | Fatigue or saturation | Test new hooks, rotate audience |
| CTR dropping below 1% | Creative fatigue | Swap hooks/thumbnails |
| CTR below 2% (not profitable) | Weak creative | Rotate new creatives every 72 hours |
| CTR below 2% (but profitable) | Algorithm found buyers | Don't touch. Duplicate to test new audiences. |
| High CTR but low conversion | Funnel/landing page issue | Fix landing page, not the ad |
| High reach, low frequency | Majority hook (good) | Scale confidently |
| Low reach, high frequency | Minority hook (bad) | Create broader hooks |
| No-show rate > 35% | Framing failure | Deploy Hammer Them + audit automation |
| No-show rate < 20% | Healthy | Maintain current approach |
| Open rate below 40% | Email engagement issue | Improve subject lines, test send times |
| Learning limited | Insufficient conversions | Cyclic campaigns, fix funnel bottleneck, test audiences |
| CPA spiked after budget increase | Scaled too fast | Duplicate at target budget, shut off original |

## PART 7: CREATIVE PRODUCTION & WORKFLOW

### 7.1 Batch Shooting Workflow

**Monthly/bi-weekly schedule:**
1. Block half a day to film 4-6 short ads (or 10-20 hooks for high-spend)
2. Each variation: different hook OR environment OR framework
3. Multi-cam setup: primary talking head + secondary angle
4. 15 hooks with multi-cam = 100+ unique creatives possible
5. Shoot relevant B-roll in same session

**Production checklist:** Script > Film > Edit > Approve > Upload

### 7.2 The Creative Calendar (2-4 Week Cycle)

| Week | Activity |
|------|----------|
| Week 1 | Launch current creative batch. Monitor daily. |
| Week 2 | Check if CPL/CPA creeping up. Begin scripting next batch. |
| Week 3 | Film/edit new batch. Swap out underperformers. |
| Week 4 | Launch new creative. Evaluate winners from Week 1-2 for scaling. |

**At higher spend ($20K+/day), compress to weekly or even daily refresh cycles.**

### 7.3 Refreshing Without Reinventing

When a winning ad starts to fatigue, you don't need to start over:

- **Change the first 5 seconds** (new opening line, same body/CTA)
- **Change the environment** (office vs. outdoors vs. walk-and-talk)
- **Add text overlays** or progress bars
- **Cut a shorter version** (45s > 25s)
- **Re-order lines** (handle objection at second 10 instead of 25)
- **Update proof** ("400 students" > "600 students")
- **Seasonal hook** ("It's Q4, and if you haven't...")
- **Use sales call language** (real prospect phrases as hooks)

### 7.4 Ad Diversification Rules for Script Sets

When writing a set of 5-10 ads:
- **No two ads use the same hook formula**
- **No two ads use the same body structure**
- **Each ad leads with a different campaign angle**
- **Vary the format** (talking head, case study, Q&A, demo, skit)
- **Vary the length** (mix 30-sec, 60-sec, and 90-sec scripts)
- **Each ad centers on a different REASON to buy** (Dog Food Exercise)
- **Film each as one cohesive unit** (Andromeda-optimized)

### 7.5 Ad Creation Team Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| Copywriter/Scripter | Hook > Body > CTA drafts. Fresh angles, stats, testimonials. |
| On-Camera Talent | Confident delivery. Possibly second person for interview framework. |
| Videographer/Editor | Filming, B-roll, text overlays, short punchy cuts. Tools: CapCut, InShot, Premiere, DaVinci Resolve. |
| Ad Manager | Upload to platform, dynamic creative setup, compliance check, headlines/copy selection. |

**Solo?** Schedule separate script day, shoot day, edit day. Or outsource editing.

## PART 8: FUNNEL ALIGNMENT

### 8.1 Three Core Elements to Sync

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Headline** | Must mirror the ad's hook/CTA. If ad says "Double your rental income," headline must echo that. |
| **Hero Image/Video** | Match the ad's visual environment. No stock photos. |
| **Sub-headline/Caption** | Clarify the value, match the CTA promise. |

**All three must confirm:** "Yes, this page is exactly what the ad promised."

### 8.2 Friction Management

**Low friction (volume/low-ticket):**
- Link > Short landing page > Immediate CTA (opt-in, purchase, watch VSL)
- Minimal fields (name + email or phone)
- Disclaimers in footer

**High friction (high-ticket, $5K+):**
- Financial qualifier: "Have you set aside at least $X?"
- Identity qualifier: "Do you currently own [X]? Yes/No"
- Commitment qualifier: "Are you committed to X hours/week for Y days?"
- Use AFTER main pitch or in application form
- Caution: Overdoing friction kills conversions. Only use qualifiers that truly protect time.

### 8.3 Post-Opt-In / Post-Booking Page

The confirmation/thank-you page is a goldmine:
- Reinforce the big promise in the headline
- Short 1-2 minute "thank you" video
- 2-3 quick testimonial snippets
- Clear next steps
- Deploy Hammer Them content from this point forward

## Additional Benchmarks (Sophron Accelerator)

These supplementary rules come from the Sophron Meta Ads Accelerator program:

- **Creative iteration threshold:** Do NOT iterate on existing creatives until you have tested **25-30 unique ad concepts** first. Find your winners, then iterate on those winners. Iterating before you have signal is a waste.
- **Ad-level close attribution:** Track not just which ads generate leads, but which ads generate **closes**. Build an attribution sheet linking ad ID → lead → booked call → close outcome. Some ads produce high lead volume but low close rates (bad framing). Some ads produce fewer leads but higher quality. This distinction is invisible if you only optimize for CPL.
- **Offer heuristic for ads:** A winning offer = "specific outcome + specific process." If your offer can't be stated in that format, the ad will struggle regardless of creative quality. Benchmark: an offer works when you can sign 10+ clients from cold traffic at a $1-3K customer acquisition cost.
- **Short vs. extended VSL decision (for ad-to-funnel):** B2B with a strong offer → test short hyper-direct VSL (2-5 minutes) first. If conversion is low, extend to answer all objections. Start short, expand if needed — not the other way around.

## PART 10: SMO CAMPAIGN STRUCTURE (Sell More Online)

> Source: Launching Meta Ads Blueprint (Evan Seech, Sell More Online) + Meta Andromeda Blueprint

This section documents the SMO (Sell More Online) default campaign structure for launching ads inside Meta. This is the structure SMO uses "every single time we enter a new ad account or a pre-existing ad account."

### 10.1 Campaign Optimization: ABO for Cold Traffic

SMO defaults to **ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization) for both warm and cold campaigns**, not CBO. The rationale: "We sometimes find winning ad sets that Meta missed in a CBO."

**When to use CBO instead of ABO:**
- If you cannot afford $100/day per ad set, use CBO
- Example: $150/day budget with 3 ad sets = $50/ad set (below $100 minimum) --> run CBO at $150/day on the campaign level instead

**Minimum spend rules:**
- ABO: $100/day minimum PER ad set
- CBO: $125/day minimum on the campaign level
- If you cannot spend $100/day total, do not launch yet

**Budget commitment:** Your budget should be something you can comfortably spend for 30-60 days (45-60 days if launching ads for the first time) without seeing ROI.

### 10.2 Individual Ads, NOT Dynamic Creative

**SMO does NOT use Dynamic Creative. They launch Individual Ads.**

Each ad is a standalone creative uploaded as its own ad unit. The platform does not combine/test elements automatically -- instead, 20-30 individual ads run in each ad set, each with the same set of body copy, headlines, and descriptions attached.

> See "PART 12: Contradiction Resolution -- Dynamic Creative" below for how this relates to the existing Dynamic Creative methodology in this skill.

### 10.3 Warm Audience Campaign Structure

```
Campaign: ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization)

Ad Sets (Warm Stack):
├── 365-Day Facebook & Instagram Engagers
│   ├── Saved Posts
│   ├── Interacted With A Post
│   ├── Pressed Ad Button
│   ├── Messaged The Page
│   └── Visited Profile
├── 15-Second Video Viewers (all videos on FB & IG profile)
└── 180-Day Website Visitors

*Exclude Past Purchasers from all ad sets*

Creative Level (SAME across all ad sets):
├── 20-30 Individual Ads per ad set (the SAME ads in each)
├── 3-5 Pieces of Body Copy (same on every ad)
│   └── Mix: 2 Long, 2 Medium, 1 Short
├── 3-5 Headlines (same on every ad)
│   └── All ideally under 40 characters
└── 1-2 Descriptions (same on every ad)
```

### 10.4 Cold Audience Campaign Structure

```
Campaign: ABO (Ad Set Budget Optimization)

Ad Sets:
├── Look-A-Like (LAL)
│   └── Start 1%, broaden to 2-5%, then 6-10% as you saturate
│   └── Need 500+ SQLs/conversions minimum for quality 1% LAL
├── Interest Stack (Cold)
│   └── LAYER interests on top of each other
│   └── Keep "Audience Definition" showing broad
│   └── Don't overthink -- layer relevant interests, behaviors, demographics
└── Broad
    └── No targeting selections -- trust Meta fully
    └── Only set Age & Location under Controls

Creative Level (SAME across all ad sets):
├── 20-30 Individual Ads per ad set (the SAME ads in each)
├── 3-5 Pieces of Body Copy (same on every ad)
├── 3-5 Headlines (same on every ad)
└── 1-2 Descriptions (same on every ad)
```

### 10.5 AI Enhancement Settings (CRITICAL)

On ALL ads, turn off ALL Advantage Creative Enhancements toggles **EXCEPT**:
- **Positive Comments** -- leave ON
- **Delay CTA** -- leave ON (if available)

Also:
- Turn OFF "AI Image variation" on the creative level
- Ensure "cropping" is set to "original" for each placement, especially videos
- Toggle OFF "Share some of your budget with other ad sets" when running ABO

### 10.6 Lookalike Audience Tiers

| LAL % | Quality | Audience Size | CPM | When to Use |
|-------|---------|---------------|-----|-------------|
| **1%** | Highest quality, closest match | Smallest | Higher CPM | Start here. Quality over volume. Source must be high quality. |
| **2-5%** | Good but not elite | Medium | Lower CPM | Scale beyond 1% saturation. Stack 1-3% or 1-5% in custom audience. |
| **6-10%** | Broad similarity | Maximum scale | Cheapest CPM | Massive scale. When other LALs capped out. Quality decreases. |

**Common stacking setups:**
- Stacked 1-3% (layer all in custom audience section)
- Stacked 1-5% (layer all in custom audience section)
- Separate ad sets for 1%, 2%, 3% (ONLY at high spend for clear split test)

### 10.7 Cost Caps

Cost caps send a signal to Meta about your ideal cost per result. **Not an exact science** -- keeps you within a certain auction range.

**How to set cost caps:**
1. Determine ideal cost per result (e.g., $150 cost per call)
2. Set cost cap at **2-5x** the ideal cost per result (e.g., $300-$750)
3. Start at 3x
4. If ads don't spend, increase to 3.5x, then 4x, then 4.5x, then 5x+
5. If a campaign STOPS spending, toggle up the cost cap by $10-$30 every few hours

**When to use cost caps:**
- During high inflationary periods (holidays, election cycles, "micro holidays")
- As a scaling strategy / split test alongside highest-volume campaigns
- When you want more cost control at scale

**When NOT to use cost caps:**
- First-time launches on new accounts -- just run "Highest Volume" with no cost cap
- When learning the account behavior initially

### 10.8 Campaign Objective & Event Optimization

**Default campaign objective:** Leads (most of the time)

**Standard event selection (optimize for the deepest funnel event with sufficient volume):**

| Funnel Type | Recommended Standard Event |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Call Funnels | "Schedule" or "Submit Application" |
| Webinar/Lead Magnet Funnels | "Lead" or "Complete Registration" |
| Challenge Funnels | "Purchase" |

**Volume target:** Ideally 50 conversions per week, bare minimum 20. Push spend to hit this volume for fast algorithm optimization.

**Cyclic Campaign Strategy:** Duplicate a winning campaign and run it optimized for a different standard event to reach new people. Example for a call funnel (VSL Page > Schedule Page > Confirmation Page):
- Optimize around "Submit Application" (qualified applications)
- Optimize around "Schedule" (qualified applicants who scheduled)
- Move "Submit Application" to confirmation page (seasons new event, reaches different profiles)
- Add "Complete Registration" or "Lead" to confirmation page and optimize around those

**Pro tip:** Start conditioning multiple standard events on your confirmation page from day one, even if you only optimize around one initially. Season them in the background for future split tests.

### 10.9 Pixel Conditioning

**ONLY let your pixel see qualified prospects.** Whatever data hits the pixel and reports back to the results column IS who Meta will target next.

**Best practices:**
- Filter unqualified leads via application conditional logic so only qualified leads hit the confirmation page with the standard event
- Use CAPI (Conversion API) to fire back only qualified SQLs
- Example: Salesperson marks lead as qualified in CRM --> triggers workflow --> fires to Conversion API --> seasons the event
- CAPI engagement score: keep at 8+
- If experiencing bad audience quality: temporarily remove browser pixel, only send conversion events for qualified SQLs through CAPI, once quality improves put pixel back

### 10.10 Placement Settings

**Always exclude:**
- Audience Network (uncheck every time)
- "Allow limited spend to excluded placements" (uncheck)

**Optional (low impact):**
- Messenger and Threads can stay on -- minimal spend/results on these placements

### 10.11 Scaling Under Andromeda

**When scaling on low budgets:**
- Be more lenient with the 3-4 day decision rule -- extend it on new accounts with little data
- Still launch with 20-30 unique ads per ad set, even on small budgets
- Choose funnel types with highest probability of producing revenue at low spend

**Ad account scaling actions:**
- Keep launching new ad sets within the campaign
- If you hit ad fatigue and duplicating an ad set doesn't overcome it, relaunch the whole campaign (resets the machine learning model, launches to fresh audiences)

**New metric to watch:** Cost Per 1,000 Account Centers Reached -- if continuously going up, you're hitting the same people too many times. Drop it through creative testing.

## PART 11: IF-THEN FUNNEL DIAGNOSTICS (SMO Framework)

> Source: IF-THEN Funnel SOP -- VSL Call Funnel (Evan Seech, Sell More Online)

This is the complete diagnostic decision tree for troubleshooting a VSL Call Funnel. When a metric is bad, follow the corresponding IF-THEN path to find and fix the issue. **Test one variable at a time.**

### 11.1 IF CPMs Are High

**Diagnosis path:**

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Messaging too specific?** | Ultra-specific messaging = bidding for a narrow sliver = premium CPMs | Test across a spectrum: broad messages that still hit real pain, mid-level specificity with flexible hooks, AND hyper-specific (accept higher CPMs). |
| **Ad format not native?** | Running 16:9 videos in Reels placements (designed for 9:16) | Reformat to 9:16 vertical. Native-looking content wins every time. Overly polished/"branded" underperforms. |
| **Creative quality poor?** | Bad lighting, poor audio, jarring cuts | Meta is smart enough to detect low-quality ads visually, auditorily, and contextually. Fix production quality. |
| **Offer messaging doesn't resonate?** | Not talking about what audience actually wants | Articulate pain in their language. Messaging that resonates = cheaper CPM. |
| **Lack of creative diversification?** | All creatives are slight variations of same message | Vary pain points, tone (urgent vs. calm), angles (fear, curiosity, FOMO, proof, transformation). Multiple formats, messaging angles, visual structures. |

### 11.2 IF Click-Through Rate Is Low

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Messaging quality** | Not direct enough with CTA. Vague or motivational-poster-like. | Be direct. Speak to real-time context of what they're experiencing NOW. |
| **No emotional urgency** | Ad doesn't create an emotional spike that breaks scroll mode | Make it specific, real, personal. Use the "Dermatologist Example": don't say "55% get skin cancer, get checked" -- say "If you have a black spot on your neck, arm, or face, and you're a young male in Florida, there's a 55% chance it's skin cancer..." Emotional specificity wins. |
| **Offering something they don't want** | Even great delivery can't save an irrelevant offer | Ask: Would I stop and click this? Is the benefit obvious? Does the CTA build curiosity or urgency? |
| **Don't "look the part"** | Environment/appearance doesn't match what you sell | Does your environment match what you sell? Can you simulate a resonant situation? (See Scroll-Stopping Creative Strategy section) |

### 11.3 IF Application Rate Is Low

Defined as: Applications / Unique Page Visitors

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Page load speed** | More than 1-2 seconds to load = hemorrhaging users | Use free page speed tester. Optimize images, video, scripts, hosting. Mobile-first (80-90% of traffic is mobile). |
| **Branding and visual credibility** | Looks like a scammy affiliate funnel | Custom-branded, clean UI, real logos, high-quality visuals. Generic templates = bounce. |
| **Message congruency** | Ad says one thing, landing page says another | 100% consistent messaging throughout entire pre-click and post-click experience. Follow Power of One: ONE message, ONE solution, ONE avatar, ONE pain point, ONE desire. |
| **Page content gaps** | Doesn't answer: What is this? Why should I care? How does this help me? Can I trust these people? | Short scannable sections, emotional bullet points, case studies/screenshots, minimal fluff. |
| **Application form drop-off** | Form too long or too invasive | 3-5 questions MAX. First question must be frictionless (Yes/No). Use Typeform analytics to find bounce question. Frame complex questions with context. |

### 11.4 IF Booking Rate Is Low

Defined as: Booked Calls / Application Submissions

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Call availability** | Not enough time slots | More availability = higher conversion. Bring on closers if needed. |
| **Booking flow UX** | Sending to completely new page = 30-50% drop-off | Use native scheduler integrations (e.g., Typeform + Calendly where calendar is the last step of application). Make transition feel like one smooth step. |
| **Not selling the call** | Booking page doesn't convey call value | Must sell the value of the call itself. "We'll show you exactly where your funnel is leaking and how to fix it in 30 days." "We'll build you a 90-day scale roadmap live on the call." |

### 11.5 IF VSL Play Rate or Engagement Is Low

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **No video thumbnail** | Autoplay may not work on mobile | Split test a thumbnail with human face + curiosity text. Example: Still frame with overlay "Here's the playbook we used to 4x this campaign in 90 days..." |
| **Page headline doesn't sell the video** | Headline doesn't build curiosity or set expectations | Rewrite: "Why most agencies fail to scale past $50K/month" or "8-minute training breaks down exactly how we fix [pain] without [common objection]" |
| **VSL not framed in the ad CTA** | Prospect doesn't know what's on the next page | In ad copy say: "After you click, you'll land on a short 8-minute training that shows you how we cut lead costs by 60% and booked 175 calls in 30 days." |
| **Long video with no chapters** | Drop-off due to length without navigation | Add chapters: The Problem, The New Model, Case Study, What We Do, How to Book a Call. |
| **FAQs bloating the VSL** | Core video loses focus | Split FAQs to confirmation page or scrollable modules below VSL. |
| **Wrong length** | Too long or too short for the audience/offer | Test 4-6 min (short) vs. 15-18 min (long, for high-ticket). Track engagement by watch time %. |
| **Wrong VSL style** | Format doesn't match the audience | Test: screenshare Gamma/Google doc, direct to camera with light editing, direct to camera with heavy editing. Setting must match what you sell. |
| **Wrong hook** | Hook doesn't compel watching | Test direct hooks (state the offer/what's covered) vs. story-driven hooks. |

### 11.6 IF Show Rate Is Low

This is one of the most expensive and most fixable leaks.

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Sales team follow-up** | Generic "Hey, confirming our call" texts | Reps must act as strategic advisors: drop resources, build trust, manually reach out with VALUE. Make showing up the logical next step. |
| **No manual pre-call nurture** | No assets sent between booking and call | Send custom PDFs, case study links, voice notes, screenshare videos, personalized Loom breakdowns. A single manual message can 2x show rate. |
| **No trust assets deployed** | Sales team not sending credibility material | Deploy: client win breakdowns, internal SOPs, onboarding documents, video trainings, FAQ videos, competitor differentiation videos, negative review rebuttals, Stripe backend screenshots, behind-the-scenes walkthroughs. |
| **Weak pre-call email flow** | Generic reminder emails | Send 2-6 emails/day in a 3-4 day window before call. Each should: answer objections, set expectations, break false beliefs, deliver case studies, ask questions, pre-frame the offer. Check email deliverability health (GlockApps). |
| **No content retargeting** | No ads reinforcing between booking and call | Run 30-70 pieces of content: value-based short/long form, belief-planting, question-answering, objection-breaking content. Each piece speaks to different funnel stage (pain awareness, objection reversal, proof/credibility, trust/likability). |
| **Negative reviews uncombated** | Prospects Google you and find complaints | Make videos addressing negative reviews head-on. Screen share the thread, break down context, explain why those people didn't buy, show your 1,000+ 5-star reviews. Own the narrative. |
| **Messaging congruency breaks** | Ad says X, funnel says Y, emails say Z | Full congruency from click to call is mandatory. Same core message throughout. |
| **Poor confirmation page** | No pre-framing after booking | Add video snippets (30s-4min each): "What to expect on the call," "Who this is not for," "Biggest myths we hear," "What makes our system different," "Pricing overview," "Client success breakdowns," "Common questions," "Objection handling." Add thumbnails with the topic/question as text. Make it scrollable, mobile-optimized, easy to consume. |

### 11.7 IF Close Rate Is Low

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Sales script/flow** | Winging it, not structured | Use a 3-pillar framework. Address objections in first 5 minutes, not at the close. |
| **Entry frame** | Prospects enter skeptical/cold/guarded | Close rate is downstream of show rate best practices. Improve pre-call nurture, trust assets, and content retargeting. |
| **No real scarcity/urgency** | Nothing compelling immediate action | Use real scarcity: "We onboard 10 clients max/month, 3 spots left" (transparently show client list). "Our cohort starts Aug 15, closes for 4 weeks." Use positioning language like "if we take on this deal." |
| **Payment plan overuse** | High close count but low AOV | Payment plans becoming a crutch. Attracting low-cash clients. Frame full value properly. |

### 11.8 IF Lead Quality Is Low

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Generic ad messaging** | Using "get more leads," "free," "easy," "start from zero" | Reframe copy to speak to: business owners with cash, people already running ads, decision-makers with pain AND budget. You attract what you say. |
| **Pixel seeing unqualified data** | Unqualified leads hitting the standard event | Filter bad leads on application. Only allow qualified leads to hit schedule/confirmation page with the standard event. For bad audience bucket: temporarily remove pixel, only send conversion events for qualified SQLs through CAPI, once quality improves put pixel back. |
| **Confusing "cold" with "unqualified"** | Reps say "unqualified" but leads are just cold | Nurture and pre-frame work determines warmth at show-up. Perfect leads coming in cold, guarded, and skeptical won't buy. Fix the pre-call experience. |

### 11.9 IF Opt-In Rate Is Low (for funnels with opt-in before VSL)

Target: 30%+ opt-in rate

| Check | Problem | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Not framing the next step** | Prospect doesn't understand why there's a form | Explain the form and attach a bonus. Example: "Tell us where to send the 3 bonus scripts that pair with this free training. Instant access on the next page." |
| **Page speed / mobile issues** | Slow load, tiny fonts on mobile | Test page speed. Fix mobile input font sizes (avoid zoom on iPhones). Test full-screen Typeform popup vs. inline. |
| **Generic design** | Template-looking page | Clean, branded design. Custom elements. |
| **Consider removing the opt-in entirely** | Low opt-in is killing conversions | Sometimes removing the opt-in produces 50%+ improvement. Especially true when: selling to affluent buyers, running high-ticket application funnels, running cold traffic. Only keep if you have a strong email strategy or killer setting team. |

### 11.10 IF Offer Requires More Education

When direct-response funnels aren't working despite exhausting all optimization:

| Approach | Details |
|----------|---------|
| **Content-first ad strategy** | Run a cycle bin of educational content. Then run DR ads to 50%+ video viewers of that content. |
| **Live webinar funnel** | Webinars let you build belief, educate, and frame the buying decision. Better for offers needing more context. |
| **Alternative VSL styles** | Test free training VSL, case study VSL, podcast-style VSL. |

### 11.11 Quick-Reference Diagnostic Flowchart

```
CPM too high?         --> Test messaging width + ad format nativeness + creative diversification
CTR low?              --> Test emotional resonance + CTA directness + "look the part"
App rate low?         --> Fix load speed + message congruency + form length (3-5 Qs max)
Booking rate low?     --> Open calendar + fix UX + sell the call value
VSL play rate low?    --> Test thumbnail + rewrite headline + frame in ad CTA + chapters
Show rate low?        --> Deploy pre-call trust machine (emails + content + manual nurture)
Close rate low?       --> Fix entry frame + real urgency + structured script
Lead quality low?     --> Reframe messaging + pixel conditioning + CAPI
Opt-in rate low?      --> Remove it, test headlines, attach bonus offers
Complex offer?        --> Add content-first step or webinar
```

## PART 12: SCROLL-STOPPING CREATIVE STRATEGY (SMO Framework)

> Source: How To Make Scroll-Stopping Ad Creatives SOP (Evan Seech, Sell More Online)

This section covers the visual, environmental, and presentational elements that make people stop scrolling and watch your ad. **The majority of the heavy lifting of an ad campaign comes from the creative itself. If the creative can't get people to stop, watch, listen, and take action, the rest of the funnel doesn't matter.**

### 12.1 Creative-Level KPIs

When ads are identified as the bottleneck (no leads, no calls, CTR below 1%), drill into these sub-metrics on the creative level:

| Metric | Definition | KPI Target |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| **Thumb-Stop Rate** | % of people who stop and watch the hook | **20%+** |
| **Hold Rate (25%)** | % who watched hook AND stayed for 25% of video | Track trend |
| **Hold Rate (50%)** | % who watched hook AND stayed for 50% of video | Track trend |
| **25% Video Play-Thru Rate** | % who watched 25% of video | Track trend |
| **50% Video Play-Thru Rate** | % who watched 50% of video | Track trend |
| **75% Video Play-Thru Rate** | % who watched 75% of video | Track trend |
| **95% Video Play-Thru Rate** | % who watched 95% of video | Track trend |
| **100% Video Play-Thru Rate** | % who watched 100% of video | Track trend |

**Interpretation guide:**
- Good Thumb-Stop Rate + Good CTR + Low 25% retention = People resonate with hook, click early (can be fine if cost per result is good)
- Good Thumb-Stop Rate + Low 25% retention + Bad cost per result = People stop and click but aren't properly framed when they land, so they bounce
- Declining performance per ad correlates with declining Thumb-Stop Rate and retention

**When to drill down:** Only when ads are the MAIN bottleneck. If people aren't even clicking to the page, the issue is the creative.

### 12.2 The 8 Components of a Winning Creative

#### Component 1: Dressing the Part

**You have to "play the character" you are trying to be or portray.**

| Target Audience | Dress Code | Example |
|-----------------|-----------|---------|
| Accredited investors / fund managers | Professional, polished | Blue polo, blazer, nice watch, glasses |
| Home service professionals (HVAC, roofers, electricians) | Dress like them | Baseball hat with logo, long-sleeve polo, collared shirt |
| Internet marketers / coaches | "Work from home" attire | Black/graphic T-shirts, hoodies, designer brands (Balenciaga, All Saints, Off-White) |
| Professional services (IT, accounting firms) | Clean-cut professional | Collared shirt, nice watch, rented professional office |
| Make money online | Attention-grabbing | Shirt that says "MONEY", nice watch, necklace |

**Case study:** A Google Ads client targeting home services was filming with an info-product-style background (wood panels, LEDs, graphic tees). He looked like every agency that had ripped off contractors before. Solution: Refilmed in front of Home Depot wearing a hat and collared shirt. Result: 200+ calls at $60/call.

**Rule:** Rotate 2-3 outfits per shoot that "look the part" and resonate with your ICP.

#### Component 2: Your Environment

**The setting must be congruent with your message, brand, and target audience.**

| What You Sell | Environment Examples |
|---------------|---------------------|
| YouTube content services | Podcast studio with multiple camera angles |
| Google ads to home services | In front of Home Depot with truck in background |
| Funnel building | Office with awards, money paintings, LED lights |
| Trading/investing | In front of trading desk with charts |
| Real estate fund management | Clean office, outside large apartment complexes |
| Make money online | Studio with wood panels and LEDs, inside exotic sports car |

**Meta's algorithm takes into account your environment, physical items around you, and what you're saying when deciding who to serve the ad to.** Plan environments deliberately for each shoot.

**Case study:** YouTube content agency client filmed outdoors on a couch. Leads cancelled calls saying "I saw your setup and you don't look like you do YouTube." Refilmed in a podcast studio with multiple camera angles. Ads took off, VSL play rate went up, results poured in -- even with similar messaging.

**Rule:** Choose 1-3 environments per shoot that match your character AND who you sell to.

#### Component 3: Presentation (Energy & Quality)

**How you speak:**
- Strong intonation and energy when filming
- No stuttering, no monotone, not too fast, not too slow
- Audio must be clear -- not too quiet, not clipping
- Present yourself like a confident individual
- **Dial energy levels and vocal volume up 50% minimum when filming ads** (film a test ad first and watch it back)

**How you look on camera:**
- Invest in a decent studio setup or rent one
- Good lighting (3-point lighting setup OR natural light clearly lighting up face)
- High-quality camera (DSLR or iPhone rear camera)
- Bad camera quality or bad lighting = scroll past
- Camera quality itself can be a scroll stopper

**Rule:** Record a test ad and watch it back before every shoot to verify energy, lighting, and audio.

#### Component 4: Messaging (The Stalker Hook Concept)

**"Stalker Hooks"** are statements so specific and targeted that viewers feel you are reading their mind: "Oh yeah, this is crazy. This is exactly what I was thinking about. These guys know my problems."

**Stalker Hook examples:**
- "If you're spending a couple hundred dollars per day on ads, I bet you're hitting this annoying scaling wall at $500 or $1,000 per day in spend. Everything just seems to break there. Let me tell you why you can't get past that wall."
- "Are your leads just feeling super cold and guarded lately? Like, they just sling out a ton of objections at you, and they hold onto their wallet super tight? Here's how you can change that."
- "If you're using Google LSA or getting leads from Home Advisor, I bet you're tired of sharing leads with every other roofer in your area, and really tired of getting price shopped as a result."

**How to find Stalker Hooks:**
1. Load 10-15 qualified sales call transcripts into an LLM
2. Ask it: What problems did people have? Why did they book? What are their goals/desires? Give direct quotes.
3. Take exact phrases from prospects and formulate them into hooks
4. Case study: Client used "I don't think I'm using even 1/10th of GHL's capabilities" -- words taken directly from sales calls. One ad booked 500+ calls at 7x ROAS and ran for MONTHS.

**Key messaging rules:**
- Hooks must be punchy, direct, highly relevant to real-time problems/circumstances
- Don't go in circles repeating the same thing 3 different ways in the hook
- If messaging is loose, fluffy, or lazy, it gets skipped
- Write down the singular avatar, then list every pain point, problem, situation, and desire
- Each reason becomes its own individual ad (or hook)

#### Component 5: Visual Aids, Props & Gestures

**Visual pattern interrupts and props to incorporate:**

**Physical props:** Holding/flicking money, pouring coffee, drinking something, lifting phone, using whiteboard/flipchart, writing on paper, watering a plant, closing a book, catching/squeezing a water bottle, making a sign

**Body movement:** Walking towards camera, running, jumping into frame, spinning in chair, sitting down in first frame, standing on table/car roof/house roof, sitting on car/table/outdoor object, laying down

**Environment play:** Film in shower, film from above (laying down), sit in car, film outside on objects

**Format variations:** Podcast style, UGC style, demo/screenshare style (on computer or iPad), educational/teaching style, short-form "in-app" edited style

**Camera angles:** Wide shot, overhead shot, low shot, medium shot, zoomed-in shot

**Case study:** A simple whiteboard prop added to Listkit's ads generated over $250K in profit from one ad.

**Rule:** Add 1 visual aid, visual hook, or gesture per script. Scroll social media for 5-10 visual hooks that catch your attention and mirror them.

#### Component 6: Scene Changes for Retention

Vary scenes, camera angles, or visuals every few seconds to maintain retention:
- Sit down for a line, then stand up, then walk towards camera
- Change environment, props, or what you're doing as you progress through the ad
- Even minor background changes keep viewers engaged
- **More watching = more likely to click AND better framed when they land**

#### Component 7: Editing Quality

**Critical editing guidelines:**
- NO stock images or stock footage (looks cheap, performs terribly)
- No lazy editing with generic icons
- Visuals should aid messaging, not distract from it
- No crazy or confusing fonts, no weird gradients/filters
- Use captions always
- Test: "If I watch this with sound off, do I still know the message?"
- Keep main elements out of the "dead zone" (corners/edges cropped on mobile)
- Audio quality is non-negotiable -- bad audio ruins performance
- Background music: not too loud, not inappropriate

**Editing quality check questions:**
- Does this look good from an objective, non-biased perspective?
- Is the editing trash, mediocre, or too over-edited?
- Should there be more or fewer overlays?
- Are overlay timing and slide-in moments correct?
- Are captions free of spelling errors?

**Finding editors:** Get someone who has edited viral short-form content or e-commerce ads. Use frame.io for revisions/collaboration. Give editors performance feedback on the metrics of ads they edited.

#### Component 8: The Offer as Scroll-Stopper

A strong, cold-traffic-optimized front-end offer can itself stop the scroll:
- Must have an attractive, interesting, fresh value proposition
- Weave proof into the hook/body so people think "This is a smoking deal"
- Case study: "We will beat your best performing ad campaign guaranteed for $297" -- super attractive offer that pulled people in when combined with proof

### 12.3 Visual Editing: Banners on Video Ads

Use 4:5 aspect ratio instead of 9:16. Top 10-15% of frame = banner with black/colored background containing a pain statement, question, desire, or offer articulation.

**Purpose:** Communicates value proposition before they even hear audio -- a "visual hook" in text format.

### 12.4 Text Overlays in First 3-5 Seconds

Add text overlays in the first 3-5 seconds that articulate the overall angle, pain point, question, direct statement, payoff, guarantee, or desired outcome.

**Purpose:** People read the overlay super fast and immediately know what the ad is about, pulling them in. Works like an in-app "short form" overlay.

### 12.5 Film Prep Checklist

1. Shave, clean haircut
2. Pick 2-3 outfits that "look the part" for your ICP
3. Choose 1-3 environments matching your character and target audience
4. Set up high-quality camera (DSLR or iPhone rear camera)
5. Ensure 3-point lighting OR clear natural light on face
6. Test audio -- not clipping, sounds good
7. Dial energy/vocal volume up 50% minimum
8. Record a test ad and watch back to verify everything
9. Choose 5-10 visual hooks from social media to mirror
10. Add 1 visual aid/gesture per script
11. Block 2-3 hours for filming session
12. Film 3-5 ads in one setting/outfit, then switch for next 3-5

## PART 13: META ANDROMEDA COMPLIANCE (SMO Framework)

> Source: Meta Andromeda Blueprint (Evan Seech, Sell More Online)

This section details the specific creative diversity requirements from Meta's Andromeda retrieval engine update, supplementing the Andromeda overview already in Part 5 of this skill.

### 13.1 What Andromeda Actually Does

Andromeda is a change in the **ad retrieval process** (the first step in how Meta serves ads). It sifts through millions of possible ad candidates and narrows them to a few thousand most relevant ads, which then pass to the ranking stage (Lattice).

**Key facts:**
- Andromeda can now sift through **10,000x more ads** at any given time
- Your 3-5 new creatives per week are competing against an OCEAN of content
- "The system uses complex latent relationships between people's interests, products, and services to deliver more personalized ads" (Meta)
- Andromeda has already led to an 8% improvement in ads quality (Meta's measurement)
- Fully rolled out by ~October 2025 across all accounts

### 13.2 The 3 Dimensions of Creative Diversity

Meta's definition of creative diversification requires variation across three specific dimensions:

| Dimension | Definition | What "Same" Looks Like (PENALIZED) | What "Different" Looks Like (REWARDED) |
|-----------|-----------|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Theme** | Topic/ad angle -- the subject of the ad | All ads about "scaling ads" | Ad 1: pricing ceiling, Ad 2: referral dependency, Ad 3: price shopping, Ad 4: creative fatigue |
| **Messaging** | What you actually say -- the specific words | "The #1 way to fix show rates is X" vs. "The best way to fix show rates is X" (NOT varied) | Completely different pain points, desires, case studies, circumstances articulated per ad |
| **Visuals** | Editing + Environment + Creator in the video | Same creator + same setting = same ad to Meta | Different camera angles, locations, shirts, creators, editing styles, aspect ratios |

**All three must vary together.** Changing one dimension while keeping the other two the same will still be penalized.

### 13.3 The Creative Similarity Penalty

Meta is rolling out a **Creative Similarity metric** that shows how similar your ads are to each other. You will be penalized if they are too similar.

**How Meta determines similarity:**
- Two ads are "identical" if they share similar messaging, visuals, and especially the **first 3 seconds**
- Even if copy, message, or CTA is different, similar visuals = same ad
- Slight copy changes will NOT be recognized as a "new ad" or "new concept"

**Specific similarity triggers:**
- Same creator + same setting = same ad
- Same messaging + same visuals = same ad
- Hook & Body method with little variation = same thing to same people

**Consequence:** Meta will only serve one of the "duplicate" ads, effectively wasting your creative slots and limiting your reach.

### 13.4 Persona-Based Ad Creation Exercise

Instead of creating "one-size-fits-all" ads, create ads built around specific sub-avatars (personas):

**Step 1: Define 1-4 avatars you sell to**
Example for a paid ads agency:
- Avatar 1: Coaches spending < $500/day on ads (confused about scaling)
- Avatar 2: Coaches spending > $500/day on ads (need expert help)
- Avatar 3: Coaches NOT running ads (afraid to start)

**Step 2: List all reasons/pains/desires/circumstances per avatar**
Example for Avatar 1 (coaches < $500/day):
- Confused about how to scale
- Hired agencies before that didn't work
- Keep turning ads off and on with no consistency
- Struggling to work the ads manager
- Threw together a funnel with freelancers and it has holes
- Cannot figure out why ads won't scale
- Copying competitors and still not working

**Step 3: Each reason becomes its own INDIVIDUAL ad**
- Hook centered on ONE specific reason/pain/desire/circumstance
- Body supports that specific hook (can "pepper in" 1-2 related angles)
- CTA aligned to the central theme
- Film as one cohesive unit from start to finish

**Protein powder example (41 angles for one product):**
1. Organic ingredients, 2. Clean ingredients, 3. Doesn't clump, 4. High absorption, 5. Celebrity endorsement, 6. Tastes good, 7. Available locally, 8. Budget-friendly, 9. Stays full longer, 10. Kills cravings, 11. Has collagen, 12. Low calorie, 13. Tired of bars, 14. Tired of cooking, 15. Fits busy routine, 16. Has creatine, 17. Beef organs, 18. Not lab-made, 19. Third-party tested, 20. Low sugar, 21. Great with water, 22. Great with milk, 23. Meal replacement, 24. US-made/2-day shipping, 25. No fillers/gums, 26. No seed oils, 27. No artificial flavors, 28. Third-party clean, 29. Nutrient dense, 30. Blends into smoothies, 31. Supports energy, 32. No midday crash, 33. Overall wellness, 34. For hardcore athletes, 35. For casual fitness, 36. For leisure athletes, 37. No Yellow 5/Blue 5/Red 40, 38. Baking recipes, 39. Added colostrum, 40. No bloating, 41. No microplastics

Each is its OWN ad reaching a DIFFERENT type of person through Andromeda.

### 13.5 The Two Paths to Andromeda Success

| Path | Volume | Diversity | Creative Lifespan | Best For |
|------|--------|-----------|-------------------|----------|
| **Path 1: High Volume** | 50+ creatives per week | Can be narrower in messaging range | Shorter (fatigue quicker, relaunch weekly) | Large teams, agencies with production capacity (~200 creatives/month) |
| **Path 2: Diverse Messaging** | 15-20+ unique ads per month (minimum 30 individualized ads per launch) | Wide range of themes, each ad a different reason to buy | Longer lifespan per ad | Most businesses. Accounts implementing this saw ZERO cost inflation during rollout. |

### 13.6 Hook & Body Method: Updated Guidance

**Old practice (pre-Andromeda):** Film 10-30 hooks and 1-2 bodies, splice them. Majority of each video export is the same.

**Current reality:** Hook & Body method only works now if you launch 50+ new creatives per week. They fatigue quicker. For most businesses, this is unsustainable.

**New best practice:** Film full-length individualized ads from start to finish. Hook, Body, CTA all aligned around one specific reason. This gives better longevity.

**"Sprinkling In" technique:** The hook should be centered around ONE central pain/desire/outcome. You CAN pepper in other angles in the body, but they should support and flow from the central hook. (See the annotated MSP ad scripts in the Andromeda Blueprint source for detailed examples.)

### 13.7 Creative Iteration Under Andromeda

When you find a winning theme, you can "play within that sandbox" -- but differently than before:

**What NO LONGER works:**
- Reshoot with same words in different shirt
- Duplicate campaign with 90% same creatives and minor tweaks

**What DOES work when iterating a winner:**
- Fully rescripted ad in a different setting (change the visual AND the words)
- New diverse individualized ads WITHIN that theme (same theme, different messaging)
- Different editing style, especially in first 3 seconds
- Weave in new B-roll
- Change the creator/actor
- Try new ad format (educational, testimonial, case study, demo, selfie, skit)
- If winning ad was outcome-driven, try desire-driven, pain-driven, or curiosity-driven hooks on same theme

**Visual variation checklist for iterations:**
- New locations (inside/outside/different background)
- New creator in the ad
- New camera angles
- Different shirts (film 3-5 in one outfit, switch for next 3-5)
- Weave in current events or "grand narrative"
- New ad format (carousel, static, etc.)
- Recycle winners with fresh B-roll
- Change aspect ratios (4:5, 9:16, 4:5 with banner, 1:1)

**Key rule:** ALWAYS continue feeding the algorithm with DIFFERENT and NEW themes, even while doubling down on winning themes.

### 13.8 Influencing Lattice (The Ranking Stage)

After Andromeda (retrieval), ads pass to Lattice (ranking) which pairs ads with users most probable to convert using the Total Value Equation:

```
Total Value = Advertiser Bid + Estimated Action Rate + Ad Quality
```

**Ad Quality is comprised of:**
- Quality Ranking (Below Average / Average / Above Average)
- Engagement Rate Ranking
- Conversion Rate Ranking

**How to influence Lattice:**
1. **Messaging in your ad** -- hyper-contextual ads speaking to real-time needs
2. **Pixel conditioning** -- only clean, qualified data reported back
3. **Visuals of your ad** -- native-looking, high-quality, engaging

**Key insight:** Even with a SMALLER budget, if your ads are hyper-contextual you can get priority placements and great results (high Estimated Action Rate compensates for lower bid).

### 13.9 Reaching New Audiences Under Andromeda

Three levers to widen reach:

| Lever | How It Works |
|-------|-------------|
| **Change standard event optimization** | Different events reach different user pools. People who convert on "Schedule" are different from "Submit Application." Each user's conversion history determines which ads they see. |
| **Widen messaging** | Feed Andromeda more diverse creatives and angles. New ad angles referring to audience with different names (e.g., "Ecom Brands" vs. "DTC Brands"). New visuals. |
| **Swap ad format** | Video watchers get served videos. Image engagers get served images. IG Shop users get shop-optimized ads. Exploit behavioral differences. |

**Formula for Meta success:** Messaging + Pixel Conditioning + Ad Quality + Diversification + Campaign Objective = KEY components

### 13.10 Writing Ads with LLMs (Andromeda-Compliant)

When using an LLM for ad scripting:
- Always check and edit outputs manually
- Remove "marketing-y" language and make it sound human
- Add more directness
- CTAs from LLMs are typically rough -- train it with feedback and examples
- Feed in call transcripts (10-15+ qualified calls from varying types of people) for diverse angle discovery
- Use the persona-based exercise to prompt the LLM for diverse ad angles per avatar
- Run scripts through hemingwayapp.com -- adjust to 5th grade reading level or below

## PART 14: CONTRADICTION RESOLUTION -- DYNAMIC CREATIVE

### The Conflict

**Existing skill position (Part 1, Section 1.2 -- "Trust the Algorithm"):** Use Dynamic Creative. Supply 10 videos, 5 headlines, 5 body copy texts = 250 combinations. Let the platform test automatically.

**SMO position (Launching Meta Ads Blueprint + Andromeda Blueprint):** "We are NOT using Dynamic Creative. We launch Individual Ads." 20-30 individual ads per ad set, each with the same 3-5 body copy, 3-5 headlines, and 1-2 descriptions attached.

### Why SMO Moved Away from Dynamic Creative

1. **Andromeda changed the game.** Dynamic Creative lets the platform mix-and-match elements, but Andromeda now evaluates the first 3 seconds and overall creative similarity. Spliced combinations may register as "same ad" to the algorithm.
2. **Control.** Individual ads give the media buyer full visibility into which exact creative is performing, making it easier to iterate on winners and kill losers.
3. **Full-length individualized ads last longer.** Under Andromeda, ads where Hook + Body + CTA are aligned around one cohesive theme have better longevity than spliced combinations.
4. **Creative similarity penalty.** Dynamic Creative variations that share the same body/CTA may be flagged as similar, reducing their effective reach.

### When Each Approach Can Work

| Approach | Best For | Tradeoffs |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Dynamic Creative (existing skill)** | Quick testing of messaging variations when production capacity is limited. Good for finding winning elements (which headline resonates? which body copy?). Lower barrier to entry. | Less control over exact combinations served. May hit creative similarity penalty faster. Hook-swapping lasts shorter duration. |
| **Individual Ads (SMO)** | Maximum control and Andromeda compliance. Full visibility into per-ad performance. Better for scaling. Each ad is a cohesive unit filmed start-to-finish. | Requires more production effort (20-30 unique ads per ad set). Needs robust scripting pipeline. More creative capacity required. |

### Recommendation

**For new clients or testing phases:** Either approach can work. Dynamic Creative is faster to set up for initial signal gathering.

**For scaling and Andromeda optimization:** Transition to Individual Ads following the SMO campaign structure. Film full-length individualized ads with cohesive Hook-Body-CTA alignment. This is the approach showing the best longevity and stability at scale as of late 2025.

**Let the user decide** based on their production capacity and current phase. Present both options and their tradeoffs.

### Angle Category Matrix

Before writing variations, establish 3-5 distinct angles:

| Category | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Pain point | "Stop wasting time on X" |
| Outcome | "Achieve Y in Z days" |
| Social proof | "Join 10,000+ teams who..." |

| Urgency | "Limited time: get X free" |
| Identity | "Built for [specific role/type]" |
| Contrarian | "Why [common practice] doesn't work" |

### Wave-Based Batch Generation

For large-scale creative production (100+ variations per cycle):

**Wave 1: Core angles** — 3-5 angles, 5 variations each = 15-25 pieces
**Wave 2: Extended variations** — Double down on top 2 performing angles
**Wave 3: Wild cards** — Contrarian, emotional, hyper-specific angles

Quality filter after each wave: remove over-limit, deduplicate, flag policy violations, verify headline/description combos make sense together.

### Performance Iteration Loop

When user provides performance data (CSV, paste, or dashboard):

**Step 1: Analyze Winners** — By CTR, conversion rate, or ROAS:
- Winning themes (what pain points appear in top performers?)
- Winning structures (questions? statements? numbers?)
- Winning word patterns (specific recurring phrases?)
- Character utilization (are top performers shorter or longer?)

**Step 2: Analyze Losers** — What angles fall flat, what patterns appear in low performers

**Step 3: Generate New Variations** — Double down on winners with fresh phrasing, extend winning angles into new variations, test 1-2 new unexplored angles, avoid loser patterns

**Step 4: Document the Iteration**
```

- Top performers: [list with metrics]
- Winning patterns: [summary]
- New variations: [count] headlines, [count] descriptions
- New angles being tested: [list]

```

### Common Creative Mistakes

- Writing RSA headlines that only work together (they get combined randomly)
- Ignoring character limits (platforms truncate without warning)
- All variations sound the same (vary angles, not just word choice)
- No CTA headlines (always include action-oriented options)
- Iterating without data (gut feelings < metrics)
- Testing too many variables at once (one change per test cycle)
- Retiring creative too early (allow 1,000+ impressions before judging)
