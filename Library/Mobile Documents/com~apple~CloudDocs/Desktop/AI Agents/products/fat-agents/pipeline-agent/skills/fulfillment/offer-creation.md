## Frameworks

### Framework 1: The "Hot Girl" Positioning Methodology

**Core concept:** Making a good offer is like becoming the hot girl on Instagram. Everybody wants to buy everything for you, fly you out, give you special treatment. The entire offer creation process is about turning your offer into the "hot girl" — making the market ravagingly obsessed with you.

**Cold Traffic Front-End Offer:** An offer, or packaging of your service/product, that is specifically designed to work on cold traffic. Cold traffic are complete strangers who have never heard of you.

**The test:** Imagine two complete strangers approach you. They each give you a different proposition:
- "I'll set up an automated content system for your business and guarantee you sign your next 3 clients in 6 weeks"
- "I'll help you set up a content system for your business"

Which of these people are you going to entertain a conversation with? Obviously #1. Your prospects are the same when you're approaching them — they don't know who you are. You're a stranger.

**Key insight:** It is more difficult to get somebody on the phone than it is to get them to give you thousands of dollars once they're already on that phone. Everything about positioning your offer is specifically to get them to just get on the phone. That's 80% of the battle.

**The "Hot Girl" signal:** If your offer is working, you will know. You will be getting tons of responses. People WILL be getting on calls with you. It will be extremely obvious if you have a good offer. If it feels like you need to climb a mountain and run 3 marathons just to get a single person on the phone, your offer is not good.

Just like how you ask "does he/she like me?" — you will know. If you're confused, they don't. If you're getting mixed signals from the market, the market isn't into you. And you need to change.

### Framework 2: Offer vs. Service Distinction

**Service** = What you do (the deliverable itself)
**Offer** = The result the client gets as a result of the deliverable

| Service (WRONG for cold traffic) | Offer (RIGHT for cold traffic) |
|---|---|
| "Video editing" | "1,000,000 views guaranteed" |
| "Done For You cold email" | "We help you sign 5-10 clients per month with cold email" |
| "Done For You Ads & Funnels" | "Done For You Ads & Funnels: Sign 10-20 clients per month or you don't pay" |

**Rule:** You do not get cold traffic to buy services. They will simply never give you money. Cold traffic only buys offers. You must be absolutely clear about the RESULT the prospect gets.

**What NOT to do:** "We do email marketing and google ads and facebook ads and linkedin ads and website building." Successful business owners in today's market are not a glorified ecommerce store where you put services into a shopping cart. Adding a bunch of services to your packages will not get you more calls booked. Promising results will get you more calls booked.

### Framework 3: The Three Buying Motivations (B2B Context)

There are only 3 reasons anybody buys anything:
1. **Health** — Buying this will make me feel better, look better, live longer, and avoid physical pain
2. **Love** — Buying this will result in me getting more dates, getting married, and procreating
3. **Wealth** — Buying this will deliver a return on investment that results in me being richer than I was before

Because we are B2B, all clients & prospects fall under the **Wealth** category. The only reason a client will buy from you is if they believe your service will make them richer.

**Therefore:** To make a good offer, you must promise a specific result, and then make the prospect confident you can actually get them that result.

### Framework 4: Unique Selling Proposition (USP) Framework

A unique selling proposition (USP) is the one clear, specific reason why a customer should buy from you instead of your competitors. It answers: "Why should I choose you?"

**Strong USPs do one or more of the following:**
- Promise a specific result (e.g., "Lose 10lbs in 30 days or it's free")
- Guarantee reduced risk (e.g., "No results, no pay")
- Highlight a feature no one else has (e.g., "The only CRM that writes your emails for you")

**Bad USPs (these are expectations, not differentiators):**
- "We care about our customers"
- "Great customer service"
- "High-quality work"

#### The USP Test

**If your competitor can say the same thing, it's not a USP.**

#### 5 Progressive USP Examples — Set 1: Real Estate Agents (Google Ads)

1. "We help real estate agents with google ads" — SERVICE, no USP
2. "We help real estate agents get more leads with google ads" — Vague offer, no USP
3. "We help real estate agents get 30 leads per month with google ads" — OFFER (quantifiable), weak USP
4. "We help real estate agents get 5 new listings per month with google ads" — OFFER (end result), stronger USP
5. "We guarantee real estate agents 5 new listings per month with google ads, or you don't pay us" — OFFER + GUARANTEE + USP (result-based pricing = full differentiation)

#### 5 Progressive USP Examples — Set 2: Ecommerce Email Marketing

1. "We do email marketing for ecommerce brands" — SERVICE, no USP
2. "We write & design marketing emails for ecommerce brands" — SERVICE, no USP
3. "We write & design emails for ecommerce brands that drive more sales without ad spend" — Offer (vague), weak USP
4. "We write & design emails for ecommerce brands that drive $50,000/mo in additional sales, without any ad spend" — OFFER (quantifiable), strong USP
5. "We write & design emails for ecommerce brands and guarantee you make $50,000 in additional sales, without any ad spend" — OFFER + GUARANTEE + USP
6. "We implement AI individualized email marketing for ecommerce brands. All emails are automatically triggered based off user behavior - and we guarantee you a minimum of $50,000/mo in new sales after implementing it, or you pay nothing." — OFFER + GUARANTEE + DIFFERENTIATION (AI/tech) + USP (the ultimate version)

#### 5 Progressive USP Examples — Set 3: Cold Email for Agency Owners

1. "We send cold emails for agency owners" — SERVICE, no USP
2. "We send 30,000 automated cold emails per month for agency owners" — SERVICE (volume), no USP
3. "We manually send personalized cold emails to 100 prospects per month for your agency" — SERVICE (differentiated method), weak USP
4. "We manually send 100 personalized cold emails for your agency per month and guarantee you sign a client" — OFFER + GUARANTEE + USP
5. "We do performance based lead generation for agency owners where you only pay if we get results" — OFFER + risk reversal USP
6. "We do cold email for agency owners & guarantee you sign 5 clients every month" — OFFER + BIG GUARANTEE + strongest USP

### Framework 5: Claims + Guarantee + Evidence Formula

The formulation of an offer contains three components in exact logical sequence that prospects ask:

```
CLAIM: What result will they get?
  +
GUARANTEE: What if it doesn't work, what happens?
  +
EVIDENCE: Who else has it worked for?
  =
IRRESISTIBLE OFFER
```

**Rules:**
- If you have no Evidence, it is REQUIRED to have both a Claim AND Guarantee.
- If you have Evidence (extreme evidence), you MAY remove the Guarantee.
- The necessity of a guarantee is inversely proportional with how many case studies and how much quantifiable social proof you have.

**Content Agency Example Timeline:**
```
The Videos -> Views -> Followers -> Leads -> Sales Calls -> Clients
     LEFT SIDE = SERVICE                RIGHT SIDE = OFFER
```
If you are on the left side of the timeline, you are a service. If you are on the right side of the timeline, you are an offer. The only way to sell to cold traffic is if you promise an end result on any dimension listed on the timeline.

### Framework 6: Guarantee Types & Strategy

**Types of guarantees (from least risky to most risky):**

1. **Performance Basis** — "You pay as results occur" / "You only pay when it works"
   - Zero risk to the client
   - Strongest possible conversion driver
2. **Money-Back Guarantee** — "You get refunded if you don't get the results"
   - Some risk to the client that you might not refund
   - Still very strong conversion driver

**Guarantee Phrasing Rules:**
- Do NOT list every contingency in your headline. "Performance based lead generation where we'll book you sales calls and only charge a $500 tech fee and refund you all the calls we couldn't get booked for you" is NOT marketing.
- There is a time and a place to get into the details of your offer, and it's NOT the very first sentence a prospect sees.
- All of the details about an offer and arrangement you make with a client occurs AFTER they're on the phone.
- 99% of the time the prospect won't even mention the guarantee once they're on the phone. They'll forget you even mentioned it.

**Key insight:** Every deal you do with your first 30-50 clients is going to be custom anyway. What you're advertising on the front end as your headline is NOT the exact offer that you're selling, because every deal you make with a client is going to be different.

**If using a guarantee, DO NOT sign clients who can't get the result.** When using a guarantee you need to heavily qualify clients to make sure it's possible to get them results. With a higher conversion rate, you must increase your filtering and accept only the most qualified people.

**Script for disqualifying on a call:**
> "Normally when we sign clients on this offer, it's because I'm absolutely certain we can get them that result. Based off what you have and because of x, y, and z factor, I do not believe we'd be able to get you that result. What I think is more reasonable of a result for you is x. If that's something you'd still be interested in talking about, I'd be happy to continue the conversation."

**Rule:** Do not ever lock yourself into ONLY ONE SINGULAR DELIVERABLE for ONLY ONE SINGULAR PRICE and ONLY ONE SINGULAR RESULT. Be dynamic. Sell everybody exactly what they are willing to buy until you are at $100k/mo.

### Framework 7: Bad/Good Loop Framework

#### The Bad Loop (what happens without a strong offer)
1. You promise an underwhelming result
2. You only attract low quality clients who think that result is a lot
3. It's impossible to get results for the client
4. You can't generate case studies
5. You can't charge more money and consistently increase revenue
6. You become insecure and unsure of yourself

#### The Good Loop (what happens with a strong offer)
1. You make high result claims and large guarantees in your front-end copy
2. You attract high quality clients who are only swayed by those high numbers
3. High quality clients are easy to get results for
4. You generate tons of case studies easily
5. Booking more calls becomes progressively easier & you build authority quickly
6. You increase prices and keep making more money

**Key insight:** A REASON you don't get client results is because your offer is not good enough to attract the quality clients in the first place. So the only people you ever talk to are low quality clients. This means you rarely get them any results. Which means you're scared to promise results. The cycle perpetuates.

By making your offer substantially better (promising clear & quantifiable results), you would book 2-4x more calls near immediately, giving you more exposure to people who actually have great businesses that are easier to get results for.

### Framework 8: Full Refund Math Simulation

**The core problem:** You cannot afford to only get 6 sales calls average per month for 6 months.

#### Scenario: Without Guarantee
- 6 months x 5.5 calls/month = ~33 total calls booked
- 15% close rate = 5 clients signed over 6 months
- Account for client churn = living in absolute desolate poverty, need another job
- Not running a serious business

#### Scenario: With Guarantee (6x call volume multiplier)
- 6 months x 33 calls/month = ~198 total calls booked
- 15% close rate = 30 clients closed (average of 5 per month)
- Substantial revenue to work with
- Affords the opportunity to actually produce case studies
- Case studies lead to: more calls booked, more content, more referrals, more inbound lead flow

#### Detailed Financial Simulation

**Assumptions:**
- $3,000/mo service
- Average client stays 4 months
- LTV = $12,000
- Gross margin to fulfill = 70%

**Without Guarantee (5 clients over 6 months):**
- Zero refunds
- Revenue: 5 x $12,000 = $60,000
- Profit: $60,000 x 0.70 = $42,000
- MRR trajectory is flat/declining due to churn — possibly $0 MRR in competitive spaces

**With Guarantee (30 clients over 6 months, assuming 6% refund rate):**
- 30 clients x $12,000 = $360,000 gross revenue
- 6% refund rate = ~$21,600 in refunds
- Net revenue: ~$338,400
- Profit at 70% margin: ~$236,880
- MRR trajectory is compounding upward

**Real-world refund rates with proper stipulations: 2-3%.** If client communication is on point (status updates 3+ times per week), refund rate will typically be less than 1%.

**Proof points:**
- ListKit has a money back guarantee
- Client Ascension has a money back guarantee
- Hit $1.2 Million per month with guarantees
- After watching over 1,000 agency owners come through Client Ascension, people with a guarantee grow 3x-4x faster
- That's the difference between: starting at $5k/mo and reaching $50k/mo in 2 years vs. starting at $5k/mo and reaching $50k/mo in 6 months

#### Real Case Study: Guarantee Impact on Revenue
- Josh guarantees "appointments" -> makes $10k/mo
- His competitor guarantees "clients" -> makes $270k/mo (27x more)
- Going from guaranteeing "appointments" to "clients" = 27x more sales volume

#### Real Case Study: Cold Email Agency Guarantee Switch
- Agency owner stagnating at $15,000/mo with standard tech fee + cost per lead pricing
- Switched to guaranteeing clients signed for his clients
- Rocketed to $45,000/mo in a couple months (3x revenue)
- Used a call closing rate stipulation (clients must close at least 15% of calls or no refund eligibility)

### Framework 9: 7-Stipulation Contract Template

When using guarantees, you must protect yourself against unjustified refund requests. Stipulations are things the client is required to do in order to be eligible for the guarantee.

**Two types of stipulations:**
1. **State Stipulations:** The client must already HAVE something in order to qualify for the guarantee
2. **Action Stipulations:** The client must DO specific action items in order to qualify for the guarantee

**The stipulations should be generated based off of what is required to get results anyway.** They are not unreasonable — they are literally what has to be done to get the results in the first place.

#### Verbatim Example: Content Agency — "10 clients signed per month or your money back"

**Stipulation 1:** Client must stay subscribed to the service for a minimum of 3 months and be fully paid. Non-payment that results in cancellation of the service before 3 months have elapsed automatically makes the money back guarantee null & void.

**Stipulation 2:** Client must film all 90 video scripts provided by the company and allow us to post all of them on TikTok, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, and X.

**Stipulation 3:** If the client's accounts are banned or suspended, the client is no longer eligible for the guarantee.

**Stipulation 4:** Client must respond to at least 50% of comments made on the videos.

**Stipulation 5:** Client must have a guarantee on their own offer that they're promoting in order to be eligible for the money back guarantee.

**Stipulation 6:** Client must maintain a minimum sales closing rate of 15%, and give company access to their call scheduling software in order to audit the amount of calls booked. If client fails to close at least 15% of the total calls booked within the 90 day period, the money back guarantee is null & void. This is to deter against the situation where the project is considered a "failure", when in fact it is a sales problem, which company has no control over.

**Stipulation 7:** If client wishes to request a refund, they must provide call recordings of all calls booked inside the scheduling platform in order for company to audit whether the clients were closed or not.

**Additional contract design:** You can set the contract up in a fashion where, if they do request the refund, it's given on a tiered system. For instance, if they only closed 25 clients total (out of a promised 30), they only get a 16.7% refund.

**Key rule:** Adding additional stipulations typically does NOT decrease conversion rates, if all of the stipulations are reasonable (i.e., they are what is required to get results in the first place).

### Framework 10: Transfer of Logistical Intensity

When people buy services, what they are purchasing is a transfer of logistical intensity. The more logistical intensity you can allocate towards yourself, the more somebody will pay for the offer.

**What to add to your offer:** The answer is always just the thing that nobody else wants to do. Both your competitors AND your clients don't want to do it. And if nobody wants to do it, the supply is low. When supply is low, you can charge higher prices.

**Having a competitive advantage simply means being able to do the things that require logistical intensity.**

#### Pricing by Logistical Intensity Level

```
Client does everything (course/info)         ->  $500-$2K
Client does some, you guide (coaching)       ->  $2K-$10K
You do most, client participates (hybrid)    ->  $10K-$25K
You do everything (done-for-you)             ->  $25K-$100K+
```

#### Example: Roofing Marketing Company
- **Situation 1:** You do NOT qualify the leads for your client -> Same as every other roofing marketing company with zero differentiation
- **Situation 2:** You DO qualify the leads for your client -> Basically nobody else on the planet does this. You are automatically differentiated.

#### Example: Podcast Booking Service
Goal: Client wants to land 3-4 podcast episodes a week to promote their new book.

**Intensity Points (each one you capture lets you charge more):**
1. Creating the lists of relevant podcasts
2. Writing the email scripts to get on the podcasts
3. Finding the email addresses of the podcast hosts & sending the emails
4. Scheduling the podcasts
5. Giving the client talking points on the podcast

The more of these intensity points you capture on your end, the more you are able to charge.

#### Amazon Prime Example
Amazon Prime guarantees 1 day shipping because they own the planes, trucks, and shipping network. That offer is impossible to have unless they own the entire shipping infrastructure with distribution centers across every state. They overtook the single most logistically intense process that exists on the planet, and therefore became a trillion dollar company.

**Critical distinction:** "Building Value" is NOT about stacking different services on top of one another. It's about capturing more logistical intensity of a SINGULAR service. It's better to offer lead generation all the way down to getting the meeting scheduled on their calendar, vs. offering 5 different ways of generating leads but the client has to do the response handling, booking, and lead qualification.

### Framework 11: Demand Capture vs. Demand Generation

Every offer sits on a sliding scale of Demand Capture vs. Demand Generation.

**Demand Capture Offer** = An offer somebody will buy only if they're already looking for it
**Demand Generation Offer** = An offer you can "create" demand for

#### Demand Capture Offer Examples
- Websites
- Bounce house rentals for parties
- IT services
- Real estate agent listing services
- Insurance
- Recruiting
- Automation buildouts
- Bookkeeping
- Inventory management
- Codebase/Security auditing
- Tasks Management / Data Entry / Data Extraction
- Credit card processing

#### Demand Generation Offer Examples
- Getting people more leads with Facebook ads
- Building funnels
- Converting more free trial users with email marketing
- Helping people get leads/clients on Twitter/X
- LinkedIn automated outreach to generate leads/clients
- UGC ad creatives
- Influencer marketing
- Content production offers
- Cold calling lead gen
- Cold email lead gen
- FB/Google/TikTok ads
- Webinars

#### Offers Falling in the Middle
- Discord community creation ("I help you grow your business by creating a discord community for you")
- Conversion Rate Optimization (leans toward demand generation)
- Organic YouTube Management (leans toward demand generation)
- SEO
- Public Relations
- Ad writing & designing (without funnel building)
- Video production (without it being among a content system intended to produce new clients)

#### Mechanisms
- **Demand Capture Mechanism** = Something like Google ads. You get in front of people actively searching for it (literally capturing demand)
- **Demand Generation Mechanism** = Something like cold email, Facebook ads, cold DMs. You are INTERRUPTING somebody (literally trying to GENERATE the demand)

#### Traffic Volume Benchmarks

**Demand Generation Offer** (with Quantifiable Promise + Guarantee + Social Proof):
- ~1 client for every 1,000-3,000 prospects contacted via Cold Email
- ~1 client for every 50 Dream 100 outreaches (includes multiple weeks of follow up)

**Demand Capture Offer:**
- ~1 client for every 5,000-20,000 prospects contacted via Cold Email

#### Selling a Demand Capture Offer with a Demand Generation Mechanism

If you want to sell a Demand Capture Offer (like Web Design) with a Demand Generation Mechanism (like Cold Email), you can ONLY do 3 things:

1. **Extremely high volume** — by happenstance find somebody who already needs one
2. **Position your offer to be unique** — morph it toward demand generation side
3. **Outreach based on demand capture indicators** — target prospects showing intent signals

**Key insight for demand capture + demand gen mechanism:** You must sell FIRST the concept of why they would want the offer, THEN sell them on YOUR offer. (Example: If selling life insurance via content marketing, you HAVE to convince people why they need life insurance FIRST before selling them on YOUR life insurance.)

#### Demand Capture Indicator Outreach Examples

- **Is hiring for a specific role:** "I saw you were hiring for customer service roles. Given that, I can create an AI model based on every conversation your CS team has ever had and automate your entire customer service division"
- **Just installed a specific technology:** "Saw you guys were using Webflow for your site, have you considered building out VSL or webinar funnels instead?"
- **Following/interacting with specific people on social media:** "Saw you interacting with Hormozi's post, went to your profile, and figured this would make sense: I can build you a full VSL Opt-In funnel and guarantee you make your next $30,000 in sales, or you pay nothing"

#### Morphing Demand Capture into Demand Generation (Full Examples)

**Web Design Example:**
1. "I'll make you a website" (pure demand capture)
2. "I'll make you a website with internal fraud monitoring software to stop chargebacks" (adding differentiation)
3. "I guarantee I'll increase your website conversion rate or you get a refund" (adding guarantee)
4. "I'll build you a VSL Opt-In Funnel, Run Cold Traffic to It, And Guarantee You Make Your Next $30,000 in Sales or You Don't Pay" (fully morphed into demand generation — not even a web design business anymore, now a funnels business)

**Recruiting Example:**
1. "I can recruit software engineers for your business" (pure demand capture)
2. "I can recruit a software engineer for your business who can create an AI model based on your data, meaning you can create internal tech that can automate your customer service" (adding differentiation + value)
3. "I'll create an AI model based on all of your customer data that automates your entire customer service division" (fully morphed — not even recruiting anymore)

### Framework 12: Offer Decay

When a market becomes sufficiently sophisticated (you get monumentally more competitors), all Demand Generation Offers become Demand Capture Offers.

**Example:** "I'll make you a website" in 2001 was a Demand Generation offer. Now it's a Demand Capture Offer.

This is a battle that exists throughout your entire life. At any point in time, your offer is always decaying away from Demand Generation.

**Prediction:** This is exactly what will happen to "AI Automation." This is WHY you should sell SPECIFIC BUILDS, not "AI Automation" generally. Because specific builds can be pitched with specific results, morphing it into a Demand Generation offer.

### Framework 13: Expansion of Service (Upsell Framework)

Most people are only selling one half of the equation (Traffic Generation OR Traffic Conversion).

**Traffic Generation** = everything that has to do with getting traffic:
- Facebook ads, Google Ads, YouTube Ads, TikTok Ads, Cold Email, Content Marketing, Direct Message Outreach

**Traffic Conversion** = everything that has to do with converting traffic you already have:
- Email marketing, Sales, Follow up, Conversion rate optimization, Copywriting (sales pages)

**The interdependency:** In order for YouTube Ads to work, you need Email Marketing, Sales, Follow Up, CRO, and Copywriting. In order for Email Marketing to do anything, you need traffic from Facebook ads, Google Ads, YouTube Ads, TikTok Ads, Cold Emails, and Content Marketing.

**Starting with one half is smart because:**
- It's easier to get started
- It's simpler to sell
- It's easier to get results quickly
- It requires less expertise on your end

**The upsell opportunity:** When selling one end of the equation, you are creating your own customers to sell the other half.

**Example:** You sold the client a Webinar Funnel Build w/ Ads (Demand Generation / Traffic Generation Offer). The recurring upsell to that offer is email list management. Email list management is a Traffic Conversion offer — it's not NEW MONEY so it's difficult to sell to cold traffic, but it's NOT difficult to sell to warm traffic (people who have already paid you).

**It is typically easier to sell a Traffic Generation offer** because most people don't have traffic in the first place. However, Traffic Conversion offers typically see clients that stay longer and have higher LTV — the only people who buy it have their shit together.

### Framework 14: Value Propositions & Differentiation

**Value Proposition** = you are quite literally proposing something of high value
**Differentiation** = you are quite literally actually different

**Test:** "Am I actually different than all the other people selling what I'm selling?"

#### Three Valid Ways to Differentiate

1. **Client has to do less work** than they would ordinarily need to do with competitors
2. **Client will get results faster** working with you
3. **Client incurs less risk** working with you

#### Differentiation Examples

**#1: Using AI**
- Making hundreds of ad creatives extremely fast
- Creating content without the client needing to sit down and film themselves

**#2: One Time Payment / "Setup Offers"**
- Literally saying "One Time Payment" increases calls booked 2-3x
- However: requires you have the sales skills and back-end infrastructure to properly work an upsell process to get them onto recurring services

**#3: Results Based Pricing (guarantees)**
- "You only pay when it works"
- "Performance basis"
- Money back guarantee
- "You Get Results, or You Don't Pay"

### Framework 15: "Noticing" Methodology (Product-Market Fit Discovery)

There is zero situation where you should ever make something up. Every single business you run should exclusively be derived because you saw or figured out that somebody else was making money doing it.

**Offer** = The positioning of how you convey your offer to cold traffic in a single headline or sentence
**Product** = The actual problem you're solving via a set of deliverables

Sometimes you can have an OFFER that follows all guidelines (big number claim, big guarantee) but it still won't sell because it lacks product-market fit — it's just not something that market wants. You HAVE to find somebody actively making money doing the thing. If you can't find anybody, then don't do it.

#### Method 1: Noticing

Become a "noticer." Actively observe the market:

1. **See the ads you're getting served.** Actually watch them. Go through their funnels.
2. **Get on Twitter and watch what people are posting about.** Follow thought leaders in the space. See what's getting traction.
3. **See who is making money inside communities.** Check wins channels and case studies. See what they're selling.
4. **Watch competing programs and see if their students are getting results.** Find out what they're selling.
5. **Notice the LACK of things.** If a competitor exclusively uses case studies from a DIFFERENT business model they used to teach — this is because all of their current students do not get results. They're trend hopping. NOTICE.
6. **Understand when you're being subjected to propaganda.** Someone posting "the info market is cooked" is probably about to launch an offer to info product owners they just scared.

**Key example of what to notice:** There is a massive overlap between "AI Automation" services and actual standard agency services in terms of what has active product-market fit. Selling standard agency services fulfilled via AI is objectively better than selling "AI Automation" or "AI Arbitrage" exclusively.

#### Method 2: AI Deep Research (3 Verbatim Prompts)

##### AI Research Prompt 1: Product-Market Fit Discovery
```
I'm running an AI automation agency. Please conduct research about what specific builds / automations specific companies are actively buying. I'm specifically looking for products that have product-market fit. Don't make random services/builds up. I only want to know things that are actively being sold, that have active market demand, work as a front-end cold traffic offer, and have true product-market fit. The purpose of me asking you this question is in order to find products/services I can promote that I know for a fact have product-market fit so that I don't waste a bunch of time and money trying to sell something that isn't going to even end up working.
```

##### AI Research Prompt 2: Beginner Agency Offer Discovery
```
Okay what about simple agency services that a beginner can sell? Find me offers with product-market fit for them
```

##### AI Research Prompt 3: Differentiation Against Competitors
```
Here is a description of my offer: {{description of your service, generally what kind of results people can expect working with you, the current payment structure, and guarantees if you have one}}

I want you to help me differentiate my offer against competitors in the market who are selling the same thing as me or something similar to me. The only valid ways to differentiate are by any of the following: requiring less work from the client, making results for the client happen faster, or making the client incur less risk.

Can you please come up with some ideas for me?
```

**Recommended tools for deep research:** ChatGPT, Claude Deep Research, and Manus — run the same prompt on all three for maximum coverage.

**If you find an offer/automation/deliverable that has active market demand but you don't know how to build it:** Go to YouTube and figure out how to do it. That's the first step of the research process: "Okay, let's see who else has done this and what info I can find about it and we will start from there." Once you have that info, combine it with what you already know, and just try to build it.

### Framework 16: Authority, Evidence & Case Studies

The growth of your company is directly & solely a result of how much social proof and authority you have.

**The Authority Principle:** If Alex Hormozi offered you a single one hour consulting call for $10,000, 95% of you would convert with precisely zero objections. This is because of his: Past Results + Display of Competence.

#### If You Don't Have Astounding Past Results

You can still sign high ticket clients through the concept of "undiscovered talent." Every business owner is looking for an A-Player. Even if they haven't "proven" by accomplishing something for somebody else, you can "prove it" by Competence Display.

**How to Display Competence:**
1. Post content that explains WHAT to do to get results (in line with your business model)
2. Do free work / "service based lead magnets" (covered in Client Acquisition Dream 100 section)
3. Create Case Studies OR Strategy Breakdowns (making a "study of a case")

**If you could do ONE thing only — the single most effective marketing strategy ever:** Collecting client case studies and posting them on YouTube, LinkedIn, and Twitter. This singular action works better than any growth strategy by a magnitude of at least 10x.

**Case study impact:** Case studies alone are probably responsible for 50-80% of what ultimately gets people to buy. Humans weigh social proof extremely heavily. They do not like being the first to do something or taking chances.

#### What Makes Effective Social Proof
- Revealing who the client is
- Stating direct quantifiable result working with you

**Best forms of social proof (descending order):**
1. Interview with the client, where they reveal their business & revenue made with you
2. Video testimonial of just the client, where they reveal their business & how much money they made with you
3. Screenshots of the results they got

#### You Only Get Social Proof if You Ask

Script: "Hey {{name}}, I'm trying to collect case studies so that I can sign more clients. Would you be open to getting on a video interview with me talking about the results we've got for you?"

95% of people will say yes.

If they say no: Gather all of the results and make a case study showing those results in a presentation on Gamma. Use Loom to record yourself. Post that video on YouTube. Embed it on your landing page AND use it as a sales asset.

**Timeline:** If you're just starting out, it will probably take 6 months until your first case study. Clients typically take 3-6 months to turn into a case study.

**Rule:** The sole & only purpose of your entire company is to produce as many case studies as possible. There is no such thing as "too many case studies." Every single one matters, and every single one increases your call booking rates & conversion rates.

### Framework 17: Quantifiable End Results (Picking Your Claim)

**Quantifiable end results come in dimensions.**

Example: Content Agency (sells scripted & edited content). Ask yourself "why would somebody buy videos from me?" They would do so for these reasons:
- Views
- Followers
- Leads
- Sales Calls booked
- Sales & Revenue

**To create a quantifiable end result:** Pick any of those dimensions and put a number on it.

Example claims on the same service:
- "1,000,000 views in 90 days, guaranteed"
- "10,000 new followers guaranteed"
- "1,000 new leads guaranteed"
- "100 sales calls booked guaranteed"
- "10 new clients guaranteed"

**"But I can't promise somebody 1,000,000 views."** Yes, you can. If you do what is required to get them 1,000,000 views, and you contractually require the client to do what is necessary in the first place. You use stipulations. Design contracts so the only way the result could ever be messed up is if it's your fault. Completely eliminate the possibility that the client gets a refund if it's their fault.

**Which claim sounds most attractive?** The further right on the timeline (closer to revenue/clients), the more attractive the claim.

### Framework 18: "Slinging Deals" Sales Methodology

#### How to Close 100% of Sales Calls

Everything about trying to Jiu Jitsu your sales calls into manipulating somebody to purchase is stupid. Your sole & only function on a call is to determine what their problems are, and offer a solution that will maximize them achieving their end goal.

**Opening script:**
> "Thanks for coming to the call today {{name}}. I'm going to operate under the assumption you want to grow your company, yeah?"
> "Yes."
> "Okay cool, so what's your problem right now? Why haven't you been able to do that so far?"

Now you learn all of the things they tried that didn't work — their frustrations, and the exact reasons they haven't been able to grow.

**When they push back:**
> "Well what do you want? What makes the most sense for you?"

Now they literally tell you EXACTLY what they want.

#### Dynamic Selling (The Core of Slinging Deals)

Every week starts at zero. Every day starts at zero. You reset every single day. You need to GET DEALS OVER THE LINE ALL WEEK & EVERY DAY.

Each deal might be custom, for a custom deliverable, and for a custom price. You are NOT locked into a singular deliverable & a singular price.

**Example: Selling DFY but prospect wants to learn it themselves:**
- Don't say "well I don't do that"
- Instead: sell them consulting calls & swipe files
- CROSS-SELL: done-with-you offer, get on a call once per week and build out everything together ($6,000-$12,000)

**MAIN GOAL:** Get as many humans to give you any amount of money as possible. Maximize the amount of humans on the planet who have paid you any amount of money ever.

**Why?** Because the moment somebody pays you any amount of money, it increases the probability they continue to pay you more money by about 1,000x.

**Below $100k/mo:** Don't worry about scale. Get to $100k/mo first by slinging deals and getting maximum customers.

#### Upsell / Downsell / Cross-Sell Framework

**Example: Webinar Funnel Buildout for High Ticket Coaching Client**

You pitch at $8,000 buildout + 10% gross revenue. They say that's too much.

**Step 1:** Backtrack — see if they're actually sold on the concept of the webinar in the first place.

**IF THEY WANT THE WEBINAR:**
- DOWNSELL: Remove specific elements for less money. Allocate some logistical intensity toward them for a lower price and less work on your end.

**IF THEY DON'T WANT THE WEBINAR:**
- CROSS-SELL: Offer a different funnel (low ticket product ascension funnel, VSL funnel) that doesn't require conducting a live webinar.

**If they say "I don't want to present live":**
- UPSELL: "I'll personally conduct the webinar for you" ($5,000 up front + additional 10% collected)
- CROSS-SELL: "I'll build you a different funnel where you don't have to do that"

**If they say "I want to do this myself":**
- CROSS-SELL: done-with-you offer, weekly calls, build everything together ($6,000-$12,000)

**Principle:** There's always a reason somebody isn't moving forward, which always presents an opportunity to upsell or downsell. You are always a consultant recommending what makes logical sense for every individual you speak to — like a doctor prescribing based on the patient's particular situation.

### Framework 19: Pricing Psychology

#### Anchoring & ROI Math
- Anchor high (total value), then reveal actual price
- Show the ROI math: "If you close just 2 additional clients at $3K each, that's $6K from a $2K investment"

#### Pricing Examples from the SOP

| Offer Type | Pricing Structure |
|---|---|
| $3,000/mo service, avg 4 month retention | LTV = $12,000 |
| Webinar funnel buildout | $8,000 buildout + 10% gross revenue |
| Consulting upsell from webinar | $5,000 up front + additional 10% |
| Done-with-you consulting package | $6,000-$12,000 |
| Lead gen with tech fee | $500/mo tech fee + $3,000/mo retainer with proportional refund |
| Performance-basis lead gen | Front end says "you only pay for qualified sales calls that show up" (details negotiated 1-on-1) |
| "One Time Payment" setup offer | Increases calls booked 2-3x (requires upsell process to recurring) |
| Course/info product | $500-$2K |
| Coaching | $2K-$10K |
| Hybrid (you do most) | $10K-$25K |
| Done-for-you | $25K-$100K+ |

#### Key Pricing Principles
1. Do not ever lock into one deliverable, one price, one result
2. Every deal with your first 30-50 clients will be custom
3. What you advertise on the front end is NOT the exact offer you sell — it gets prospects on the phone
4. Clean established packages are the WRONG way to build the business below $100k/mo
5. Your goal is to collect cash by any means

## Process

### Step 1: Gather Inputs
Collect all required inputs. If the user doesn't provide them, ask. Pay special attention to: what they sell, who they sell to, current price, delivery method, existing case studies/social proof.

### Step 2: Determine Product-Market Fit
Before building the offer, verify the product has actual market demand:
- Use the "Noticing" methodology — has anyone else made money doing this?
- Run the AI Research Prompts (Prompts 1 and 2 from Framework 15) if needed
- If no evidence of product-market fit exists, flag this to the user before proceeding

### Step 3: Classify the Offer Type
Determine where on the Demand Capture <-> Demand Generation spectrum the offer falls (Framework 11). This determines:
- Messaging approach (sell the opportunity first vs. sell your unique angle)
- Expected traffic conversion rates (1 per 1K-3K cold emails for demand gen vs. 1 per 5K-20K for demand capture)
- Whether you need to morph the offer toward demand generation

### Step 4: Define the Transformation
Answer these:
- What is the client's prospect's life like BEFORE? (specific pain)
- What is their life like AFTER? (specific outcome)
- How long does the transformation take?
- What proof exists that this transformation is real?

**Formula:** "We help [WHO] go from [BEFORE STATE] to [AFTER STATE] in [TIMEFRAME] through [MECHANISM]"

### Step 5: Pick the Quantifiable End Result
Using Framework 17, identify all the dimensions of results the service could promise, then pick the most attractive one (furthest right on the value timeline, closest to revenue/clients). Put a specific number on it.

### Step 6: Build the USP
Using the USP Framework (Framework 4), ensure the offer passes the USP Test: "If your competitor can say the same thing, it's not a USP." Walk through the progressive examples to find the strongest positioning level.

### Step 7: Apply Transfer of Logistical Intensity
Using Framework 10, identify all intensity points in the service delivery. Determine which ones the client currently handles vs. which ones can be absorbed. More intensity absorbed = higher pricing justified.

### Step 8: Craft the Claim + Guarantee + Evidence
Using Framework 5:
- Define the CLAIM (specific quantifiable result)
- Design the GUARANTEE (performance basis or money-back — match to client's risk tolerance and evidence level)
- Catalog the EVIDENCE (case studies, social proof, competence displays)
- If no evidence: REQUIRE both a strong claim AND guarantee

### Step 9: Write the Stipulations
Using Framework 9 (7-Stipulation Contract Template):
- Write State Stipulations (what the client must already have)
- Write Action Stipulations (what the client must do)
- Ensure all stipulations are things required to get results anyway
- Target 2-3% refund rate with proper stipulations

### Step 10: Build the Offer Stack (Deliverables)
List everything included, applying value anchoring:

| Deliverable | Value | Included |
|-------------|-------|----------|
| Core service/product | $X,XXX | Yes |
| Bonus 1: [specific tool/resource] | $X,XXX | Yes |
| Bonus 2: [access/community] | $X,XXX | Yes |
| Bonus 3: [templates/frameworks] | $XXX | Yes |
| **Total Value** | **$XX,XXX** | |
| **Your Investment** | **$X,XXX** | |

### Step 11: Set Pricing Using Logistical Intensity Tiers
Use Framework 10 pricing tiers and Framework 19 pricing psychology:
- Price according to how much logistical intensity is captured
- Show ROI math that makes the price feel like a no-brainer
- Include payment plan option if applicable
- Build in upsell/downsell/cross-sell options (Framework 18)

### Step 12: Position Against Alternatives
Map the 5 alternatives every prospect considers:
1. **Do nothing** — What's the cost of inaction?
2. **DIY** — Why is doing it themselves harder/slower/riskier?
3. **Hire someone else** — Why is the competition worse?
4. **Different category** — Why is this approach better than alternatives?
5. **Buy from competitor** — What makes this offer specifically better?

### Step 13: Compress into a Single-Sentence Headline
The entire offer must be expressible in a single sentence/headline. This is what goes on the landing page, in cold emails, in ad copy. It must:
- Lead with the RESULT, not the service
- Include a specific number where possible
- Include the guarantee where possible
- Be attractive enough to get the prospect on the phone (that's 80% of the battle)

## IF-THEN Funnel Diagnostics: Offer-Level Insights (SMO Enhancement)

The SMO *IF-THEN Funnel SOP* provides a diagnostic framework for troubleshooting VSL call funnels. Several diagnostic paths point back to the offer itself as the root cause:

**When the funnel says the offer is broken:**
- **IF** CTR is high BUT landing page conversion is low → The ad promise doesn't match the offer on the page. Realign the offer positioning or rewrite the landing page to match the ad's angle.
- **IF** show rate is good BUT close rate is below 20% → The offer may lack sufficient value stacking, the price-to-value ratio feels off, or there's no compelling mechanism. Revisit the offer stack and guarantee.
- **IF** leads are booking BUT sales team reports "not qualified" → The offer is attracting the wrong avatar. Either sharpen the offer to a more specific audience or adjust targeting to match who the offer actually serves.
- **IF** objections on calls consistently cite price → The value stack isn't strong enough to justify the price. Add more deliverables, strengthen the guarantee, or introduce payment terms.
- **IF** prospects say "I need to think about it" → The urgency element is missing. Build real urgency into the offer (see trust-asset-creation skill for urgency frameworks).

**Key insight from SMO:** "The strength of the offer dictates the performance of the marketing." If funnel metrics are consistently underperforming across multiple angles and creatives, the offer itself is likely the bottleneck — not the copy, not the ads, not the funnel.

## APPENDIX: Pricing Research Methods (Merged from pricing-strategy skill)

> Unique frameworks not covered above. For SaaS/subscription pricing research and tier structure design.

### Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Method

Four questions that identify the acceptable price range for any offer:

1. **Too Expensive** — "At what price would you consider this too expensive to consider?"
2. **Too Cheap** — "At what price would you question the quality?"
3. **Expensive but Acceptable** — "At what price would this start to feel expensive, but you'd still consider it?"
4. **A Bargain** — "At what price would this feel like a great deal?"

Plot the four curves. The intersections reveal:
- **Point of Marginal Cheapness** (Too Cheap x Expensive) — Price floor
- **Point of Marginal Expensiveness** (Too Expensive x Bargain) — Price ceiling
- **Optimal Price Point** (Too Cheap x Too Expensive) — Where resistance is lowest
- **Indifference Price Point** (Expensive x Bargain) — Equal pull in both directions

**When to use:** Before setting price points. Requires surveying 30+ target prospects.

### MaxDiff Analysis for Feature Packaging

Identifies which features customers value most to inform tier packaging:
- Show sets of 4-5 features at a time
- Ask: "Most important?" and "Least important?"
- Statistical analysis reveals true feature hierarchy
- Results inform what goes in each tier

### Good-Better-Best Tier Framework

| Tier | Role | Pricing | Contains |
|------|------|---------|----------|
| **Good** (Entry) | Get them in the door | Low anchor | Core features, limited usage |
| **Better** (Recommended) | Where most customers land | 2-3x Good | Full features, reasonable limits |
| **Best** (Premium) | Maximize revenue per customer | 2-3x Better | Everything + advanced features, priority support |

**Differentiation levers:** Feature gating, usage limits, support level, API/SSO/branding access.

### When to Raise Prices

**Market signals:** Competitors raised prices, prospects don't flinch, "it's so cheap!" feedback.
**Business signals:** Conversion >40%, monthly churn <3%, strong unit economics.
**Product signals:** Significant value added since last pricing, product more mature.

**Strategies:** Grandfather existing customers, delayed increase (3-6 months notice), tie to new value, full plan restructure.

### Pricing Psychology Quick Reference

- **Anchoring:** Show higher-priced option first
- **Decoy effect:** Middle tier should look like best value
- **Charm pricing:** $49 vs $50 (value-focused segments)
- **Round pricing:** $50 vs $49 (premium positioning)
- **Annual discount:** 17-20% off monthly to lock in commitment
